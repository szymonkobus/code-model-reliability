The measure catalog: 39 ways to measure task difficulty

A static copy; data as of 22 September 2026.

Each measure: generic definition, two instantiations (AI-eval and a non-AI domain), then an independent skeptical critique (validity, generality, gameability, kill test, verdict).

Recurring terms (plain language). IRT, item response theory: solvers get ability numbers, tasks get difficulty numbers, estimated jointly from who-solved-what; 2PL is its standard model, giving each task b (difficulty: capability needed for 50% success) and a (discrimination: how sharply success flips as capability rises; low/negative a marks broken or lure tasks); θ is solver capability on that scale; the scale is the fail rate stretched at both ends (in symbols, log(p/(1−p))). pass@k / pass^k: solved at least once in k tries / all k times. Elo/Glicko/WHR: chess-style rating systems over win/loss records. PVI, pointwise V-information: how much usable information an input gives a model family about the answer. MDL: minimum description length (shortest adequate description). KL: Kullback-Leibler divergence, an information-theoretic distance between distributions. DIF: two solver populations ordering the same tasks differently. Field-specific terms are explained where they appear.*

M01 · Frame-relative failure rate

M01 · Frame-relative failure rate: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Fix a frame F = (solver population P, protocol/budget R, verifier V). The measure is the expected failure probability D_F(s) = 1 − E_{π∼P}[p(s, π, R, V)], where p is the probability that solver π's attempt on specification s is accepted by V under R; the frame is part of the measure's identity: tasks are equally difficult in F iff they sit on the same level set of this functional, which every other behavioral difficulty measure re-parameterizes. In estimated form, per-solver success counts receive hierarchical (empirical-Bayes) shrinkage on the stretched fail-rate scale, giving finite, principled values to never-solved and always-solved cells, and aggregate failure is decomposed into attempt-level stochasticity E[p(1−p)] versus instance-level heterogeneity Var(p), distinguishing "every task a coin flip" from "half trivial, half impossible", identical means but opposite objects.

  • Facet: Overall operational solvability (the anchor outcome scale for behavioral difficulty) on a censoring-aware, unbounded stretched scale; plus where difficulty lives: in the instance draw or the attempt draw (epistemic vs aleatoric).
  • Relative to: Fully frame-relative by construction: jointly relative to solver population P (with declared weights), protocol/budget R, and verifier V; changing any component defines a different measure. The concentration decomposition is additionally relative to a declared task distribution T.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Population skill composition (a weak population's failures read as task difficulty); solvers' prior exposure to the task or its solution (memorization scores as ease); verifier strictness, false-accept/false-reject rates, and verifier measurement noise all folded into "difficulty"; budget censoring and near-frontier censoring (p≈0 indistinguishable from impossible at any finite n; ordering among never-solved tasks is set by the prior, and prior-family choice moves extreme ranks); binomial sampling noise p(1−p)/n; solver stochasticity settings (e.g., sampling temperature) directly change within-instance variance; with k attempts the mixing distribution is identified only up to its first k moments, so shape claims need large k; apparent instance heterogeneity also reflects specification ambiguity and verifier disagreement, not only difficulty.
  • Related work: Thorndike 1904; Gulliksen 1950; Lord & Novick 1968 (classical test theory item p-values); Rasch 1960 (difficulty on the stretched scale); Skellam 1948 (beta-binomial); Teicher 1963 (mixture identifiability); Efron & Morris 1975 (EB shrinkage); Vaupel et al. 1979 (frailty heterogeneity); Gelman et al. 2013 (hierarchical binomial); Miller 2024 (Adding Error Bars to Evals); Polo et al. 2024 (tinyBenchmarks); Yao et al. 2024 (tau-bench pass^k vs pass@k)

AI-eval instantiation. Declare the frame (model list, attempts k, sampling parameters, verifier version) and run N models × k sampled attempts per task (e.g., three model sizes × 128 attempts) with automatic verification. Naive form: D = 1 − pooled pass fraction, published with the frame. Canonical form: per model, fit a beta-binomial (or logit-normal) over task pass probabilities by marginal likelihood; report D_t = −(1/N)·Σ_m E[logit p_tm | k_tm] with the per-model values alongside, so 0/k and k/k cells get shrunken finite scores instead of ties. Decomposition: report intra-instance correlation ρ and the fractions of tasks with posterior p̂ below 1/k ("effectively impossible") vs p̂ in (0.2, 0.8) ("coin-flip zone").

Other-domain instantiation. Psychometric item analysis: an item's difficulty is the fraction of a defined examinee cohort answering it correctly under standard timing (shrunk toward the form mean for small cells); likewise, the fraction of 1800-rated club players finding a chess puzzle's winning move within 5 minutes. For the decomposition: a basketball program making 50% of shots overall may be every player at 50% per shot or half the shot positions unmakeable, separable by repeated shots at fixed positions, as frailty models separate individual risk heterogeneity from event randomness.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 8/10)

  • Validity: Within its declared frame it is difficulty by definition, which is the weakness: it records outcomes rather than explaining them, and "hard for whom" is settled by fiat. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: population-task exposure mismatch (contamination/memorization, format familiarity) plus verifier false-accept/false-reject rates; in current AI benchmarks these plausibly explain as much task-to-task variance as any intrinsic property. Ranks among never-solved (0/k) cells reflect the shrinkage prior, not data.
  • Generality: Broadest in the catalog: any domain with a population, budget, and acceptor. Breaks where attempts are non-repeatable or the solver learns across attempts (human examinees remembering attempt 1, a first-of-kind surgery), so i.i.d. p(s,π) is undefined; also where no verifier exists (open-ended creative work) or the population has size one.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Honest: the core IS plain pooled failure rate, formalized. Genuine increments: (1) the E[p(1−p)] vs Var(p) decomposition ("all coin flips" vs "half impossible"), invisible to pooled rate and decisive for whether resampling or a better solver helps; (2) the explicit frame card, enabling comparability audits. The finite shrunken scores at extremes are cosmetic; that ordering is prior-chosen.
  • Gameability: Saturated frames: all-k/k ties, shrinkage fabricates an ordering from the prior. Defective tasks (broken verifier or gold label) masquerade as maximally hard; memorized/leaked tasks as trivially easy. Frame cherry-picking (weak population, strict verifier, high temperature) manufactures "hard" benchmarks; verifier reward-hacking manufactures ease; raising k inflates apparent solvability.
  • Cost: Low-moderate for AI frames (N models × k attempts with automatic verification; 128 attempts is compute-heavy but routine). Expensive for human frames: cohorts, one uncontaminated attempt per person.
  • Kill test: Generic: semantics-preserving perturbation audit; perturb surface form or swap an equally legitimate frame component; substantial rank reordering convicts it of tracking exposure/frame artifacts, not task difficulty. Concrete: score GSM8K originals vs matched functional rewrites (GSM-Symbolic style) in one fixed frame; a large easy-shift on originals exposes memorization-as-ease. Tail check: swap beta-binomial for logit-normal and show 0/k orderings flip.
  • Best improvement: Report identified vs prior-driven ranks separately: intervals like [0, 1/k] for never-solved cells, never point ranks; mandate an M17 contamination/paraphrase screen and an M21 verifier-error estimate before labeling D "difficulty"; publish a two-frame rank-correlation audit with every release. Redundancy: keep as the anchor scale; M06/M33 re-parameterize it, so fold nothing.

M02 · Search cost to first success

M02 · Search cost to first success: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The expected effort a stochastic solver from P must spend under protocol R before first emitting an output that verifier V accepts: attempts are draws from the solver's default policy π0, and success is landing in V's acceptance set A. The canonical scale is the surprisal of success −log π0(A) (the nats of steering needed to condition default behavior onto A), realized operationally as the attempt horizon k50 = log(1/2)/log(1−p), expected retries 1/p, or a compute price (per-attempt cost)/p. A companion exchange rate measures how far search substitutes for capability: the smallest budget k at which a weaker solver's best-of-k matches a stronger solver's single attempt (infinite = capability-gated rather than luck-gated).

  • Facet: Extensive search depth: the inference/effort cost of a first verified success given free verification; places tasks on the regime axis solvable-reliably → solvable-by-search → unsolved-at-budget, and is the unique scale on which difficulty adds under independent conjunctive composition.
  • Relative to: Solver- and verifier-relative: a designated solver's sampling prior (or the best over population P) under protocol R, with V assumed freely available to select among attempts; the exchange-rate variant is relative to ordered solver pairs within P.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: For a fixed solver every version is a monotone transform of per-attempt success rate, so the added content is the extensive scale, units, and censoring discipline, not a new ordering. Assumes free verification at selection time; without it best-of-k is not a deployable solver, so practical difficulty is understated, while lenient verifiers shrink horizons by accepting lucky wrong outputs. The zero-success tail is censored, prior-sensitive, and often defect-dense, so extreme values partly measure task brokenness (report interval bounds, never point ranks). Everything rescales with the solver's prior: temperature choice shifts all horizons; contamination/memorization deflates cost. Compute-denominated variants inflate with verbose outputs, and long required outputs accumulate nats even when each step is trivial. Small-p estimation needs ~e^D attempts and exchangeable samples.
  • Related work: Chen et al. 2021 (pass@k unbiased estimator); Brown et al. 2024 (Large Language Monkeys: repeated-sampling coverage, small-model+search cost frontier); Snell et al. 2024 (compute-optimal test-time scaling); Hu et al. 2023 (PassUntil); Haldane 1945 (inverse binomial sampling); Kwa et al. 2025 (METR time horizons); Shannon 1948 (surprisal); Kappen 2005 / Todorov 2009 (KL control); Levine 2018 (RL as inference); Gao et al. 2023 (best-of-n KL ≈ log n − (n−1)/n)

AI-eval instantiation. Sample n i.i.d. attempts per task per model at fixed temperature; estimate per-attempt acceptance p against the automatic verifier; report log2 k50 = log2[ln(1/2)/ln(1−p)] for the best model, with rule-of-three censored bounds where nothing passes, then uncensor the tail by sequential sampling to first success (inverse binomial inference, budgets up to 10^4–10^5 attempts). Variants: FLOP price log10 min_m (2·N_m·t̄_m/p_m) over model sizes; steering work −ln p cross-checked against the attempt-free bound −ln q(reference solution | s) under the solver's own likelihood (separates rare-but-in-distribution from mis-weighted); exchange rate k_eq(weak→strong) = min{k : pass@k_weak ≥ pass@1_strong}, censored entries flagged capability-gated.

Other-domain instantiation. High-throughput discovery: for each target property, a lab draws candidate compounds from its standard generation pipeline and runs the assay (the verifier) until the first hit; difficulty = log screens-to-first-hit, with never-hit targets reported as censored bounds. Exchange-rate variant: how many screens a cheap pipeline needs before matching a premium pipeline's single-shot hit rate. Analogues: log effective keyspace remaining to a safecracker's heuristics; shaping steps (≈ constant nats each) to first elicit a target behavior in animal training.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 8/10)

  • Validity: For a fixed solver it is a monotone transform of per-attempt pass rate (M01), so its ordering inherits all of p's flaws; the new content is only scale, censoring, and additivity. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: acceptance-set breadth relative to the solver's prior, i.e., answer format, contamination, and verifier leniency, not cognitive difficulty. Guessable formats cap cost at log|answer-space| (a 4-way MCQ can never exceed 2 bits however hard); long required outputs accumulate nats though every step is trivial; memorized tasks deflate. Within strict-verifier generative tasks it does track a real facet: rarity of success under default behavior, uniquely additive under independent conjunction.
  • Generality: Requires cheap, exchangeable resampling plus free reliable verification. Fits code/math/screening (the assay instantiation is apt). Breaks in one-shot irreversible domains (surgery, live negotiation) where best-of-k is not a deployable solver; for humans, whose attempts are non-exchangeable (they learn), 1/p misdescribes effort; and where verification itself is the bottleneck (open conjectures).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: (1) the extensive log scale separates p=1e-3 from 1e-9 where pass rate reads 0.00, via sequential-sampling uncensoring; (2) additivity under conjunction; (3) k_eq is a true two-solver functional: luck-gated vs capability-gated regime split. Honest: for mid-range p it adds only units over success rate.
  • Gameability: Contamination makes hard tasks look easy; lenient/hacked verifiers shrink horizons via lucky wrong passes; temperature/prompt tuning reshuffles all horizons; the censored tail is defect-dense, so broken tasks masquerade as maximally hard; small-p extrapolation is researcher-degrees-of-freedom. Saturated tasks behave fine (k50→1).
  • Cost: Cheap at moderate p (reuses standard pass@k sampling); ~e^D attempts (10^4–10^5 plus verifier calls) exactly in the tail where it adds most; k_eq needs a solver ladder.
  • Kill test: Generic: show the uncensored tail carries signal beyond a flat "unsolved" flag and survives prior perturbation. Concrete: on ~200 tasks at 0/100 passes, sequentially sample to 1e5 attempts at two temperatures and paraphrased prompts; kill if cross-condition Spearman of log k50 < 0.5, or if tail ranks predict which tasks the next model generation first solves no better than the flat flag.
  • Best improvement: Subtract the chance/guessing floor (−log π0(A) − log c) or restrict to open-ended strict-verifier tasks; normalize nats per decision fork (tie to M15) to kill length inflation; mandate co-reported screens (M17 contamination, M21 strictness elasticity) and a prior-sensitivity band across temperatures/paraphrases; never point-rank the censored tail; report reachability (M03) plus interval bounds jointly.

M03 · Irreducible-failure ceiling

M03 · Irreducible-failure ceiling: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The failure mass that survives in the limit of a scaled resource: the asymptotic deficit 1 − c, where c is the limiting success probability as either solver ability in P grows without bound (upper asymptote of the task's response curve) or a stochastic solver's attempt count under R goes to infinity (plateau of the coverage curve). It splits difficulty into rarity (an accepted solution lies in the solvers' support but is hit rarely per attempt) versus unreachability (no V-accepted solution is in the support at any resource level). Reported as the pair (asymptote, approach rate), it isolates the difficulty component orthogonal to capability level and to sampling variance.

  • Facet: The asymptotic/modal structure of difficulty: outside-the-solver vs rare-for-the-solver (support vs measure of the success distribution), which any scalar failure rate conflates.
  • Relative to: Doubly indexed. Attempts-limit version: one stochastic solver (or family) at fixed decoding/protocol settings. Ability-limit version: the ability-to-infinity limit over population P, the most solver-independent index outcome statistics offer. Both remain verifier-relative: V's strictness is part of what is "irreducible," and V's false accepts manufacture fake reachability.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Verifier or specification defects are observationally identical to genuine task ambiguity (both cap the asymptote), so unscreened, the measure grades V (or a broken reference solution) rather than s. Finite attempts censor rather than prove: k samples only bound per-attempt success above ~3/k, coverage keeps climbing out to k~10^4, and plateau extrapolation is fragile (heavy-tailed rate mixtures mimic plateaus and vice versa, while information about c grows only logarithmically in k). The support is setting-indexed (temperature, prompting, and decoding change what is reachable), and one solver family's blind spot reads as Bayes error though another population solves the task instantly. Ability-limit variant: a finite ability range weakly identifies asymptotes (wide CIs), contamination in top solvers inflates the ceiling, and monotone response curves are assumed (inverse-scaling tasks violate). When the ceiling arises from one dominant wrong attractor, that attractor's mass is mechanically bounded by the failure rate, may be a defensible alternative reading of an underspecified s (ambiguity, not a lure), and can be manufactured by a popular wrong artifact in training data.
  • Related work: Barton & Lord 1981 (4PL IRT upper asymptote); Wichmann & Hill 2001 (lapse rates in psychometric functions); Chen et al. 2021 (pass@k estimator); Brown et al. 2024 (Large Language Monkeys coverage scaling); Snell et al. 2024 (test-time compute); Gema et al. 2024 (benchmark error floors); Vendrow et al. 2025 (platinum benchmarks / label-noise ceilings); Haladyna & Downing 1993 (attractive distractors); Weinreich et al. 2006 (accessibility in fitness landscapes); classical Bayes error / PAC realizability.

AI-eval instantiation. Sample k = 10^2–10^4 attempts per task from a fixed model/temperature with automatic verification; fit a saturating model to pass@k and report plateau ĉ with CI plus half-life k½; for zero-success tasks report −log10 of the rule-of-three upper bound (explicit censoring). Classify tasks sampling-solvable (ĉ≈1, low rate) vs capability-blocked (ĉ<1); the former are exactly where best-of-n and rejection-sampling RL get signal. Ability-limit upgrade (needs a panel): 15–30 solvers spanning ability at 8–32 samples each, fit 4PL P(pass|θ) = c + (d−c)·σ(a(θ−b)), report 1−d̂, identified only if several solvers sit well above b. Mandatory screen first: verify the reference solution passes and audit test strictness, else the measure grades the harness; optionally cluster failures by behavioral signature and report the largest wrong-class mass to diagnose the ceiling's mechanism (single attractor vs diffuse).

Other-domain instantiation. Comparative animal cognition: whether any individual ever opens the puzzle box given unlimited trials (support) versus mean trials to first success (rate); a species can be slow yet capable, or fast on easy boxes yet permanently blocked. Directed evolution in protein engineering: which fitness improvements are accessible at all via single-mutation walks versus expected waiting time when accessible.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: The support-vs-measure split is a real structural property (Bayes-error analog), but in practice most variance in ĉ across tasks is plausibly harness variance, not task unreachability: label/verifier defects dominate observed error floors (Gema 2024; Vendrow 2025 platinum results), decoding settings index the support, and finite-k plateau extrapolation is unidentifiable against heavy-tailed rate mixtures (info about c grows ~log k). Strongest alternative explanation: ĉ measures verifier quality plus the fitted functional form, with the residual reflecting one solver family's blind spots.
  • Generality: Conceptually broad (puzzle-box reachability, mutational accessibility in fitness landscapes) but requires iid repeatable attempts and a stable binary verifier. Breaks wherever attempts aren't exchangeable: human/animal solvers learn across trials, so the coverage curve confounds acquisition with rarity; graded-quality tasks (essay writing) have no binary asymptote; drifting/adversarial environments (M37) admit no stable c. Ability-limit variant breaks under non-monotone (inverse) scaling and bounded populations.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: distinguishes rare-but-reachable (where best-of-n and rejection-sampling RL get signal) from unreachable (needs capability or spec change), decision-relevant information no fixed-k pass rate or M01 frame rate provides. But the rate half of the (asymptote, rate) pair duplicates M02; the ability-limit asymptote is just the 4PL upper parameter estimated alongside M06/M07; verifier-caused ceilings duplicate M21.
  • Gameability: Lenient or hackable verifiers manufacture reachability: one false accept flips a task to sampling-solvable; contamination makes memorized tasks read reachable in the ability limit; conversely temperature-0 decoding or small-k rule-of-three reporting manufactures unreachability; early-plateau functional forms fake ceilings (Brown 2024 coverage is still power-law at k~10^4); saturated tasks return ĉ≈1 vacuously.
  • Cost: High compute, cheap infra: 10^2–10^4 automated samples/task (10–100x normal eval cost); ability-limit needs a 15–30 solver panel and is still weakly identified.
  • Kill test: Generic: test whether "capability-blocked" labels survive resource extension and harness repair. Concrete: take tasks with ĉ<0.9 (tight CI) at k=10^3, one model, T=0.8; rerun at k=10^5, T=1.0, a second model family, and after a platinum-style label audit; also fit on k/2 and predict held-out coverage at k. If under ~50% of unreachable labels survive, the measure grades harness plus estimator, not tasks.
  • Best improvement: Report censored bounds, never extrapolated plateaus, unless the fit predicts a held-out doubling of k. Define the ceiling as the minimum over ≥2 solver families and decoding settings after a mandatory verifier/reference audit. Move the rate component into M02, keeping M03 pure asymptote, and tag each ceiling with mechanism (spec ambiguity → M26, verifier strictness → M21, lure attractor → M20).

M04 · Mode-distribution discordance

M04 · Mode-distribution discordance: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. When a solver's protocol induces a distribution over attempts, compare the verifier's verdict on the solver's single preferred attempt (its mode: the argmax/greedy output, the plurality of its own samples, or its top self-ranked candidate) with the expected verdict over attempts drawn at random from that distribution. The signed discordance D = (sampled success rate) − 1[preferred attempt succeeds] separates two regimes invisible in success rate alone: D>0 marks a lure task, where the preferred output is a systematic wrong attractor although correct solutions exist in the distribution (self-selection entrenches error); D<0 marks a fragile mode, where the preferred output is correct but success collapses under stochastic exploration.

  • Facet: Systematic vs stochastic failure at matched success rate: whether difficulty lives in the argmax (wrong attractor basin) or in the distribution tail (diffuse noise); equivalently, fidelity of the solver's implicit self-ranking of its own outputs. A direction-of-error diagnostic that reorders tasks within difficulty bands, not a monotone difficulty index.
  • Relative to: Solver- and protocol-relative: a property of one solver's attempt distribution under a designated protocol pair (self-selected/modal decoding vs stochastic sampling at a chosen exploration level), optionally averaged over population P; not task-intrinsic.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: The modal arm is often a single deterministic draw per solver (a few Bernoulli bits), so one verifier flake flips it, and modal-decoding pathologies (degeneration, repetition loops, truncation) fail for reasons unrelated to the task. Mechanically censored: |D| is squeezed toward 0 at success rates near 0 or 1 and arises trivially near 0.5, so it must be read within success-rate strata. The distribution arm sits at an arbitrary exploration setting (e.g., one temperature); serving/execution nondeterminism blurs what "the mode" is; plurality selection depends on behavior-equivalence granularity: a fragmented correct class facing one large wrong class can lose the vote artifactually.
  • Related work: Wang et al. 2023 (self-consistency: mode vs marginal correctness); Chen et al. 2021 (pass@k, greedy vs sampled); Li et al. 2022 (AlphaCode clustering-based selection); Holtzman et al. 2020 (T=0 degeneration); Holtzman et al. 2021 (surface-form competition); Song et al. 2024 (The Good, the Bad, and the Greedy); Renze & Guven 2024 and Zhu et al. 2024 (temperature effects); Kruger et al. 2005 (first-instinct fallacy)

AI-eval instantiation. Per model m: Δ_m = p̂_m(T≈0.8, n≥32 samples) − 1[greedy T=0 attempt passes]; task value = mean_m Δ_m, plus flags "mode-lure" (greedy fails, p̂>0.5) and "fragile-mode" (greedy passes, p̂<0.5). With one greedy bit per model, report posterior discordance probabilities rather than raw differences and stratify by p̂. Selector variants from the same rollouts: plurality-by-behavioral-signature among samples (lift = 1[modal signature passes] − p̂) and max-mean-logprob rescoring of stored samples (open weights, one scoring pass, no new generation). Extension: sweep T ∈ {0, 0.2, …, 1.2} × 32 samples to trace each task's temperature-response curve p(T) and report a robustness width: the T at which p halves from its T→0 value. (On wave 1 with a 1.5B model the regimes genuinely dissociate: 24 greedy-fail-but-majority-pass tasks vs 19 the reverse.)

Other-domain instantiation. Exam items under a commit-then-brainstorm protocol: each examinee first commits to a single best answer, then separately produces k independent plausible answers (or is re-tested on separated occasions). Item score D = (fraction of the k candidates judged correct) − 1[committed answer correct], averaged over examinees. Strongly positive D flags lure items where the population's first-instinct answer is a systematic distractor despite the knowledge being present (the first-instinct fallacy in answer-changing research); strongly negative D flags fragile knowledge that survives deliberate commitment but not free regeneration.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Tracks failure character (where error mass sits in the attempt distribution), not difficulty magnitude; it self-describes as a within-band reorderer, so it is not a difficulty index at all. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: sampling noise plus decoding artifacts. The modal arm is one Bernoulli bit, so raw D is binomially noisy (maximal near p=0.5, censored at extremes); T=0 degeneration, verifier flakes, and answer-equivalence granularity generate large |D| with no task property involved. Residually it measures the solver's selection calibration (a solver trait) as much as anything about the task.
  • Generality: Requires a solver with a distribution over attempts plus a canonical preferred pick. Fits LLM decoding and contrived commit-then-brainstorm exams; breaks for one-shot embodied tasks (surgery, physical prototyping: no 32 resamples), deterministic solvers, and open-ended generation (essays, design) where every sample is behaviorally unique, making mode/plurality degenerate.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: at matched success rate it separates systematic wrong attractors (predicting majority-vote/self-consistency futility and RL-on-own-samples entrenchment) from diffuse noise (predicting large selection-scaffold gains). Success rate is blind to error direction. But it is a diagnostic moderator of other measures, not difficulty itself, and its D>0 half largely duplicates M20's realized modal wrong-behavior mass while the self-ranking-fidelity reading overlaps M31.
  • Gameability: Censored to ~0 on saturated and floored tasks: silent exactly where benchmarks saturate. Memorized tasks masquerade as fragile-mode (greedy regurgitates the memorized string; samples wander). Flaky verifiers flip the single greedy bit, minting spurious lure flags. Equivalence-granularity choices manufacture or erase plurality discordance. If optimized against, trivially zeroed: define the preferred attempt as a random sample and E[D]=0 with no capability change.
  • Cost: Cheap marginal cost atop a pass@k harness: one greedy decode plus verification per model-task (~3% over n=32); temperature sweep ~7x; logprob rescoring needs open weights. Human commit-then-brainstorm roughly doubles per-examinee testing cost.
  • Kill test: Null-calibration plus reliability: within success-rate strata, compare observed |D| to the exchangeable null (preferred pick treated as one more random draw) and check split-half/replicate reproducibility of flags; if flags neither beat the null nor reproduce, the measure is noise. Concretely: in the wave 1 1.5B setup, re-run greedy 5x under serving nondeterminism and split the 32 samples 16/16; if the 24 mode-lure and 19 fragile-mode flags show kappa<0.2 across replicates within p-strata, the claimed dissociation is a sampling artifact. Secondary: confirm D>0 tasks actually resist self-consistency gains out of sample.
  • Best improvement: Replace the one-bit modal arm with replicated preferred picks (posterior P(mode wrong)) or plurality-of-n with bootstrap CIs; report calibrated deviation from the exchangeable null within success-rate strata instead of raw D; define behavior-equivalence classes by verifier signature; make the temperature-response width p(T) the headline statistic. Route D>0 evidence into M20 and selection-fidelity claims into M31 to avoid double-counting; keep M04 only as the unified signed diagnostic (fragile-mode side has no other home).

M05 · Difficulty-measurement precision

M05 · Difficulty-measurement precision: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The precision with which a task's difficulty value and rank are pinned down by whatever process estimates them: the uncertainty interval induced by finite solver attempts and verifier noise (ex post), or the dispersion of difficulty judgments within and across raters (ex ante). It is a second-order measure attached to every first-order difficulty measure, capturing how contested or illegible a task's difficulty is rather than how high it is. Tasks can be easy-but-illegible or hard-but-obviously-hard; this axis separates the two, and sets the resolution below which differences between tasks are meaningless.

  • Facet: Meta-uncertainty / identifiability: the difficulty of assessing difficulty; a precision floor for all other measures, orthogonal to hardness level.
  • Relative to: The measurement apparatus rather than the solver population per se: the finite (P,R,V) sample and estimator in the ex-post variant, or the rater panel standing in for expert assessors in the ex-ante variant. It quantifies uncertainty conditional on that apparatus and excludes uncertainty over unmodeled choices (alternative solver populations, protocol variants) it conditions on.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Conditions on the fixed solvers, prompts, and sampling settings, so it understates total uncertainty. Rank-CI width depends on the local density of neighboring tasks, mixing task properties with suite composition. Verifier-noise floors are only estimable where duplicate attempts occur, i.e., mostly on easy tasks. Rating dispersion has a mechanical inverted-U relation to level: consensually trivial or brutal tasks get low spread via scale compression (analyze residual dispersion given median rating). Dispersion also reflects rubric vagueness and rater-sampling temperature, properties of the apparatus, not the task. Small ensembles give noisy variance-of-variance estimates, and precision improves with attempt budget, so the value is partly a budget descriptor.
  • Related work: Efron 1979 (bootstrap); Miller 2024 (Adding Error Bars to Evals); Madaan et al. 2024 (quantifying variance in evaluation benchmarks); Plank 2022 (human label variation as signal); Uma et al. 2021 (learning-from-disagreement survey); Baan et al. 2022 (calibration to human disagreement)

AI-eval instantiation. Ex post: from N models x k sampled attempts per task with automatic verification, fit a shrunken (hierarchical) success-probability model; draw B=1000 parametric-bootstrap replicates from the posterior predictive; report per task the 95% CI width on the stretched pass rate and on rank within the suite, plus suite-level Kendall tau across replicates. This turns "the sort is noise at the tails" into a per-task number: near-0% and near-100% tasks become certifiably unrankable. Add a verifier-noise floor: the rate at which identical attempts receive different verdicts (flaky tests, timing nondeterminism). Ex ante: have m judge models x r resamples rate each task's difficulty; fit a random-effects decomposition into within- and between-judge variance; per-task total SD (with between-judge share as a secondary coordinate), validated against audit tags: underspecified/contradictory tasks should concentrate in the top dispersion decile.

Other-domain instantiation. Rock-climbing grade consensus: a route's difficulty is the median grade proposed by ascensionists in a logbook database; the meta-measure is the spread of proposed grades per route plus a bootstrap CI on the route's rank in a regional list, obtained by resampling ascents. This separates contested routes ("soft", "sandbagged", body-size-dependent) from consensually graded ones at the same grade, and flags routes with too few ascents to grade at all, the analogue of unrankable tail tasks.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 4/10)

  • Validity: Explicitly second-order: it tracks the apparatus, not difficulty. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: it is a near-deterministic transform of first-order quantities; the stretched-scale CI width is largely a function of pass rate and attempt budget (binomial arithmetic, exploding at the tails), rank-CI of suite density, and rater dispersion of rubric vagueness plus the mechanical inverted-U in level. The task-intrinsic content is only the residual (overdispersion, verifier flakiness, rater disagreement given level), and that residual heavily overlaps M26 (spec ambiguity) and the error bar on M29.
  • Generality: Very general wherever repeated attempts or rater panels exist (climbing logbooks work). Breaks on one-shot, irreversible real-world tasks (a surgery, a live negotiation): n=1, no resampling, no expert panel; ex-post variant undefined, ex-ante degenerates to whatever raters guess.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine but narrow: it says which differences between tasks are meaningful at all (certifiably unrankable tails) and flags illegible tasks. Honest accounting: beyond pass rate + budget, the new information is only overdispersion, verifier-flake rate, and level-adjusted rater disagreement; the rest is arithmetic every competent analysis reports anyway (M01 already bundles shrinkage/variance decomposition).
  • Gameability: Precision is budget-buyable (crank Nk); rank-CIs shrink by padding suites with spaced filler; scale choice games tails (saturated tasks look precisely easy on probability scale, unrankable on the stretched scale); correlated judges (same base model, shared rubric) fake consensus, so memorized or defective-but-familiar tasks read as highly legible; verifier-flake floor is only observable on easy tasks, hiding flakiness exactly where it matters.
  • Cost: Low ex post (reuses existing attempts + bootstrap); cheap-to-moderate ex ante (m×r judge calls).
  • Kill test: Regress the per-task precision value on pass rate, attempt budget, and suite-local density; if R²>0.9 it is apparatus arithmetic, not a task property. Concretely: on the current suite, fit CI-width ~ f(p̂, Nk), then test whether the residual predicts underspecified/contradictory audit tags beyond M26/M29 scores; if not, the task-intrinsic claim dies.
  • Best improvement: Demote from difficulty axis to mandatory meta-layer on every measure. Report the decomposition, not raw width: budget term / position term / verifier-flake rate / level-adjusted rater residual, standardized to a reference budget (e.g., beta-binomial overdispersion φ). Only the residual counts as task illegibility, and only after showing incremental validity over M26 and M29.

M06 · Latent ability location (IRT b)

M06 · Latent ability location (IRT b): definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Fit a latent-trait model to the solver-by-task outcome matrix: each solver in P receives an ability theta and each task a location b on the same scale, with P(pass) = sigma(theta - b), optionally scaled by a per-task discrimination slope (2PL). The task's difficulty is b (the ability at which its pass probability crosses 50%), a position on a jointly estimated capability axis rather than a rate under one fixed solver mix. Equivalently, the task's Bradley-Terry/Elo rating when every attempt is scored as a solver-vs-task match; under the model's assumptions b is invariant to which solvers from P were sampled, though the scale itself is identified only up to affine transform.

  • Facet: Capability-threshold location: at what solver ability the task flips from mostly-failed to mostly-solved, on an interval scale (the stretched scale) that decompresses the bounded pass-rate tails and, in 2PL, separates where the flip happens (b) from how sharply it happens (discrimination a).
  • Relative to: Solver population P, jointly with the protocol R and verifier V that generate pass/fail; the latent scale is calibrated on the tested panel. A narrow panel makes b panel-relative (one family's training recipe); a wide, diverse panel approaches population-invariance only insofar as unidimensionality holds.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Unidimensional ability is false in detail: tasks mix distinct skill demands, and crossing item-response curves across solver families get averaged away. Solver contamination or prior exposure to a task lowers its b without the task being easy, compressing the scale for the most-copied items. Panels are not iid draws from "possible solvers" (shared training corpora, architectures, recipes correlate examinees), so even consensus b embeds ecosystem-wide bias. Few distinct ability points under-identify the link function, which is per-task untestable; repeated attempts by one solver sharpen cells but add zero examinee diversity. Equal-discrimination (1PL) fails exactly where discrimination varies, including inverse-scaling items. For all-solved/all-failed items b is set mostly by the prior or penalty, and b and a trade off unstably at the extremes, where verifier noise also moves positions a lot. Anchoring is arbitrary, so b is only affine-unique across refits.
  • Related work: Rasch 1960; Bradley & Terry 1952; Elo 1978; Lord 1980; Lalor et al. 2016, 2019 (IRT for NLP); Martínez-Plumed et al. 2019 (item analysis of AI benchmarks); Rodriguez et al. 2021 (evaluation examples are not equally informative); Vania et al. 2021; Polo et al. 2024 (tinyBenchmarks); Kipnis et al. 2024 (metabench); Ruan et al. 2024 (observational scaling laws)

AI-eval instantiation. Build the k-passes-out-of-n binomial matrix from per-model sampled attempts under one harness and verifier. Many diverse examinees at few samples locate b far better than few models at many samples: 20-50 LLMs spanning families, sizes, and generations at 8-16 samples each beats 3 sibling models at 128. Fit Rasch as a binomial GLM with solver and task effects (or 2PL via py-irt/mirt marginal ML), anchor mean theta = 0, and put a hierarchical prior on b so censored all-pass/all-fail items get finite shrunken estimates with honest intervals. Report b with SE, plus drop-one-family refits as a panel-sensitivity diagnostic and a fitted discrimination or 2-factor check for unidimensionality. Shared examinees concurrently calibrate multiple benchmarks onto one equated scale as a byproduct.

Other-domain instantiation. Chess-puzzle ratings: score every attempt as an Elo match between player and puzzle and update both ratings online; a puzzle's converged rating is the player strength at which solve odds are even (Lichess does exactly this). Identically, educational measurement calibrates an item bank by administering items across student cohorts and fitting Rasch: b is the ability at 50% correct, and shared anchor items equate different test forms onto one scale.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks difficulty, but note: under 1PL with a complete fixed panel, pass count is the sufficient statistic, so b is a monotone transform of M01's pass rate and inherits all its variance sources. The extra content IRT claims (interval scale, panel-invariance) is exactly the least valid part. Strongest alternative explanation of b's variance: overlap between the task and the panel's shared training corpora (contamination/coverage) plus panel composition where item curves cross; b locates tasks on "the current ecosystem's shared axis," not an intrinsic capability threshold.
  • Generality: Strongest cross-domain pedigree in the catalog (education, chess puzzles, LLM zoos: anything with a solver-by-task binary matrix). Breaks where no diverse independent solver population exists: frontier one-of-a-kind tasks (open research problems, bespoke embodied-robotics tasks with 1-3 attemptable systems) leave b unidentified; strongly multidimensional suites (mixed vision+tool-use) make b a panel-weighting artifact; all-fail items get prior-determined b.
  • Confound severity: medium
  • Added value over plain success rate: Honest: with a fixed panel and 1PL, essentially none over shrunken pass rate, identical ranking. Real added value is conditional: tail decompression via WHICH solvers pass (separates two 2%-pass tasks), tolerance of missing cells, cross-benchmark equating through shared examinees, and (2PL) separating where the flip happens from how sharply. Its ordering diverges from pooled pass rate only when panels are incomplete or discrimination varies.
  • Gameability: Contaminated/memorized tasks get low b while remaining intrinsically hard. Defective items (broken verifier) pin at extreme b, indistinguishable from genuinely impossible. Saturated all-pass items get prior-set b: pure artifact with a confident-looking number. Panel stuffing with correlated sibling models moves the mean-theta=0 anchor, silently shifting every b; arbitrary anchoring lets refits relabel the whole scale.
  • Cost: Fit cost negligible (binomial GLM). Real cost is the panel: 20-50 diverse models x 8-16 samples/task; often free where leaderboard matrices already exist.
  • Kill test: Disjoint-panel refit invariance: split solvers into unrelated families, refit, affine-align, compare b; if cross-family agreement falls far below same-family split-half reliability, the invariance claim (the measure's whole point) is dead and b is panel-relative. Concrete: Rasch on ~40 LLMs over GPQA+repository-level tasks; compare b from a closed-frontier panel vs an open-weight (Llama/Qwen/Mistral) panel; many items shifting more than one step after alignment kills it.
  • Best improvement: Always co-report raw pass rate and flag b/pass-rate divergences as the informative cases; make M08 misfit/DIF and M17 contamination screens mandatory companions; fix an anchor set of solvers+items so refits are comparable; use hierarchical 2PL with censoring-aware priors; make the drop-one-family refit band, not the model SE, the headline uncertainty.

M07 · Capability slope & diagnosticity

M07 · Capability slope & diagnosticity: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Fix a calibrated ability or resource axis over the solver population P and consider the task's response curve: the probability that verifier V accepts an attempt, as a function of solver ability under protocol R. This measure summarizes the curve's shape rather than its location: the slope (discrimination) per unit ability, the width or abruptness of the low-to-high transition, and the Fisher information the outcome carries about ability at a designated point such as the current frontier. Steep narrow transitions mark threshold-gated tasks; near-zero slopes mark difficulty orthogonal to the axis; negative slopes mark inverse tasks that stronger solvers fail more often; peak information marks tasks that bite exactly at the ability of interest.

  • Facet: Second-order structure of difficulty: sharpness, direction, and diagnostic position of the success transition along an ability axis, orthogonal to location/level; it separates "hard because it demands more capability" from "hard for reasons capability does not buy" (prior misalignment, ambiguity, luck).
  • Relative to: Solver population P equipped with a calibrated ability/resource axis (slope sign and transition width are family- and range-specific), jointly with verifier V, since the scoring rule can induce or dissolve sharpness; the information variant is additionally relative to a chosen frontier ability.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Sparse ability panels barely identify curve shape: slope is confounded with any non-logistic response and correlates with the location estimate; the ability axis is itself estimated (errors-in-variables), and real solver ladders vary in more than the intended capability knob, so slope is not a causal capability response; differential contamination or exposure across solvers mimics or flattens discrimination; abruptness can be a verifier artifact (binary/exact-match scoring makes smooth competence look discontinuous; report graded or log-space companions); floor/ceiling censoring leaves slope unidentified, and a flat slope is ambiguous between saturated and hopeless unless conditioned on location; sampling noise fakes jumps when the total improvement is small; a low slope may reflect off-axis multidimensional difficulty rather than genuine diffuseness, and heterogeneous panel variants strain the unidimensionality assumption; frontier information inherits squared slope noise, conflates level with discrimination by construction, and its verdicts expire as the frontier advances.
  • Related work: Birnbaum 1968 (2PL discrimination); Lord 1980 (item discrimination and information); Wichmann & Hill 2001 (psychometric function slope); van der Linden & Glas 2010 (adaptive testing); Rodriguez et al. 2021 (evaluation examples are not equally informative); Srivastava et al. 2022 (BIG-bench breakthroughness/linearity); Wei et al. 2022 (emergent abilities); Ganguli et al. 2022 (predictability and surprise); Schaeffer et al. 2023 (emergence as metric mirage); McKenzie et al. 2023 (Inverse Scaling Prize); Hu et al. 2023 (PassUntil); Ruan et al. 2024 (observational scaling laws); Polo et al. 2024 (informative-item selection)

AI-eval instantiation. Assemble a solver panel spanning capability: a size ladder within one model family, optionally densified with temperature, checkpoint, and quantization variants as separate examinees, or a broad multi-family panel scored by a common capability index. Run n sampled attempts per solver per task under a fixed harness with automatic verification; fit per task a 2PL logistic of pass probability on ability (joint IRT theta or log-parameters), with Firth penalization against separation. Report: slope with CI and sign class {graded, flat-easy, flat-hard, inverse}; 10-90% transition width; breakthroughness (largest adjacent increment over total increment, in probability and on the stretched scale, with bootstrap P(stepwise), defined only above a binomial noise floor); and Fisher information a^2 P(1-P) at a designated frontier ability, plus each task's share of total test information to expose benchmark redundancy. Compare binary against graded verifiers to detect metric-induced sharpness; cross-tab inverse/flat tasks against specification-defect audits; an ex-ante proxy is the slope of canonical-solution log-likelihood across the ladder.

Other-domain instantiation. Exam item analysis: for each question, plot proportion correct against students' total-score percentile and fit an item characteristic curve: steep items gate a grade boundary, flat items carry no rank information, and items that stronger students miss more often (seductive distractors) are inverse items; exam boards retain items whose information peaks at the pass mark. Athletics contrast: a high-jump bar yields a near-step success profile across athlete ability, while marathon finishing quality degrades smoothly: same difficulty location, opposite transition widths.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: By design it tracks the shape, not the level, of difficulty; it is a second-order property. Much of its empirical variance plausibly reflects measurement machinery rather than task structure: binary/exact-match verifiers manufacture steepness (Schaeffer 2023), sparse ladders (5-10 models) barely identify a 2PL slope, slope and location estimates are correlated, and the ability axis is itself estimated (errors-in-variables). Strongest alternative explanation: slope variance = verifier granularity x panel density, not intrinsic transition width. Low slope is also ambiguous between "capability-orthogonal difficulty" and "wrong axis" (multidimensionality, M08's territory).
  • Generality: Portable wherever a calibrated unidimensional ability axis over many solvers exists (exams, athletics, model ladders). Breaks in one-shot institutional/adversarial domains: a specific merger negotiation or poker against adapting opponents has no solver panel and no fixed response curve; also degrades for heterogeneous multi-skill populations where no single axis is defensible.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: two tasks with identical pooled pass rate (M01) can be a threshold gate vs a flat lottery; slope separates "capability buys success" from luck/ambiguity/prior-misalignment, flags inverse tasks, and prices a task's diagnostic worth at the frontier. This is the catalog's main carrier of second-order structure. But its sign classes partly duplicate M10 (negative slope) and M03 (flat-hard ceiling), and it needs M06's location to be interpretable at all.
  • Gameability: Highly gameable: choose exact-match scoring to sharpen transitions; clip the panel range to censor the transition into any desired class; contaminated tasks (memorized only by large models) masquerade as steep frontier-diagnostic items; label-defective tasks pollute the inverse class; saturated tasks yield unidentified slopes that Firth fits render as confident artifacts; selecting items for peak frontier information Goodharts the benchmark to today's frontier and expires immediately.
  • Cost: Low marginal cost when piggybacking on existing multi-model eval sweeps; high if a dense ladder (10+ solvers x n attempts) must be run per family. Human-domain version needs large examinee samples, standard but expensive.
  • Kill test: Generic: refit the response curve under (a) graded instead of binary scoring and (b) disjoint half-panels; if sign/width class assignments flip for a large share of tasks, the measure reports artifacts. Concrete: take BIG-bench "breakthrough" tasks, rescore with per-token log-likelihood partial credit and bootstrap the model ladder; if >30% of steep/inverse classifications change class or CIs span flat-to-steep, kill it.
  • Best improvement: Make the graded-scoring companion mandatory, report slope-class only when binary and graded fits agree; gate on identifiability (censored/saturated -> "unidentified", never "flat"); cross-screen steep items against M17 contamination and inverse items against spec-defect audits before classification; cede the inverse sign class to M10 and report M07 strictly as {width, discrimination, frontier information} conditioned on M06 location.

M08 · Solver-dependence & scalar validity

M08 · Solver-dependence & scalar validity: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. How far a task's difficulty fails to be a single solver-invariant scalar. Fixing V and R, characterize s by its success-probability profile across population P and measure its departure from one-dimensional (Guttman/Rasch) structure: excess cross-solver dispersion beyond sampling noise at matched overall ability, incomparability with other tasks in the dominance order (some solvers find s harder, others s′), and residual variance off the first factor of the task-by-solver response matrix. High values mean any scalar difficulty assigned to s is an artifact of population composition; the measure is a validity qualifier attached to every other difficulty measure.

  • Facet: Scalar validity / solver-dependence: whether hardness is a location on one ability axis or a task–population relation, an orthogonal coordinate to difficulty level, kin to IRT discrimination and misfit rather than to the difficulty parameter itself.
  • Relative to: Constitutively relative to solver population P: it quantifies non-invariance across P and between named subpopulations (families, species, curricula); and verifier/protocol-relative through the responses. P's diversity sets a ceiling on what is detectable.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Verifier noise manufactures heterogeneity and inflates residuals, worst near success rates 0/1. A homogeneous P (near-clone solvers) understates everything (dominance turns spuriously total, factor dimensions collapse), so scores characterize the sampled population as much as the task; small panels cannot separate idiosyncratic task from idiosyncratic solver, and per-task mass testing needs FDR control. Disagreement peaks at mid difficulty (inverted-U), so it is a complementary coordinate, never a rank substitute. Interface fit (format, tokenization, instruction style) and differential contamination masquerade as deep solver-dependence, while contamination shared by all of P is invisible. DIF ability-matching is circular when DIF is widespread; cross-species equating is a strong, partly untestable assumption; binary-response factoring suffers rotation indeterminacy and difficulty-spread-as-spurious-factor artifacts.
  • Related work: Spearman 1904; Dushnik & Miller 1941 (order dimension); Guttman 1944; Loevinger 1948; Mokken 1971; Cronbach et al. 1972 (generalizability theory); Wright & Masters 1982 (infit/outfit); Holland & Thayer 1988; Swaminathan & Rogers 1990 (DIF); Seung et al. 1992 (query by committee); Reckase 2009 (MIRT); Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017; Lalor et al. 2016/2019; Vania et al. 2021; Hernández-Orallo et al. 2014; Martínez-Plumed & Hernández-Orallo 2020; McKenzie et al. 2023; Burnell et al. 2023; Ruan et al. 2024; Ilić & Gignac 2024; Zhang et al. 2024 (GSM1k)

AI-eval instantiation. Run M models (≥3 families, spread of sizes) × tasks × k sampled attempts, auto-verified. Per task report: (1) heterogeneity: LR test of a common success rate plus residual deviance from a fitted ability curve (Rasch infit/outfit), FDR-corrected; (2) order violations: largest weaker-beats-stronger inversion, and the comparability fraction: share of tasks dominance-ordered with CI-separated rates across ≥95% of models; (3) factor residual: logistic PCA/MIRT factor-1 communality vs residual; (4) behavioral contention: normalized MI between model identity and answer signature, catching models converging on different wrong answers at equal pass rates; (5) family DIF at Mantel-Haenszel-matched ability. Benchmark-level: Loevinger H and the scree curve as "difficulty dimensionality"; gate every scalar difficulty ranking on these. A human-examinee arm (joint Rasch calibration on a stratified task subsample) adds species-level DIF but needs new data.

Other-domain instantiation. Olympiad problems across national cohorts: at matched total score, test whether the intended P1<P2<P3 ordering holds in every cohort or reverses for differently-trained teams (geometry-heavy curricula inverting the order); publish the problem set's dominance partial order, each item's comparability fraction, and cohort DIF. Same recipe: language-group DIF review in standardized testing; decathlon events' loading on general athleticism versus event-specific residual.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: By design it does not track difficulty level; it tracks whether a solver-invariant scalar exists. That framing is honest, but most of its empirical variance has a mundane alternative source: (a) mechanical dependence on pass rate (heterogeneity, DIF power, and factor residuals all peak near p=0.5 and vanish near 0/1, so scores are partly a nonlinear transform of M01); (b) panel composition: the same task scores anywhere from invariant to wildly solver-dependent depending on who is sampled; (c) interface fit and differential contamination masquerading as deep non-invariance. It measures a task-panel interaction, not a task property, unless panel-replicability is demonstrated.
  • Generality: Applies wherever a diverse solver population gives repeated verified attempts: LLM zoos, cohort testing, sports. Breaks at frontier tasks with 1-2 capable solvers (research math), one-shot human tasks, monoculture populations (similar-data LLMs make dominance spuriously total), and cross-species settings where ability equating is untestable.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: pooled pass rate cannot say whether a difficulty ranking is an artifact of who was averaged. It catches equal-pass-rate tasks where models fail differently (contention/MI) and flags rank reversals across families: the only catalog measure that audits whether "difficulty" is well-posed at all. It adds a validity gate, not a difficulty value.
  • Gameability: Panel curation games it both ways: a same-family size ladder makes any benchmark look cleanly unidimensional; one adversarially odd solver manufactures solver-dependence. Saturated tasks (p≈1 everywhere) trivially score "perfectly scalar." Defective verifiers and differentially memorized/contaminated tasks score as maximal solver-dependence, conflating leakage with deep non-invariance; contamination shared by the whole panel is invisible. Five parallel statistics invite forking-paths cherry-picking.
  • Cost: Moderate: reuses the M×N×k response matrix M06/M07 already need; marginal cost is analysis (MIRT, MH-DIF, FDR) plus mandating ≥3 model families. Human/species arm is expensive new data.
  • Kill test: Split-panel replication with pass-rate partialed out: estimate per-task scores on two disjoint, ability-matched, equally family-diverse panels; if cross-panel rank correlation of DIF/misfit/comparability is weak after regressing out |p−0.5|, the measure characterizes panels, not tasks. Concrete: 24 open models (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma, DeepSeek, Phi) split into two 12-model panels on GSM8K+BBH; require split-half Spearman >0.5 on residualized per-task scores.
  • Best improvement: Make panel-replicability (G-theory generalizability coefficient) a mandatory reported statistic; residualize all outputs on |p−0.5|; separate shallow DIF via format-randomization ablation and contamination screens (coordinate with M17) before claiming deep solver-dependence; collapse the five statistics to two headline numbers (comparability fraction, factor-1 residual). Keep distinct from M05 (sampling precision) and M07 (slope): it is structural, not statistical, uncertainty.

M09 · Resource-threshold horizon (scale)

M09 · Resource-threshold horizon (scale): definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Fix a solver family parameterized by a single titratable resource r (model scale, training or inference compute, attempts, time, information access) and a success criterion τ under verifier V. The measure is D_τ(s) = inf{r : p(s, solver(r), R, V) ≥ τ}: the minimal resource at which fitted success probability reaches criterion, i.e., the inverse of the capability–resource curve evaluated at the task. Difficulty inherits the resource's physical units (so on a ratio-scaled axis "twice as hard" is meaningful), and is censored (below the smallest tested budget, or infinite/right-censored) when the curve sits above or never reaches τ.

  • Facet: Capability demand in resource units: where the acquisition threshold sits along a titrated resource axis. Converts difficulty from probability units into interpretable physical units, ranks tasks that raw success rates leave tied at floor, and supports (cautious) extrapolation beyond the tested range.
  • Relative to: A resource-parameterized solver family (or one population run at varying budgets) under fixed protocol R and verifier V. The choice of resource axis and of τ are part of the measure. Explicitly family-relative: units are "family-parameters" or "family-FLOPs", and cross-family invariance is an empirical claim to test, not an assumption: no solver-independent reading.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Different resource axes induce different orderings (compute-hard ≠ knowledge-hard ≠ time-hard ≠ attempts-hard), so the axis choice smuggles in a theory of difficulty. τ is arbitrary and reorders tasks wherever curves cross. Non-monotone or plateauing curves (documented inverse-scaling tasks) make the infimum unstable or undefined; fits must be gated on monotonicity. Heavy two-sided censoring leaves floor/ceiling tasks tied. Few ladder points make the fitted crossing fragile, and the parametric form (logistic in log-resource) can be misspecified where solvability is threshold-like: metric-induced "emergence" artifacts. Ladder steps within a family differ in data mix, distillation, and tuning, so the resource is an impure ability axis. Memorization/contamination shifts whole curves left, deflating the threshold regardless of intrinsic difficulty. Extrapolated crossings beyond the tested range are recipe-specific speculation. On the attempts axis it is a deterministic transform of per-attempt success probability and inherits all its confounds.
  • Related work: Fechner 1860; Cornsweet 1962 (psychophysical staircase/threshold titration); Levin 1973 (time-bounded search cost); Kaplan et al. 2020 (capability–compute curves); Srivastava et al. 2022 (BIG-bench per-task scaling); Wei et al. 2022 (emergent abilities); Schaeffer et al. 2023 (emergence as metric artifact); Hu et al. 2023 (PassUntil cross-scale extrapolation); OpenAI 2023 GPT-4 TR (predictable scaling of difficulty buckets); Owen 2024 (benchmark predictability from scale); Ruan et al. 2024 (observational scaling laws); Brown et al. 2024 (inference-compute scaling); Kwa et al. 2025 (METR human-time horizons)

AI-eval instantiation. Take a fixed-recipe model family spanning several sizes (e.g., 0.5B–32B); run every size on every task with an identical harness, 64–128 sampled attempts each, automatic verification. Per task fit a binomial logistic p(x) = sigmoid(a(x − b)) in x = log non-embedding parameters, partially pooling slopes a across tasks to stabilize few-point fits; report S50 = b, convertible to training FLOPs via 6ND when the family shares a data budget. Report interval-censored values ("≤ smallest size", "> largest size, extrapolated b with bootstrap CI, flagged") rather than bare point estimates, and exclude non-monotone tasks. The same recipe on other axes is computable from one model's rollouts: minimal k with pass@k ≥ 0.5 (log2 attempts), minimal thinking-token or tool-call budget to criterion. Replicating on a second family's ladder tests family-invariance.

Other-domain instantiation. Classical psychophysical titration: staircase the exposure duration of a mammogram until a radiologist cohort detects the lesion at 80% accuracy; the crossing duration is the image's difficulty in milliseconds. Likewise: minimal search depth/nodes at which a chess engine finds a study's winning line; number of practice sessions before a climbing route is sent with 50% reliability; minimal magnification at which microscopists reach criterion agreement on a slide.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6.5/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks capability demand, but S50 is where a particular recipe's data/tuning mix first covers the task, not intrinsic demand: variance is plausibly dominated by corpus coverage, contamination onset, small-model formatting/tokenization failures, and metric discretization rather than difficulty. Strongest alternative explanation: it is a recipe-coverage coordinate; the same task moves substantially between ladders trained on different mixes (BIG-bench per-task heterogeneity), and tau/axis choices reorder tasks wherever curves cross.
  • Generality: Needs a titratable solver ladder plus binary criterion; fine for psychophysics and engines. Breaks where no resource ladder exists: one-shot human professional populations (you cannot titrate a radiologist's "scale"; duration-staircasing changes the task), open-ended creative work without tau, and inverse-scaling tasks, which it must exclude, censoring exactly the structure M10 covers.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real but narrow: separates tasks a strong model leaves tied at 0% (by where on the ladder they crack), yields ratio-scale physical units (FLOPs/params) usable for forecasting when a task becomes solvable. For mid-range tasks it is nearly a monotone transform of one large model's pass rate, so the added value concentrates at the floor and in (flagged) extrapolation.
  • Gameability: Saturated tasks collapse to "<= smallest size" ties; defective/unsolvable tasks become right-censored and masquerade as maximally hard; contamination shifts curves left so memorized tasks look easy (needs M17 gate). Thresholds are manufacturable: discontinuous metrics (exact match) sharpen fake emergence (Schaeffer 2023), rare formats/tokenization make small models fail on parsing not content, and parametric-form or ladder-density choices tune extrapolated values.
  • Cost: Cheap on inference axes (attempts, tokens) from existing rollouts; moderate using public ladders (Pythia/OLMo/Qwen: ~6-8 sizes x 64-128 attempts per task); prohibitive if a fixed-recipe family must be trained. Fitting/censoring machinery is trivial by comparison.
  • Kill test: Generic: cross-family invariance vs. single-model redundancy; compute D_tau on two independent fixed-recipe ladders; if between-ladder rank agreement is no better than what one big model's plain pass rate already predicts, the physical-units framing adds nothing over M01/M06. Concrete: fit S50 for ~200 BIG-bench-hard tasks on Pythia (70M-12B) and on the OLMo ladder at tau=0.5 and tau=0.8; report Spearman(S50_pythia, S50_olmo) against Spearman(S50, largest-model pass rate) and the fraction of tasks whose rank flips across tau.
  • Best improvement: Restrict M09 to the scale/compute axis and explicitly delegate other axes (attempts->M02, thinking tokens->M11, training tokens->M12, human time->M27, hints->M32). Make two-ladder replication with reported cross-family rank agreement, censoring fractions, tau-sensitivity band, continuous scoring, and an M17 contamination gate mandatory parts of the reporting standard, not optional caveats.

M10 · Inverse scaling & error persistence

M10 · Inverse scaling & error persistence: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Given a capability-ordered sequence of solver populations P_1 < … < P_k attempting specification s under fixed protocol R and verifier V, this measures the negative component of the capability gradient: how much success falls, rather than rises, as solvers strengthen. A companion error-structure reading tracks the dominant wrong behavior's mass along the same ordering: whether the modal error dissolves with capability (soluble noise), persists, or grows (a lure rooted in priors that strengthening amplifies). High values mark difficulty consisting of misalignment between the task's demands and the defaults capable solvers acquire: invisible to pooled success rates and not fixed, possibly worsened, by capability growth.

  • Facet: Robustness to lures / prior–task misalignment: the sign and shape of the capability response rather than its level, whether difficulty is capability-soluble or a stable-to-worsening misconception ("fails more" vs "fails the same way more").
  • Relative to: A specific capability ordering of solver populations (typically one training-recipe or curriculum family); the gradient's sign can flip across orderings, so it is ordering-conditional until replicated across families. Also verifier-relative: reversals can be artifacts of V pinning arbitrary conventions.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: False reversals from multiple comparisons unless FDR-controlled. Many surviving reversals are verifier artifacts (tests pinning an arbitrary convention) or spec defects (underspecification, contradiction) rather than solver-relevant difficulty; without a validity screen this measures benchmark defects. Verifier flakiness adds noise. Few capability rungs give a coarse gradient: U-shaped profiles (dip then recover) are invisible or misclassified, and slopes fit to ~3 points are unstable. Strong solvers fail rarely, so error-mass estimates are noisiest exactly where the question matters. Matching error classes across solvers can break on non-critical differences (mitigate by comparing behavior only on inputs where classes diverge from canonical). A single-family ladder conflates capability with recipe: the "strengthening priors" may be training-exposure artifacts, and contamination can differ by rung.
  • Related work: McKenzie et al. 2023 (Inverse Scaling Prize); Wei et al. 2022 (inverse scaling can become U-shaped); Lin et al. 2022 (TruthfulQA, capability-anticorrelated task); Schaeffer et al. 2023 (emergence as metric artifact); Luchins 1942 (Einstellung); Bilalić et al. 2008 (expert lures in chess).

AI-eval instantiation. Fix one training-recipe model family across sizes (e.g., 1.5B/3B/7B); sample ~128 attempts per task per size under identical prompting, giving pass rates p_i. Inverse-scaling score IS(s) = max over ordered pairs i<j of (p_i − p_j), zeroed unless significant (two-proportion z, BH-FDR across the task set); variant: area between the size profile and its isotonic regression. Error-persistence companion: cluster wrong outputs into behavior classes by signature on the inputs where they diverge from canonical; fit the modal wrong class's mass vs log(params); label dissolving (slope<0), persistent (≈0, mass above threshold), or anti-scaling (>0). Screen out reversals whose audit tags show spec defects or convention-pinning tests; only lure-driven reversals count (e.g., a stronger prior for a convenient idiom that violates an explicit unconditional edge-case requirement).

Other-domain instantiation. Chess tactics puzzles on a server logging solver rating per attempt: bin attempts by Elo band, compute per-puzzle solve rate by band, and flag puzzles where the rate drops significantly for stronger bands (FDR-controlled across the puzzle pool). Code wrong first moves: a puzzle is a lure when the modal wrong move is a stereotyped motif (the standard-looking capture or mating pattern) whose share grows with rating, expertise-strengthened pattern priors misfiring (Einstellung). Analogues: clinical vignettes where seniors anchor on the classic presentation of an atypical case; children's over-regularization ("goed" appearing after "went" was mastered) as U-shaped learning.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Tracks prior-task misalignment, not difficulty-as-demand; a task trivially easy under one convention scores high if strengthening priors point elsewhere. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: benchmark defects and recipe artifacts (convention-pinning verifiers, mislabeled keys, rung-varying contamination, RLHF refusal/format changes parsed as failure). The Inverse Scaling Prize's own aftermath (Wei 2022: most winners went U-shaped) suggests flagged reversals are often properties of the sampled ladder segment, not the task. The audit screen helps but is itself judged and leaky.
  • Generality: Needs a defensible unidimensional capability ordering plus many attempts per rung; works for model-size ladders and chess Elo bins. Breaks where stronger populations self-select onto different instances (clinical: seniors see the atypical cases, so per-vignette rate drops confound selection with Einstellung) and in taste-verified domains (essay quality) where the ordering and the verifier share priors. Zero for the large majority of tasks, so it is a rare-event flag, not a scale.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: the sign of the capability response, and whether the modal error dissolves or grows; pooled success cannot distinguish "hard because rare" from "hard because misleading," and this predicts which failures scaling will not fix (safety-relevant). But it decomposes as sign-bit of M07's slope plus longitudinal M20, gated by M17's paraphrase screen; the unique residual is the error-trajectory reading.
  • Gameability: Trivially manufactured: pin an anti-conventional answer key and better models score worse. Adversarial prompt selection creates reversals that vanish under paraphrase. Memorization gamed both ways: small-rung contamination fakes reversals; stronger-model contamination masks real lures. Near-saturation, ceiling noise plus max-over-pairs (upward-biased statistic) yields spurious flags even FDR-controlled. Refusals/format drift at instruct rungs masquerade as anti-scaling errors.
  • Cost: Moderate-high: k rungs x ~128 attempts per task, an intact single-recipe ladder, error clustering, and a manual audit screen (the labor bottleneck). Near-free in logged domains (chess servers).
  • Kill test: Cross-ordering replication with ladder extension: reversals that are task properties must keep their sign and modal-error class under a different recipe family and added rungs. Concretely: take FDR-surviving reversals on Pythia 1.4B-12B, re-run on Qwen and OLMo ladders plus a larger/CoT rung; if under ~30% retain a significant negative gradient and matching error class, M10 is measuring ordering and verifier artifacts.
  • Best improvement: Redefine the score as cross-family sign agreement (min over >=2 recipe families), require paraphrase robustness (M17 gate), report profile class (monotone-down / U-shaped) from isotonic fits instead of biased max pairwise drop, separate refusal/format failures before error-persistence fitting, and emit a categorical flag with effect size rather than a difficulty scalar, or formally fold it in as the signed extension of M07 crossed with M20's trajectory.

M11 · Serial deliberation demand

M11 · Serial deliberation demand: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The quantity of sequential deliberation (working time, reasoning steps, or thinking budget expended before committing to an answer) that a task demands from a solver for success. It has an observational form (expected effort-to-succeed, modeled jointly with accuracy so speed and success are separate parameters) and a causal form (the smallest imposed deliberation budget at which success probability crosses a threshold). Two tasks with identical pass rates can demand very different amounts of work, so this orders tasks on an axis orthogonal to success odds.

  • Facet: Serial reasoning depth / effort demanded before success: the sequential complement to parallel-sampling breadth (how long one attempt must think, not how many attempts are needed), distinct from pass probability.
  • Relative to: Solver population P plus the elicitation/budget-control protocol R: a (task, solver, elicitation) property. Cross-solver agreement of the demanded budget is itself the test of whether the demand generalizes beyond one policy; values are not composable across solver families.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Observed effort conflates demanded thinking with demanded output size and verbosity/style (residualizing on reference-solution length only partly separates them). Trained-in overthinking inflates deliberation on easy items, and topic-triggered verbosity inflates it by genre, independent of difficulty. Conditioning on successes biases hard-task demand downward (only lucky short paths survive). Length caps and budget sweeps censor the distribution on both sides. Imposed budgets perturb behavior (mid-thought truncation, forced-continuation artifacts), so budget–success curves mix budget effects with elicitation effects; results are scaffold- and stop-condition-sensitive. Short tasks saturate at every budget, leaving no dynamic range. Memorization/contamination collapses demand (a recalled answer needs no deliberation). Solver-specific effort units (tokenizers, speed) require per-solver scale terms.
  • Related work: van der Linden 2006, 2007 (lognormal response-time model; hierarchical speed–accuracy); Thissen 1983 (timing in testing); Snell et al. 2024 (scaling test-time compute); Muennighoff et al. 2025 (budget forcing); Guo et al. 2025 (DeepSeek-R1 inference-time scaling); Chen et al. 2024 (overthinking on easy items); Sui et al. 2025 (efficient-reasoning survey)

AI-eval instantiation. Tier 1, from existing rollouts: token-count each sampled solution or reasoning trace; fit the lognormal RT model log tokens_{m,t,r} = beta_t + tau_m + eps jointly (or two-stage) with a Rasch accuracy model; report time-intensity beta_t overall and conditional on passing, residualized against reference-solution length to isolate deliberation from output size. Tier 2, causal (new runs): a budget-controllable reasoning model with budget forcing of that kind at thinking caps {0, 256, 1K, 4K, 16K} tokens x 16 samples/task under the unchanged automatic verifier; fit pass@1 vs log-budget; report B = the 50% crossing with two-sided censoring, median thinking tokens on passing runs, and the sign of the budget gradient (tasks that worsen with more thinking are overthinking lures worth flagging). Run 2+ model families and check rank agreement of B.

Other-domain instantiation. Timed human testing: log per-item response times in an exam and fit van der Linden's hierarchical speed–accuracy model; the item's time-intensity parameter is its deliberation demand, separated from its difficulty. Causal version via speededness: administer matched forms with per-item time limits (10s / 30s / 2min / untimed) to comparable examinee groups; an item's demand is the smallest limit at which accuracy reaches, say, 90% of its untimed level. Chess analog: a position's demand is the time control (bullet/blitz/rapid/classical) at which strong players' engine-verified move quality crosses a fixed threshold.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks difficulty. The observational form largely tracks the solver's learned effort-allocation policy plus required output size: reasoning-trained models spend tokens by genre (overthinking on easy math), and conditioning on successes selects lucky short paths on hard items. Strongest alternative explanation of variance: verbosity/genre policy + output length, not demanded deliberation. The causal B* form is closer to true demand but inherits elicitation artifacts (mid-thought truncation, forced continuation), and is only meaningful where serial computation (not knowledge or luck) is the bottleneck.
  • Generality: Broad in principle (human RT psychometrics, chess time controls, LLM thinking budgets). Breaks on retrieval/knowledge tasks: if the fact is unknown, no budget crosses 50% (B* undefined, RT flat). Also breaks on physical/interactive tasks where elapsed time is execution or environment latency, and on populations that cannot be budget-controlled cleanly.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: separates long-think from many-try difficulty; pass-rate-matched tasks can differ 100x in required serial compute; predicts inference cost and flags overthinking lures via negative budget gradients. Serial complement to M02, behavioral realization of M14; overlaps M27's human elapsed time but factor-separates speed from ability.
  • Gameability: Contaminated/memorized items collapse to near-zero demand and masquerade as easy. Saturated tasks are left-censored at budget 0 (no dynamic range); defective never-passed tasks have no B. As an optimization target it Goodharts: distilling short reasoning paths deflates B without the task changing; models can pad thinking tokens or covertly reason in the answer channel; long-output requirements inflate observational demand.
  • Cost: Tier 1 nearly free (token counts + joint lognormal/Rasch fit on existing rollouts). Tier 2 moderate: ~80-160 runs/task (5 budgets x 16 samples x 2+ families) with budget-forcing scaffolds. Human causal version (matched timed forms) is expensive.
  • Kill test: Generic: on pass-rate-matched task pairs, test whether demanded-budget rank survives a change of elicitation protocol and solver family after residualizing output length; if not, it is a policy/elicitation artifact, not a task property. Concrete: for MATH items with pass@1 in [0.4, 0.6] for both models, compute B* via budget forcing and via prompt-based token caps for two reasoning-model families; if cross-protocol Spearman falls well below within-protocol cross-family agreement (e.g., <0.5), the causal claim dies.
  • Best improvement: Make causal B the definition; demote observational beta to a proxy validated against it (this drops confounding to medium). Gate every estimate through the M17 contamination screen. Report a three-valued outcome (saturated-at-zero, B with CI, never-crosses) at multiple thresholds (B*25/50/75) instead of imputing censored values. Require two elicitation protocols and two solver families with published rank agreement; residualize on required output length with genre fixed effects; measure only the thinking channel, separated from the answer channel.

M12 · Acquisition horizon & contingency

M12 · Acquisition horizon & contingency: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Index the solver population P by developmental time: accumulated learning experience along one training trajectory, or successive solver generations over calendar time. The measure maps (s, P, R, V) to the earliest trajectory position at which V-verified success crosses and holds a threshold: difficulty as order of acquisition, right-censored for tasks never acquired. A companion component measures contingency: the dispersion of that acquisition point across independent realizations of the same developmental process, distinguishing law-like acquisition (every run crosses at the same point) from lottery-like (run-dependent).

  • Facet: When along the experience/time axis the capability crystallizes, and how predictable that crossing is: a trajectory facet invisible to end-state scores or capacity ladders (two tasks with identical final solve rates, or identical minimal-solving-scale, can differ in when and how reliably they are acquired).
  • Relative to: Solver population, strongly so: one specific developmental trajectory (fixed recipe and data order, growing experience) or a dated frontier of solver generations; for the contingency component, the recipe treated as a distribution over independent runs. Never solver-independent; V and the threshold set the readout.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Trajectory-idiosyncratic: curriculum/data ordering and mix shift acquisition points wholesale (e.g. a late domain-heavy annealing stage), so the measure reads the schedule as much as the task; it conflates "needs much experience" with "relevant experience is rare in the stream": availability, not intrinsic hardness. Observation granularity coarsens the ordering; weak trajectory families leave a heavy never-acquired (censored) mass. Calendar variants conflate data, method, post-training, and architecture progress into one axis; contamination grows with date (later solvers have seen public tasks), biasing horizons early; date grids are sparse and size classes only approximately matched. A single run gives no variance estimate; the contingency component confounds seed noise with data-order noise unless varied separately, and threshold readouts mechanically couple measured window width to curve abruptness (smooth improvement reads as a wide stochastic window even when curves are identical across runs). Protocol drift along the trajectory (early-stage vs late-stage solvers need different prompting formats); full multi-run designs are prohibitively expensive at relevant scales, and cheap replica proxies may not share the phenomenology (grokking-style delayed acquisition).
  • Related work: Biderman et al. 2023 (Pythia checkpoints and seed ablations); Xia et al. 2023 (training trajectories across scales); Groeneveld et al. 2024 (OLMo); Liu et al. 2023 (LLM360); Wei et al. 2022 (emergence); Kwa et al. 2025 (METR time horizons); Ho et al. 2024 (algorithmic progress); Xiao et al. 2024 (densing law); Sellam et al. 2022 (MultiBERTs); Power et al. 2022 (grokking)

AI-eval instantiation. Token horizon: use an open-training-run model with staged public checkpoints (OLMo-2-7B, Pythia, LLM360 CrystalCoder); sample 16-32 attempts/task at 15-25 log-spaced checkpoints through the existing automatic-verifier harness; acquisition point = first tokens-seen with pass rate above a threshold (0.25-0.5) sustained over 2-3 consecutive checkpoints; difficulty = log10(tokens), right-censored. Calendar horizon: fix a size class (~7B) across release dates from several vendors (DeepSeek-Coder 2023-11, StarCoder2 2024-02, CodeQwen1.5 2024-04, Qwen2.5-Coder 2024-11, Qwen3 2025); fit a per-task logistic of pass rate vs release date; horizon = 50% crossing date, secondary stat pp/year. Contingency: the ideal K>=10 independent pretraining runs per scale is currently a thought experiment; realistic proxy = data-order-permuted continued-pretraining or fine-tuning replicas of a small model, scoring across-replica flip variance per task. Validation: rank-correlate within-run acquisition order against a cross-model capacity ladder to test how much acquisition order transfers across recipes.

Other-domain instantiation. Age-of-acquisition norms in human development: a word's or milestone's difficulty is the median age at which children in a cohort first reliably produce or pass it (WordBank/CDI vocabulary norms; Piagetian conservation tasks), right-censored for skills many people never acquire. Contingency is the inter-child spread of that age: walking is law-like (narrow window near 12 months) while reading readiness or idiom mastery is lottery-like (wide, exposure-order-dependent). Calendar analog: years from a mathematical problem's posing to its first accepted solution, the field-as-population's acquisition horizon, with still-open problems as censored observations.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks difficulty, largely tracks the recipe. Within one run, tokens-seen is a monotone resource axis, so acquisition order mostly re-derives capability threshold (M09/M06) plus relevant-data availability in the stream (M25): the psycholinguistics AoA-vs-frequency collinearity exactly. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: exposure density and curriculum schedule, not intrinsic hardness; the calendar variant additionally reads field progress plus contamination.
  • Generality: Good conceptual generality: age-of-acquisition norms are among the best-validated human difficulty proxies, and time-to-first-solution works for open problems. Breaks wherever solvers have no developmental trajectory: fixed algorithms/tools (a SAT solver acquires nothing), one-shot expert humans, and calendar populations whose composition drifts (a theorem open 300 years may index fashion/attention, not difficulty).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuinely new facet: timing and predictability of crystallization (grokking-style delayed acquisition, law-like vs lottery-like windows, and forecasting when a capability arrives); invisible to end-state rates. Honestly, most of that increment is the contingency component, which is unaffordable in its honest form (K>=10 pretraining runs); the affordable horizon readout adds little beyond M09 on a different axis.
  • Gameability: Contamination pulls horizons early and worsens with date; memorized tasks cross via verbatim recall long before capability (screen with M17). Saturated tasks are left-censored (no signal), defective/never-solved tasks pile into right-censored mass indistinguishable from 'very hard'. A lab can anneal task-adjacent data late to game acquisition curves; threshold+consecutive-checkpoint rules flip with checkpoint spacing and sampling noise near threshold.
  • Cost: Token variant cheap: public checkpoint suites (Pythia/OLMo), inference only (~25 checkpoints x 32 attempts x tasks). Calendar variant very cheap (5-8 released models). Honest contingency prohibitive (multiple pretraining runs); cheap replica proxies of doubtful fidelity.
  • Kill test: Generic: show acquisition order is recipe-invariant AND carries residual information beyond capacity ladder + data availability. Concrete: on ~200 auto-verified tasks, compute token-acquisition on both Pythia-6.9B and OLMo-2-7B checkpoints; if cross-recipe rank correlation < ~0.5 it is a schedule property, and if within-recipe order is explained (R^2 > 0.85) by cross-size S50 (M09) plus Pile frequency of task-relevant n-grams (M25), the measure has no independent content.
  • Best improvement: Report exposure-adjusted acquisition (relevant-tokens-seen, regressing out M25 availability), require two independent trajectory families and keep only the recipe-invariant rank component, replace threshold crossing with fitted latent curve location+slope so the contingency window decouples from abruptness (M07), and gate calendar dating behind an M17 contamination screen. If the recipe-invariant residual proves empty, fold horizon into M09 and contingency into M07.

M13 · Solution-object complexity

M13 · Solution-object complexity: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The static size and structure of the smallest solution object the verifier accepts: how much content must be produced at all (minimal description length under a fixed representation language, a computable proxy for Kolmogorov/MDL complexity), how many independent case distinctions it must encode (decision/branch breadth), and how many partial results must be held simultaneously live while producing or simulating it (state-tracking width). Difficulty is located in the demanded artifact itself, not in any solver's search or the verifier's effort, and is approximated as a minimum over known accepted solutions.

  • Facet: Irreducible construction burden of the target artifact: information content (length, hence log search-space under a prior), case-analysis breadth (control depth), and working-memory width, distinct from how hard the solution is to find, state, or check.
  • Relative to: Nominally solver-independent: a property of the verifier-accepted solution set given V and a fixed representation language/prior (P and R ideally irrelevant). In practice estimated as a min over solutions actually found by a specific population plus an author, so estimates are only as tight as that exploration.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Primitive absorption: one powerful library call/lemma/tool collapses conceptually heavy work into a tiny artifact, so small size is not low insight: knowledge-heavy tasks are underrated, mechanical loop-heavy ones overrated. Short is not easy for lure tasks whose minimal solution is brief but improbable under any solver's prior (the pure quantity is conditional MDL given the specification, which is uncomputable). Exploration bias: hardest tasks yield the smallest solution pools and the weakest golfing, inflating the estimated minimum exactly where difficulty is highest and coupling the measure to pass rate; contamination/memorization can conversely surface anomalously short solutions. Style and idiom variance plus constant boilerplate offsets survive normalization and min-over-variants only partially. Aggregation blindness: cannot distinguish many trivial cases from one genuinely hard one; containers and chunking launder state width. Coarse dynamic range with heavy ties on short tasks; the three components correlate with each other and with raw length, which independently enlarges the per-token error surface (an outcome-side confound when validating against success rates); the empirical link between structural metrics and human effort is weak.
  • Related work: Kolmogorov complexity / MDL (Rissanen 1978; Li & Vitányi 2008); Levin 1973 (universal search); McCabe 1976 (cyclomatic); Halstead 1977 (volume); Chaitin 1982 (register pressure / liveness); Sweller 1988 (cognitive load); Gulwani et al. 2017 (synthesis cost scales with program size); Campbell 2018 (cognitive complexity); Hendrycks et al. 2021 (APPS difficulty tiers); Peitek et al. 2021 (fMRI: complexity metrics vs. comprehension effort); Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal 2024 (state tracking); Hsieh et al. 2024 (RULER).

AI-eval instantiation. Code benchmark with test-suite verifier: pool the reference solution with every verifier-passing sampled rollout, optionally extended by a golfing loop (LLM proposes progressively shorter rewrites; keep only those passing all tests). Normalize each program (parse; strip comments/docstrings/annotations; alpha-rename identifiers; canonical unparse), then take per-task minima of three statics: size (compressed bytes and AST nodes), breadth (cyclomatic decision count, nesting depth), width (peak live-variable count from backward liveness on the CFG). Task value = min over pool (robust: 10th percentile); ex-ante variant uses only the reference plus a few verified refactorings; report reference-minus-min as a style-noise calibration.

Other-domain instantiation. Formalized mathematics: for a theorem in a proof-assistant library (verifier = kernel checker), gather all accepted proofs (the library proof, alternates, community proof-golf entries) and score (a) shortest normalized proof-script/term length, (b) number of case splits and induction branches in that minimal proof, (c) maximum number of hypotheses and open subgoals simultaneously in context at its deepest point. The trio ranks theorems by the burden of the proof object itself, independent of who found it.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks difficulty's necessary floor (you cannot do less than emit the artifact), but the strongest alternative explanation of its variance is demanded output volume: domain verbosity and representation-language choice, i.e., typing not thinking. Fixed-language MDL diverges from length-under-the-solver's-prior exactly on insight tasks (short-but-deep one-liners score trivial). Worse, exploration bias makes the min-estimate loosest on hard tasks, so estimated M13 partially inherits pass rate, manufacturing spurious validation correlations.
  • Generality: Works where verifiers accept constructive artifacts (code, formal proofs, structured plans). Breaks on answer-only domains: multiple-choice or short-answer QA has a one-token minimal object whether the question is trivial arithmetic or a research problem: total tie collapse. Also ill-defined for policies/interaction (negotiation, robotics) and rubric-verified creative work where length is set by the brief.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuinely ex-ante and solver-free: computable from the reference solution before any rollout, unlike success rate. Its real use is decompositional: separating "long but easy" from "short but deep," so pass-rate residuals after conditioning on M13 isolate search/insight difficulty. Modest as a standalone difficulty score; real as a covariate.
  • Gameability: Trivially inflated by demanding verbose output or banning libraries; deflated by primitive absorption (one library call hides the work). Defective verifiers (weak test suites) admit degenerate tiny solutions, silently labeling broken tasks easy. Contamination surfaces anomalously short memorized/golfed solutions. Saturated short-task benchmarks give heavy ties and no dynamic range; containers and chunking launder state width.
  • Cost: Low-moderate: static analysis on already-collected passing rollouts is near-free; the golfing loop adds bounded LLM calls. Fully automatable for code/proofs.
  • Kill test: Generic: build contrast pairs matched on all three statics of the minimal solution but with order-of-magnitude pass-rate gaps (and the converse: equal pass rate, 10x size gap); test whether M13 predicts failure within length-matched strata. Concrete: on Codeforces-style problems, pair div2-A items with 2800+-rated items whose golfed minimal solutions are within ±10% normalized bytes/cyclomatic/width; if M13's partial correlation with model failure vanishes after controlling raw reference length, it measures verbosity. Also bootstrap pool size: if hard-task minima keep shrinking as pools grow, estimates are pass-rate-confounded.
  • Best improvement: Rebrand as a construction-burden floor, never a difficulty score: pin an explicit primitive/library basis, report the three components separately with a min-convergence (pool-size bootstrap) diagnostic, and always pair with solver-prior codelength (M15) so short-but-improbable solutions are flagged as insight-hard rather than easy.

M14 · Serial/compositional depth

M14 · Serial/compositional depth: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The length of the longest chain of essentially sequential, must-all-succeed steps in the dependency structure (subtask or dataflow DAG) of a correct solution to s: the critical path that no reordering or parallelization can shorten; equivalently, how deeply operations compose and how many intermediate results must be related and carried forward, with each link weighted by coupling tightness (can an error at that stage be caught and repaired before contaminating successors?). Its behavioral twin tests whether whole-task difficulty composes from parts: under a serial null law p̂(s) = Π p(s_i) (≈ p^k at depth k for per-stage reliability p), the composition gap Δ = log p(s) − log p̂(s) is the irreducible remainder (Δ<0: emergent interface difficulty; Δ>0: synergy from shared context).

  • Facet: Serial/compositional structure, the span of the required work rather than its volume: chain length, relational order of composed operations, coupling tightness, and multiplicative error propagation. Distinguishes deep-and-narrow from broad-and-shallow tasks: deep, tightly coupled tasks are hard even when every stage is individually easy.
  • Relative to: Quasi solver-independent as a structural property of known solutions, but strategy-relative: the DAG reflects the strategies available to P (a new strategy can shorten the path); the fitted exponent k̂ and gap Δ are estimated from P's error behavior under protocol R, with Δ defined per (task, decomposition, null-law) triple; V enters via what counts as final success and part/whole verifier consistency. Most diagnostic for bounded-serial-depth solvers (fixed-depth architectures, bounded working memory).
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Decomposition is not unique: depth and Δ depend on the annotator's ontology and stage granularity (report inter-annotator agreement); measures a known strategy's structure, so only an upper bound on task-minimal depth; powerful primitives/tools hide depth (one call performs a deep operation at surface depth 1; score a residual-depth variant); correlates with raw horizon and solution length, so compare at fixed duration; a chain of mechanical steps scores equal to a chain of genuine inferences; iteration counting (structural vs unrolled) is definitionally ambiguous and changes rankings; the p^k fit assumes independent, homogeneous stages while attempt-level noise is correlated within a trajectory; parts measured in isolation lose shared context, biasing p̂ in either direction; part verifiers may be leniency-mismatched with the whole-task verifier; silent errors mislocate first failure; coarse ordinal depth rubrics tie heavily where tasks concentrate, and raters may back-infer depth from an imagined implementation.
  • Related work: Kelley & Walker 1959 / Malcolm et al. 1959 (CPM/PERT); Blelloch 1996 (work-span); Ferrante, Ottenstein & Warren 1987 (program dependence graphs); NC-vs-P depth-size tradeoffs; Simon 1962 (near-decomposability); Barlow & Proschan 1975 (series-system reliability); Halford, Wilson & Phillips 1998 (relational complexity); Commons et al. 1998 (hierarchical complexity); Embretson 1998; Dziri et al. 2023 (Faith and Fate); Merrill & Sabharwal 2023 (transformer depth limits); Press et al. 2023 (compositionality gap); Lake & Baroni 2018; Yu et al. 2023 (Skill-Mix); Kwa et al. 2025 (task-length success curves).

AI-eval instantiation. Ex-ante: three annotators or an adjudicated LLM panel, shown the prompt only, decompose each task into a minimal DAG (edge = "consumes the output of"); D = median critical-path length, links weighted by whether an intermediate check exists (loose vs tight coupling); for coding tasks, cross-check against the longest def-use/expression path of the canonical solution and the min over passing rollouts (loops counted structurally, unrolled variant noted). Ex-post: over N attempts with per-stage checks, locate first failures and fit pass ≈ Π p_i (or p^k); report k̂ and whether it predicts pass-rate falloff across model scale at matched human-time horizon. For Δ: run each part standalone with true intermediate state provided; Δ = ln pass(whole) − Σ ln pass(parts).

Other-domain instantiation. Multi-step organic synthesis: depth = number of serial reaction steps to the target molecule, and overall yield is literally the product of per-step yields (published total syntheses report both), so two routes with equal bench-hours but 5 vs 25 serial steps separate depth from horizon. For the composition gap: relay racing (predict team success from individual leg performances; the shortfall from handoff failures is a negative Δ).

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks a real thing (depth-not-work is fundamental: NC vs P, p^k reliability, Dziri-style compositional failure), but as operationalized it measures the annotator's decomposition of one known strategy, not the task. Strongest alternative explanation of variance: solution length/horizon (longer solutions get longer annotated chains, so D is substantially relabeled length); second, annotator granularity and back-inference from felt difficulty. Coupling-weighting is the load-bearing part yet is the noisiest rubric. Depth of mechanical chains (add 20 numbers) is high while difficulty is low for any solver with external memory: validity is solver-architecture-contingent.
  • Generality: Broad in principle (coding, math, synthesis, planning; the chemistry instantiation is genuinely apt). Breaks where difficulty is not chain-shaped: single-step insight problems (a hard riddle or research-taste judgment has depth ~1), perception/recognition tasks with no meaningful subtask DAG, and adversarial/interactive tasks where the DAG is opponent-contingent. Δ requires runnable, verifiable parts, rarely available outside code/math.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: it is ex-ante and mechanistic where pass rate is ex-post and mute. Predicts which equal-pass-rate tasks resist retry/parallel-sampling (deep ones), explains multiplicative falloff, and Δ localizes difficulty in interfaces vs parts. Distinguishes deep-narrow from broad-shallow, invisible to success rate and only partly covered by M13 (volume) and M11 (serial budget).
  • Gameability: Easily gamed: unroll loops or split steps to inflate k arbitrarily; call one powerful tool to collapse a 30-deep task to surface depth 1. Memorized/contaminated items (a published 25-step synthesis route) are solved by depth-0 retrieval, so D wildly overpredicts. Saturated tasks: per-stage p→1 makes p^k flat and depth undiagnostic; defective part-verifiers manufacture fake emergence (Δ<0). Annotators back-infer depth from difficulty, creating circularity.
  • Cost: Ex-ante: cheap-moderate (3 annotators or LLM panel per task). Ex-post k̂ and Δ: expensive (instrumented per-stage checks, N rollouts, and hand-built standalone part-tasks with injected intermediate state); harness engineering dominates.
  • Kill test: Matched-length deconfound: build task pairs identical in solution length, token count, and step count, differing only in dependency structure (k serial vs k parallel-aggregated steps); if annotated D adds no predictive power for pass rate beyond length, or inter-annotator depth agreement is near chance, the measure is exposed. Concrete: arithmetic-DAG or program-synthesis pairs (chain of k dependent ops vs k independent ops then reduce), checking also whether fitted k̂ matches annotated D and whether stage failures are actually independent.
  • Best improvement: Fix identifiability: define edges operationally (typed intermediate artifact that must be carried forward), take min depth over all observed passing strategies, and report residual depth relative to the solver's primitive/tool set. Always report D alongside matched-length contrasts, inter-annotator agreement, and a correlated-stage null (not independent p^k); validate part-verifiers against the whole-task verifier before interpreting Δ.

M15 · Solution codelength & decision entropy

M15 · Solution codelength & decision entropy: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The information a correct solution demands from a solver: the codelength (negative log-probability) of a correct solution under the solver's own predictive distribution (a designated reference solution, or the cheapest correct solution the solver actually found), together with the entropy profile along the solution path: how many genuine forks (high-uncertainty choice points whose branches diverge in eventual verifier outcome) must be resolved, and how much probability mass leaks onto failing branches. Codelength locates how deep the correct region sits in the solver's search distribution, complementing success rate, which measures only its mass; the fork profile distinguishes tasks hard at one critical choice from tasks hard everywhere.

  • Facet: Information/search depth of the nearest correct solution under the solver's proposal distribution, plus the density and localization of critical decision points along the path; equivalently, within-attempt decision work (choice bits resolved during one solution), as opposed to across-attempt retry effort.
  • Relative to: Solver-relative by construction: defined for any solver (or scorer drawn from P) that exposes step-level probabilities; values are calibrated to that solver's distribution and action granularity, so not portable across solvers or tokenizations, and never solver-independent. The solver-independent ideal it approximates (algorithmic probability / Levin search cost) is uncomputable.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Reference idiosyncrasy: an unusual but conceptually easy reference inflates ex-ante codelength; the nearest-correct-attempt variant removes this but is censored exactly on zero-success tasks. Memorization/contamination deflates codelength regardless of conceptual difficulty. Total codelength/entropy couples to solution length; per-step normalization trades that for a style confound. Teacher-forced scoring measures recognition, not generation: exposure bias lets a solver encode cheaply what it never finds. Entropy spent on semantically irrelevant choices (naming, formatting, benign paraphrases) survives imperfect masking. Min-over-k attempts is a noisy order statistic partially circular with success rate; finite sampling temperature explores one slice of the distribution; prefix-tree fork statistics go sparse deep in sequences. Miscalibrated confidence distorts the effort reading; interpret as choices resolved, not belief about correctness. The reference path may route around forks that generated solutions actually hit.
  • Related work: Ethayarajh et al. 2022 (V-usable information); Voita & Titov 2020 (MDL probes); Perez et al. 2021 (Rissanen Data Analysis); Gonen et al. 2023 (perplexity as ease proxy); Chen et al. 2021 (pass@k); Brown et al. 2024 (Large Language Monkeys); Grünwald 2007 (two-part MDL); Solomonoff 1964 / Levin 1973 (algorithmic probability, search cost); Hale 2001, Levy 2008 (surprisal as processing effort); Kadavath et al. 2022 (self-knowledge via token probabilities); Wang et al. 2025 (high-entropy forking tokens in RLVR); Bigelow et al. 2024 (forking paths).

AI-eval instantiation. For a generation benchmark with an automatic verifier (e.g. code + test harness) and an LM solver with logprob access: (1) ex ante, teacher-force the reference solution through the solver, sum -log p over solution tokens (total and per-token; mask comment/whitespace/identifier tokens; drop tasks whose reference fails the harness); (2) ex post, rescore the k stored samples under their generating model and take the minimum total NLL among verifier-passing ones (10th percentile as robust variant; censored at 0/k); (3) record per-token entropy along solutions, count positions with H > 2 bits, and on the prefix tree of the k samples sum probability mass at branch points whose subtree pass rates differ by > 0.5. Use (1)-(2) to order tasks inside the pass-rate ≈ 1 and ≈ 0 buckets where success counts are ties; use (3) to test whether "tricky" tasks localize to few forks.

Other-domain instantiation. Chess tactics puzzles: P = club players in a rating band, V = engine evaluation of the line. From a large game database, estimate the empirical move-choice distribution of that band in each position. A puzzle's codelength is the sum over its solution line of -log(empirical frequency of the correct move); its fork profile is the count of positions where a majority of empirical mass falls on moves the engine scores as losing. High codelength with one fork = a single non-obvious "only move"; high codelength with many forks = a long line hard everywhere. Same confounds apply: famous positions are "memorized" (cheap despite depth), and side-line move-order choices add style entropy.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks difficulty, but NLL variance is dominated by (a) solution length, (b) surface typicality/fluency of the reference under the solver's corpus priors, and (c) success rate itself: min-NLL over passing samples is nearly a smooth transform of pass@1, and teacher-forced scoring measures recognition, not generation. Strongest alternative explanation: "how stylistically familiar and long is this text to this model," not "how hard is the task." The fork profile is the more valid residual, but "H > 2 bits" counts style forks unless branches are verified to diverge in outcome.
  • Generality: Needs a solver exposing step-level probabilities over a discrete action alphabet plus a canonical solution string. Fine for LMs and (cleverly) chess via move-frequency databases. Breaks for continuous motor/physical tasks (surgery, driving: no token alphabet), interactive tasks where no static reference path exists, and human solvers in sparse-data domains (novel research: no empirical choice distribution to estimate).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real but narrower than claimed: (1) ex-ante ordering inside pass≈0 and pass≈1 buckets where success rate is a tie, before any successes exist; (2) the fork-localization profile distinguishes one-critical-insight tasks from uniformly-hard tasks, genuinely invisible to success rate. The raw codelength facet, however, largely duplicates M02 (steering nats / search cost) and M13 (solution MDL); M15's distinct contribution is within-attempt decision structure, not total information.
  • Gameability: Contamination collapses codelength on memorized tasks (famous chess positions, leaked benchmark solutions) while difficulty is unchanged; fine-tuning on references zeroes it. Verbose or idiosyncratic references inflate it arbitrarily (padding with rare identifiers games it upward). Masking policy and decoding temperature are free parameters that move fork counts. On saturated benchmarks it "works" only if references weren't in pretraining: usually false. Defective tasks with wrong references pass teacher-forcing silently unless the harness filter runs.
  • Cost: Cheap for logprob-exposing LMs: one teacher-forced pass per task plus rescoring stored samples. Moderate for fork statistics (prefix trees over k samples). Expensive-to-infeasible for closed APIs hiding logprobs and for humans outside data-rich games.
  • Kill test: Generic: within strata matched on pass rate and token length, test whether residual codelength/fork-count still predicts an external difficulty criterion (human solve time, hint dose M32); and inject references into fine-tuning to see if the measure collapses under unchanged true difficulty. Concrete: on wave 1, take length-matched pairs of boilerplate-heavy easy tasks vs short trick tasks; if per-token NLL orders them by style familiarity rather than human-rated difficulty, the codelength facet is dead.
  • Best improvement: Drop total NLL as headline; report contrastive codelength (reference NLL minus NLL under an answer-conditioned or paraphrase-averaged baseline) to cancel length/style, and define forks counterfactually: a position counts only if resampled branches provably diverge in verifier outcome. Add a contamination canary. Consider folding the codelength facet into M02 and keeping M15 as the fork-profile measure only.

M16 · Prompt informativeness & example load

M16 · Prompt informativeness & example load: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. How much usable information the task statement s transmits toward a verifier-accepted solution, and where that information lives across the statement's channels (prose description versus worked examples/demonstrations). Informativeness is the gain in solution encodability or success probability from conditioning on the full statement rather than a null or ablated one; channel load is the loss from deleting one channel; the demonstration coverage gap is the fraction of V-graded behavior classes never illustrated by any in-statement example. A task reads as hard when s transmits little usable information, or when its intent is concentrated in demonstrations that only partially cover the graded behavior, forcing solvers to infer rather than read the requirement.

  • Facet: Information transfer from specification to solution, guidance sufficiency versus inferential burden: does s pin down what V will accept, and through which channel.
  • Relative to: Informativeness is P-relative: usable information is defined against a reference solver's prior, and channel importance can differ across solver populations. The coverage gap is a property of the (s,V) pair, near solver-independent among solvers that use examples to disambiguate specifications.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Surface token overlap between statement and solution (names, example constants reused verbatim) inflates informativeness without semantic transfer. Memorization/contamination raises null-conditioned success and deflates it: tasks easy from prior alone read as uninformative. The null/ablation baseline matters: empty statements are off-distribution for many solvers, shuffled ones retain vocabulary information. Ablation surgery changes length and format (template artifact; needs matched-length filler), and where demonstrations double as the binding specification, deleting them creates a genuinely different, more ambiguous task, so the delta partly measures the mutant rather than the original. Likelihood-based versions inherit reference-solution idiosyncrasy. Coverage is not communication: one pragmatically chosen example can pin a rule whose remaining cases follow logically, so the gap overestimates inferential burden; when format fixes the example count, the gap largely re-expresses verifier test breadth (batch effect).
  • Related work: Ethayarajh et al. 2022 (V-usable information / PVI); Xu et al. 2020 (usable information under computational constraints); Min et al. 2022 (rethinking demonstrations); Gonen et al. 2023 (perplexity and prompt choice); Mizrahi et al. 2024 (prompt-variant sensitivity); Pu et al. 2020 (pragmatic example choice in synthesis); Gulwani 2011 (programming-by-example ambiguity); Liu et al. 2023

AI-eval instantiation. On a program-synthesis benchmark with reference solutions and test suites: (1) PVI: under a fixed open model, NLL of the reference solution given the full prompt minus NLL given a null prompt ("Write a Python function."), with shuffled-word and signature-only prompts as robustness baselines. (2) Channel load: build {full, description-only, examples-only, signature-only} variants by stripping doctest/assert examples (matched-length filler control); take NLL deltas ex ante and pass-rate deltas over ~16 sampled attempts per condition through the existing verification harness ex post. (3) Coverage gap = 1 − |behavior signatures hit by in-prompt example inputs| / |signatures across the full test suite|. Read jointly with pass rate as a 2x2: low PVI & low pass flags underspecification; low PVI & high pass flags prior/memorization doing the work; high PVI & low pass flags information transmitted but not usable for generation.

Other-domain instantiation. Classroom assessment: students (P) receive an assignment brief (s) consisting of prose instructions plus one worked exemplar; the grading rubric is V. Randomize sections to full brief, instructions-only, exemplar-only, and title-only conditions. Informativeness = mean score gain of full brief over title-only; channel load = score drop under each single-channel ablation; coverage gap = fraction of rubric criteria not instantiated anywhere in the exemplar. Prediction: criteria absent from the exemplar are the ones most frequently failed.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Tracks specification quality and inferential burden, not difficulty itself, and is direction-ambiguous: a fully explicit spec for a deep theorem pins down V's criterion yet yields low PVI because the solution is underivable, conflating "not stated" with "not derivable by this solver." Strongest alternative explanation of variance: surface token overlap between prompt and reference solution plus reference-solution length/style; NLL deltas reward string copyability, not semantic guidance.
  • Generality: Works for any instructed task with a linguistic statement (code, classroom, translation). Breaks in embodied/interactive domains where the "statement" is the environment: a chess position or a leaking pipe has no prose/example channel split, no null-prompt ablation, and often no reference solution for NLL.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real but diagnostic, not scalar: the 2x2 with pass rate separates underspecification from memorization from transmitted-but-unusable information, ex ante and without solving; coverage gap predicts which behavior classes fail. As a standalone difficulty number it adds little; the definition itself requires reading it jointly with pass rate.
  • Gameability: Pad prompts with solution-adjacent tokens (signatures, constants) to inflate PVI without adding guidance; add trivial examples hitting every test signature to zero the coverage gap; contaminated/memorized items have low null-prompt NLL, so saturated benchmarks uniformly read "uninformative" regardless of true difficulty; matched-length filler controls are reverse-engineerable; deleting binding examples creates a mutant task, so channel-load deltas can be gamed by moving the spec between channels.
  • Cost: Cheap-to-moderate: a few forward passes per variant for NLL; ~64 sampled attempts per task (16 x 4 conditions) ex post; instrumenting per-test behavior signatures is the main engineering cost.
  • Kill test: Manipulate surface overlap while holding semantic content fixed; if PVI moves with overlap rather than content, it is surface-tracking. Concretely: paraphrase wave 1 prompts to zero identifier/constant overlap with canonical solutions (verify pass rates unchanged); if PVI's rank correlation with the original drops below ~0.5, kill the PVI core. Secondary: check that uncovered behavior classes actually fail more than covered ones controlling for test breadth.
  • Best improvement: Reframe as inferential burden = bits V requires minus bits s transmits, in the same advice-length units as M32 (its scalar reading is nearly the complement of hint dose, adjacent to M36 observability); compute PVI paraphrase-averaged over zero-overlap rewrites, gated by an M17 contamination screen; deflate coverage gap by logical closure of shown examples and validate against realized per-class failure rates.

M17 · Shortcut, memorization & surface robustness

M17 · Shortcut, memorization & surface robustness: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The degree to which a task can be passed without comprehension of its specification, by blind guessing against a permissive verifier, by predicting the target from minimal surface cues (its name, format, or embedded examples), or by retrieving a memorized or publicly available solution, together with the stability of outcomes under meaning-preserving restatements of the specification. It is a validity screen: where this zero-comprehension floor is high, or outcomes and dominant errors are locked to one phrasing, every other difficulty measure for the task reflects something other than problem hardness.

  • Facet: Shortcut availability / guessability: the lower asymptote of success attainable with zero comprehension, and whether measured difficulty belongs to the task's semantics rather than to one phrasing, a weak verifier, or prior exposure. A validity gate for all other measures.
  • Relative to: Jointly relative to a floor population of minimally competent and scripted solvers below P; to P's training/exposure corpus (retrievability is corpus-relative); to verifier V (a permissive V raises the floor); and to protocol R's phrasing, since the paraphrase set defines the equivalence class "same task".
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Weak solvers retain genuine skill, so empirical floors only upper-bound pure guessing, and 3PL-style guessing parameters are classically unstable and prior-sensitive. A high floor conflates guessable specification with weak verification unless a cross-solution/verifier-strength control is run. Minimal-cue predictability entangles genuine cue informativeness with contamination (rename or decontaminated-probe reruns needed to split them); low values may reflect idiosyncratic naming rather than hardness; interpretation assumes solvers share the probe's prior; cue-rich prompt formats are partly what is measured. Retrieval signals cannot distinguish leaked items from legitimately common patterns (both imply recall-easiness; only one is an artifact), have modest per-item discrimination, miss knowledge whose verbatim regurgitation tuning suppressed, and reference an unobservable training corpus. Paraphrase generators can subtly alter terse specs where every word is load-bearing; uniform drops across paraphrases conflate broken memorization with genuine fragility; template/formatting sensitivity adds phrasing-independent variance.
  • Related work: Birnbaum 1968 (3PL guessing parameter); San Martín et al. 2015 (3PL identifiability); Hindle et al. 2012 (naturalness/identifier predictability); Carlini et al. 2021, 2023 (extraction; quantifying memorization); Shi et al. 2024 (Min-K% Prob); Riddell et al. 2024 (code-benchmark contamination); Golchin & Surdeanu 2024 (time-travel contamination tests); Liu et al. 2023 (test strength); Sclar et al. 2024 (prompt-format sensitivity); Mizrahi et al. 2024 (multi-prompt evaluation); Wang et al. 2023 (ReCode); Yang et al. 2023 (rephrasing contamination)

AI-eval instantiation. On an execution-verified programming benchmark, four wings. Floor: run scripted zero-comprehension baselines (description-deleted prompt with signature+examples only; nearest-neighbor corpus paste; hard-coded example I/O) plus tiny and base-model checkpoints, many rollouts each; empirical floor = max baseline pass rate; in parallel, pass other tasks' canonical solutions through this task's tests to split guessable-spec from weak-tests. Cue: a probe model sees only the entry-point name and signature; predictability = fraction of k samples passing; a renamed-entry-point rerun separates name informativeness (invariant part) from memorization (the drop). Retrieval (computable today): z-combine Min-K% likelihood of the canonical solution under signature-only conditioning, verbatim completion of its second half, and n-gram overlap with public corpora; flag the top decile and publish all other measures with and without flags. Paraphrase: ~8 contract-preserving prose-only rewrites (examples and I/O verbatim, spot human-verified), resampled across models; flip index = pass-rate sd minus binomial noise floor; lure persistence = share of paraphrase failures matching the original modal wrong behavior; a uniform drop across all paraphrases flags memorization.

Other-domain instantiation. Professional licensure exams (bar, medical boards). Floor: administer items to a no-preparation cohort, or simulate keyword-matching and option-elimination test-takers, to estimate the guessing asymptote and catch distractors eliminable from format alone. Cue: show examinees only the item's topic heading and answer options; high accuracy means the stem is decorative. Compromise/memorization: compare cohorts with and without access to leaked prep-bank items, and flag verbatim reproduction of worked solutions or too-fast-correct response times (item-preknowledge detection). Paraphrase: field reworded variants to matched cohorts; a valid item keeps both its difficulty and its modal distractor; difficulty shifts or distractor dissolution mark wording-dependent or compromised items, which are excluded from ability scoring.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: By design it does not track difficulty; it tracks susceptibility of the measurement to artifact. Its variance is best explained by eval-apparatus properties: benchmark age/popularity (contamination), verifier engineering quality, and format conventions, none of which are task semantics. That is acceptable for a gate, but the composite framing invites misreading "low M17" as "hard".
  • Generality: Floor/guessing wing is fully general. Paraphrase wing breaks where surface form IS the task: formal-spec domains (Lean statements, regex/code-golf, translation) admit no meaning-preserving restatement, and embodied/interactive tasks (robotics, games) have no "phrasing" and a trivially zero guessing floor, making the screen vacuous there. Retrieval wing is corpus-relative, so it is undefined for human cohorts without an exposure model.
  • Confound severity: medium
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuinely high and unique: pooled pass rate cannot distinguish "easy because trivial" from "easy because leaked, guessable, or weakly verified", and no other catalog measure certifies that. But the weak-verifier wing duplicates M21 and the name/signature-cue wing substantially overlaps M16's prompt-channel analysis.
  • Gameability: The screen is itself gameable: paraphrase-augmented or deduplicated training defeats Min-K%, n-gram overlap, and verbatim-completion probes (Yang et al. 2023) while semantic memorization persists: an item can pass all four wings and still be contaminated. Saturated tasks (floor≈ceiling) render every wing uninformative; the "top decile" flag is arbitrary and relative, so a uniformly contaminated benchmark flags only 10%.
  • Cost: High: scripted baselines, base-model reruns, renamed variants, ~8 human-spot-checked paraphrases resampled across models; largely automatable for execution-verified code, expensive elsewhere (matched human cohorts for exams).
  • Kill test: Controlled contamination: plant known leaks and measure detection. Concretely, fine-tune a model on canonical solutions for a random half of a code benchmark, run the retrieval and paraphrase wings blind, and compute AUC for identifying planted items; corroborate with a time-split (pre- vs post-cutoff publication) drop check. Near-chance AUC kills the wings that justify the measure's gate role.
  • Best improvement: Report the four wings separately, never as one scalar; move the cross-solution/verifier-strength control into M21 and cite it; calibrate the retrieval wing via planted-contamination ROC before trusting flags; define the paraphrase equivalence class operationally (bidirectional entailment plus identical verifier behavior) and exclude formal-surface domains from that wing explicitly.

M18 · Adaptation & retention dynamics

M18 · Adaptation & retention dynamics: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Difficulty as the cost for a solver to become able to do the task, plus the fragility of that ability once acquired. Acquisition difficulty is the amount of adaptation (practice trials, gradient steps, worked examples) a solver from P needs under a fixed training protocol before its output first satisfies V, indexing the distance from the solver's current state to competence, not merely how often unadapted attempts succeed. Retention difficulty is how fast that ability decays under continued adaptation to unrelated material: a short forgetting half-life marks a mapping held as brittle rote memory rather than a consolidated rule.

  • Facet: Learnability, sample/compute complexity of acquisition (adaptation-space distance to competence), and consolidation: the memorization-vs-rule character of the acquired mapping, read from its decay under interference.
  • Relative to: Solver population P under a fixed adaptation protocol (optimizer, hyperparameters, practice schedule) and, for retention, a fixed interference stream. Only orderings are meaningful, and even those need not transfer across protocols, solver scales, or streams.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Acquisition: single-example adaptation largely measures memorization speed, which tracks answer length and the solver's prior likelihood of the reference answer (report partial correlations); strong learning-rate/rank/seed/optimizer dependence; joint-training variants are contaminated by transfer between near-duplicate tasks; the paraphrase generalization readout inherits paraphrase quality; the reference solution's style stands in for "the task". Retention: interference-stream composition dominates absolute half-lives (orderings shift if the stream resembles the tasks); entangled with acquisition strength (barely-learned tasks forget fastest almost tautologically; control by training all tasks to matched criterion/likelihood); binary pass/fail verdicts make half-life estimates noisy; forgetting magnitude is partly an optimizer/solver property, not a task property.
  • Related work: Swayamdipta et al. 2020 (Dataset Cartography); Paul et al. 2021 (GraNd/EL2N); Jiang et al. 2021 (C-score); Toneva et al. 2019 (forgetting events); Ethayarajh et al. 2022 (V-usable information); McCloskey & Cohen 1989 (catastrophic interference); Jagielski et al. 2023 (measuring forgetting); Ebbinghaus 1885 (retention curves)

AI-eval instantiation. Per task: LoRA-finetune a small model (e.g., 1.5B params, r=16, lr 1e-4) on the single (prompt, reference solution) pair; after each step greedy-decode and run the automatic verifier. Acquisition difficulty = steps to first verified pass (cap ~100, several seeds); a second readout (steps until a held-out paraphrased prompt also passes) separates string memorization from acquiring the mapping. Cheap population variant: one joint run over all tasks for ~5 epochs, recording per-task first-pass epoch plus Dataset-Cartography confidence/variability (~1/500 the compute). Frozen proxy computable now: EL2N/GraNd gradient norm of the reference solution, one backward pass per task. Retention: from each at-criterion checkpoint (tasks trained to matched NLL), continue training on a fixed interference stream disjoint from the eval (~10k steps of generic corpus), re-verifying every 500 steps; half-life = steps to first sustained pass-to-fail flip (smoother: reference NLL rising 1 nat). Zero-extra-compute variant: Toneva-style forgetting-event counts within the joint run.

Other-domain instantiation. Spaced-repetition vocabulary study or clinical-skills training. Acquisition: trials until a learner first meets criterion (correct recall; certified procedure performance). Retention: the learner keeps studying the rest of the syllabus and the item is retested at spaced intervals; difficulty = negative half-life of the recall/savings curve, e.g., per-card lapse counts and interval growth under an SRS scheduler, or months until CPR performance falls below certification standard. Fast-learned/fast-forgotten items are rote; slow-learned/slow-forgotten items are structurally hard but consolidable.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Acquisition arm mostly tracks optimizer distance to reproducing one reference string: strongest alternative explanation of its variance is base-model NLL of the reference answer plus answer length and lr/seed noise, i.e., M15 re-measured through SGD. Retention variance is best explained by interference-stream similarity and acquisition strength (barely-learned forgets first, near-tautologically), not task structure. Only the paraphrase-generalization readout plausibly tracks mapping difficulty rather than string memorization.
  • Generality: Strong in principle: trials-to-criterion and forgetting curves exist for any trainable solver (SRS vocabulary, CPR recertification). Breaks for frozen/non-adaptable solvers and for one-shot instance difficulty: training on the single (prompt, answer) pair leaks the label, so it measures skill-class learnability, not instance difficulty. Concretely breaks for open-ended creative tasks with no reference solution (write a novel plot, run a negotiation), where steps-to-first-verified-pass is undefined.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Two things pass rate misses: (1) learnability: a 0%-zero-shot task may be one gradient step from solvable versus unlearnable within budget; (2) consolidation: rote vs. rule character of the acquired ability. No other catalog measure touches decay-under-interference; M12 is observational pretraining acquisition, not interventional adaptation cost. Honest caveat: the acquisition arm may add little beyond M15/M13 once NLL and length are partialed out.
  • Gameability: Contaminated/memorized tasks pass at step ~0 and score trivially easy; saturated tasks hit the floor; defective verifiers pass instantly. Padding or restyling the reference inflates apparent difficulty, short high-prior answers deflate it. Learning-rate, optimizer, and interference-stream choices reorder tasks; the step cap censors the hard tail, and matched-criterion control is impossible for never-passing tasks.
  • Cost: High: per-task finetune with repeated decode/verify, multi-seed; retention adds ~10k interference steps per task. Joint-run variant ~1/500 compute but transfer-contaminated; EL2N proxy is one backward pass but nearly reduces to gradient-norm/NLL.
  • Kill test: Generic: show acquisition adds nothing beyond codelength; regress steps-to-first-pass on base-model reference NLL + token length and test whether residuals still predict external difficulty. Concrete: 200 tasks, 1.5B LoRA; if NLL+length give R^2>0.8 and residual Spearman vs. METR human solve time <0.15, the arm is repackaged M15. Retention: recompute half-lives under two dissimilar interference streams; Spearman<0.5 means half-life is a stream property, not a task property.
  • Best improvement: Make paraphrase/held-out-instance pass the primary acquisition readout (>=3 variants per task, so mapping not string); residualize on base NLL and answer length by design; for retention, train all tasks to matched reference NLL first and report only orderings stable across >=2 dissimilar interference streams; handle capped runs with censoring-aware survival estimates.

M19 · Solution/error-space multiplicity

M19 · Solution/error-space multiplicity: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Partition a population of attempts at s into behaviorally distinct equivalence classes: distinct viable strategies among successes, distinct error modes among failures (classes induced by V's granularity), and measure the effective number and concentration of those classes via exponentiated Shannon entropy / Hill numbers, modal-class share, HHI, or the concentration of failure attribution on specific "killing" checks of V. Failure multiplicity near 1 means one attractor (a lure, misconception, or single critical boundary case); large means diffuse search failure; a narrow success-side funnel means success requires landing on one specific strategy. This characterizes the shape of a task's difficulty at fixed success rate, not its level.

  • Facet: Geometry of the induced solution-and-error landscape: single-attractor "tricky" vs many-basin "hard to find"; width of the viable-strategy funnel; localization of failure in V's check space. Separates tasks with identical pass rates.
  • Relative to: Primarily the solver population P and protocol R (sampling stochasticity/temperature); the equivalence partition itself is verifier-relative (only as fine as V's checks). Class identities may partially generalize across populations; their masses do not.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Sample size couples to success rate on both sides: near-ceiling tasks have too few failures and near-floor tasks too few successes, so estimates are censored or biased exactly where the measure is most wanted (requires bias-corrected estimators, CIs, masking). Class granularity is a free parameter: clustering thresholds, comparator strictness, and V's check fineness change counts; near-duplicate checks fake concentration without behavioral dedup, coarse suites merge distinct errors, surface/style diversity masquerades as strategy diversity. Rises mechanically with sampling temperature. Conflates task ambiguity with solver incompetence (a weak P is diffuse on a crisp task). Crash/global failures fail every check and mask structure; fail-fast recording confounds error identity with check order; nondeterministic solutions need seed pinning. Contamination/memorization concentrates mass, faking narrowness or draining the natural lure class. Direction can invert: trivially easy tasks with one canonical idiom also look narrow.
  • Related work: Behavioral clustering: Li et al. 2022 (AlphaCode), Chen et al. 2022 (CodeT); semantic entropy: Kuhn et al. 2023, Farquhar et al. 2024; error structure: Geirhos et al. 2020 (error consistency), Tambon et al. 2024; psychometric distractor/nominal-response analysis: Bock 1972, Lord 1980 (IRT discrimination); test attribution: DeMillo et al. 1978 (killing mutants), Rothermel et al. 2001, Liu et al. 2023; estimation: Miller 1955, Chao 1984; diversity: Zhu et al. 2018 (self-BLEU), Pawlik & Augsten 2016 (APTED)

AI-eval instantiation. Sample ~128 attempts per task per model at fixed temperature; execute against the full test suite, verifying the harness records ALL failing tests (if fail-fast, one re-execution pass recovers the matrix). Behavioral signature = per-test outcome vector (finer: hash of outputs on test inputs); crashes binned by exception type or held out. Report: (a) effective behavior count exp(H) over all attempts with Miller-Madow/Chao-Shen correction and bootstrap CIs, masking tasks with <10 in the conditioning set; (b) failure-conditional effective error count and modal failing-class share (lure mass); (c) top-killer share, HHI, and minimal kill cover (greedy set cover to >=95% of failures) over behaviorally deduped tests; (d) success-side strategy richness from normalized-AST clustering on a fixed-n subsample of passers (n=20; censor tasks below). Validate against human "tricky" vs "underspecified" labels; compare per-model vs pooled to distinguish coherent traps from idiosyncratic errors.

Other-domain instantiation. Free-response math exam graded across a large student cohort. Failure side: cluster wrong submissions by final answer value or a misconception taxonomy; effective number of distinct wrong answers = exp(entropy); one dominant wrong answer marks a classic lure/misconception (distractor analysis), diffuse spread marks general confusion. Killing-check concentration: which single rubric item or sub-question accounts for most failed attempts (one boundary case vs broad inadequacy). Success side: count distinct valid solution methods among correct scripts (e.g., algebraic vs geometric vs induction), subsampling a fixed number of correct scripts per problem to decouple from pass rate.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: By its own admission it measures difficulty's shape at fixed level, not level, so it is a typology, not an ordering. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: solver-output entropy quantized through verifier resolution (temperature, population weakness/diversity, test-suite granularity), i.e. a solver-x-verifier property wearing a task costume. It is also non-monotone in difficulty: trivially easy canonical-idiom tasks and genuine lure traps both look "narrow."
  • Generality: Works wherever attempts are cheap and V exposes sub-checks or discrete answers (code test vectors, math final answers, MCQ distractors; the psychometric lineage is real). Breaks in one-shot or expensive-attempt domains (surgery, long agentic deployments: no attempt population) and in holistically graded open-ended work (essays, design), where behavioral equivalence classes are arbitrary and there is no check space to localize failure.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: at identical pass rate it separates single-attractor "tricky" (one lure/killing check) from diffuse "hard to find," plus success-side funnel width, which predicts the effective intervention (targeted hint/spec fix vs more search/capability). Success rate cannot see any of this. But it adds a dimension, not a magnitude.
  • Gameability: Saturated tasks: failure side censored; one canonical idiom among successes fakes an insight-funnel. Defective tasks: a single flaky or wrong test manufactures a killing-check "lure" signature; coarse suites merge distinct errors into fake concentration. Memorized tasks: contamination collapses successes to one class and drains the natural lure class, inverting the reading. Inflatable via temperature; deflatable via output canonicalization or near-duplicate checks absent behavioral dedup.
  • Cost: Moderate: ~128 samples/task/model, full failure-matrix execution (one re-run pass if fail-fast), clustering/AST normalization, bootstrap CIs. No training runs; hours-to-days of inference+execution per benchmark.
  • Kill test: Variance-decompose the measure across solvers and nuisance knobs; if between-task variance is not clearly dominant, it is a solver/verifier property, not a task property. Concrete: failure-side effective error count on the extended test suite tasks in the 20-80% pass band, 5 model families x 2 temperatures x 2 signature granularities; kill if task ICC < 0.5 or cross-knob task-rank Kendall tau < 0.6.
  • Best improvement: Report the task-attributable share (ICC) as the headline; fixed-n subsample both success AND failure sides within matched pass-rate bands; pin granularity via behaviorally deduped checks reported at two canonical levels; validate concentration causally by targeted-hint lift on the modal error class; absorb M20 as this measure's concentrated-failure pole to remove catalog redundancy.

M20 · Lure strength (prior–task misalignment)

M20 · Lure strength (prior–task misalignment): definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The strength of a task's dominant wrong attractor: a candidate solution that is highly probable under solvers' priors (the conventional or naive reading of specification s) yet rejected by verifier V. Ex ante it shows as misdirection features in s and as divergence between s's plain reading and what V actually demands; ex post as the mass of the modal wrong-behavior class among sampled attempts, that class's consensus across independent solvers, and the brevity of the misconception producing it. High values mark tasks that are tricky (one strong trap) rather than merely hard (diffuse failure).

  • Facet: Robustness to attractive-but-wrong solution paths (prior–specification misalignment): difficulty arising from one strong trap rather than from depth or breadth of required work; predicts anti-scaling solvers and time-pressure sensitivity.
  • Relative to: Solver-population-relative at core: a lure exists only for solvers whose prior instantiates it. Cross-population error consensus is the dial that upgrades it toward a task-anchored property; misconception description length is quasi-solver-independent given the error, though P's modal failure selects which error is measured. Also verifier-relative: V is what rules the attractor wrong.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Mechanically bounded by failure rate (lure mass ≤ 1 − pass rate), so raw values track plain hardness; conditioning on failure decouples them but is unstable when failures are few. A dominant "wrong" class may be a defensible reading of an underspecified s: underspecification, not lure. Same-family solvers share priors and training data, inflating consensus; a circulating wrong solution (contamination) can manufacture both a lure and its consensus, while contamination on famous tasks can suppress them. Static symptom counts fire on innocuous phrasing and are blind to lures living in V rather than in s's text. Naive-reading probes confound probe incompetence with divergence, and example-stripping is spec surgery. Misconception description length is relative to a non-canonical description language, and minimal surface diffs overstate semantic minimality. Lure mass is the order-∞ Hill statistic of the wrong-behavior distribution, so it overlaps failure-mode entropy; exact misconception MDL remains a thought experiment, and human/cross-family arms need new data.
  • Related work: Haladyna & Downing 1989, 1993; Haladyna & Rodriguez 2013 (distractor analysis); Frederick 2005 (CRT); Hagendorff et al. 2023; McKenzie et al. 2023 (inverse scaling, resisting priors); Ettinger 2020 (negation); Geirhos et al. 2020; Mania et al. 2019; Baek et al. 2022 (cross-model error consistency); Li et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2023 (behavior clustering, mode mass); Rissanen 1978 (MDL); DeMillo et al. 1978; Zeller & Hildebrandt 2002; Winston 1970 (minimal faults, near-misses); Pu et al. 2020 (pragmatic spec reading); Miceli Barone et al. 2023.

AI-eval instantiation. Sample n≈100–400 attempts per task from each model at fixed temperature; execute against the verifier's full input set; cluster ALL attempts by behavioral signature (per-input output vector). Report lure mass = largest wrong class over all attempts, its conditional share among failures, sharpness (top:second wrong-class mass ratio), and witness inputs where the lure diverges from canonical. Consensus = 1 − generalized JSD between per-model wrong-class distributions (flag same-top-class in all models; strengthen with out-of-family models). Ex ante: a static+LLM checklist of misdirection features in s (negations, boundary/index/polarity switches, unconventional output rules; 3 seeds, per-item majority), and an example-stripped probe (an out-of-family model writes k=10 solutions, divergence = failure excess over its full-spec control). Crispness: an LLM states the misconception as a one-sentence rule, validated by applying the rule to a correct solution and checking it reproduces the lure class; rule length = proxy MDL.

Other-domain instantiation. Standardized-exam item analysis, CRT-style: tabulate thousands of examinees' free responses per question; lure strength = share of all examinees giving the modal wrong answer (bat-and-ball's "10 cents"), sharpness = its lead over the runner-up wrong answer; consensus = whether the same modal error tops cohorts with different training (countries, majors); crispness = shortest rule turning the correct procedure into the modal error ("ignore 'more than'"); item writers pre-audit stems for negation, inverted polarity, and boundary wording; a timed vs untimed arm separates lure-under-pressure from lure-always.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Tracks "trickiness" (one strong wrong attractor), a real facet distinct from depth/breadth of work. But raw lure mass is mechanically ≤ 1 − pass rate, so much of its variance is plain hardness; the conditional-on-failure version decouples but is noisy when failures are few. Strongest alternative explanation of remaining variance: shared training priors and contamination among same-family solvers (consensus manufactured by common data, not by the task), plus spec underspecification (a "defensible reading" scored wrong is M26's ambiguity, not a lure).
  • Generality: Good where a conventional reading and a behavioral input set exist: exam distractors, CRT, code, math word problems. Breaks in open-ended generative/agentic domains (design, research, long-horizon trajectories) where failure is inherently diffuse, behavioral clustering is ill-defined, and "the naive reading" has no referent; also breaks for solver populations with heterogeneous priors (no shared attractor).
  • Confound severity: medium
  • Added value over plain success rate: Distinguishes two tasks at identical pass rate: one modal trap vs diffuse failure. Predicts anti-scaling, time-pressure sensitivity, and which hint fixes the task, none visible in success rate. Genuine, but only for the trapped subset of tasks.
  • Gameability: Authors inflate it with cheap negation/polarity tricks (classic flawed-item writing): trickiness without substance. Saturated tasks: no failures, lure mass ~0, conditional share undefined. Defective tasks: ambiguity masquerades as a strong lure. Memorized tasks cut both ways: a circulating wrong solution manufactures lure + consensus; famous lures (bat-and-ball) get trained away, so scores decay nonstationarily. Clustering granularity and set choice can be tuned to merge/split the modal class.
  • Cost: Moderate-high: 100–400 executions per task per model plus clustering; ex-ante checklist cheap; cross-family/human consensus arms need new data.
  • Kill test: Generic: show lure strength predicts its signature outcomes after controlling failure rate and failure-mode entropy (M19); if the partial effect vanishes, it is redundant top-1 statistics. Concrete: assemble tasks matched at ~50% pass, split high-lure vs diffuse-failure, run a scale ladder; high-lure tasks must show flat/inverse scaling and larger deliberation/hint gains; diffuse tasks monotone improvement. No separation kills it.
  • Best improvement: Report only the conditional-on-failure lure share with a minimum-failures gate (e.g., ≥30); require one out-of-family or human arm before claiming task-anchored lure; add adjudication routing ambiguous modal classes to M26 and contamination screening via M17; validate the ex-ante symptom checklist against realized lure mass and prune non-predictive items. Note overlap: lure mass is M19's distribution's max statistic, and its scaling signature is M10; keep M20 for the ex-ante symptoms, consensus dial, and misconception-crispness pieces those lack.

M21 · Verifier-strictness / hidden-edge-case share

M21 · Verifier-strictness / hidden-edge-case share: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Place the verifier in an ordered family V_λ of increasing strictness (tolerance, rubric threshold, breadth of checked requirements) and measure how much of a task's difficulty the acceptance criterion itself contributes. The core statistic is the deceptive-pass rate: among attempts accepted at a lenient tier, the probability of rejection at a stricter tier, P(fail V_strict | pass V_lenient); and, generally, the elasticity of the difficulty profile D(s,λ) in λ together with the verifier share D(λ_strict) − D(λ_lenient). High values mean the hard part is not producing a credible solution but surviving unstated corners: apparent success is deceptive.

  • Facet: Verifier-relativity made quantitative: splits (s,V) difficulty into finding any reasonable solution vs. target narrowness imposed by the acceptance criterion, the density of hidden requirements along solvers' plausible-solution manifold, a "looks right, is wrong" component invisible to a single binary pass.
  • Relative to: Primarily the verifier family; the conditional deceptive-pass form is also solver-population-relative, since P supplies the near-miss distribution that probes the strict tier. A solver-free variant (acceptance-set narrowness −log μ(A_λ) under a reference measure μ) trades population-dependence for μ-dependence.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Substantially measures the strict tier's author-thoroughness and oracle artifacts (exact-equality conventions, flaky or noisy checks) rather than task content; lenient-tier strength varies by task, so the raw gap also reflects the weak verifier's gaps; adequacy/mutation normalization mitigates this but adds operator choices. Conditioning on lenient-pass couples the value to solver competence: tiny or undefined denominators for weak populations, collapsed thresholds when tiers nearly coincide. The strictness axis is itself a modeling choice (different λ-parameterizations give different elasticities), quality need not be monotone in λ, and verifier noise shifts the same curve while being conceptually distinct from strictness. Values near 1 across all solvers usually flag specification defects or arbitrary pinned conventions, not difficulty; the extreme doubles as a validity screen.
  • Related work: Liu et al. 2023 (base-vs-extended-tests drop, per-task-ified); DeMillo, Lipton & Sayward 1978 (mutation testing / test adequacy); Inozemtseva & Holmes 2014 (coverage vs suite effectiveness); Samejima 1969 (graded response model); Masters 1982 (partial credit); Strathern 1997 (Goodhart); Krakovna et al. 2020 (specification gaming); Zheng et al. 2023 (LLM-judge leniency); Jain et al. 2024 (test coverage)

AI-eval instantiation. Score one pool of sampled attempts per task under a nested verifier ladder: visible tests → extended edge-case tests → mutation-hardened tests, or an LLM-judge rubric swept across thresholds 1..10. Report per task: NM = #(pass lenient ∧ fail strict)/#(pass lenient) with Jeffreys intervals (NA, not 0, when nothing passes lenient); the pass-rate-vs-λ curve and its elasticity at the official operating point; optionally graded-response IRT thresholds on ordinal 0/1/2 scores (fail / lenient-only / full) so Δ = b_strict − b_lenient lands on the common ability scale. Screen NM ≈ 1 across models as suspected spec defects; mid-range NM is the genuine hidden-gotcha zone. the extended test suite-style base/plus pass statuses give the two-point version of the curve directly from stored rollouts.

Other-domain instantiation. Grade olympiad proofs twice, "key idea present" (lenient) vs "full rigor" (strict): among scripts earning key-idea credit, the share denied full marks isolates the problem's rigor load as distinct from its idea load. Same recipe elsewhere: figure-skating routines scored under completion-only vs execution-perfect deduction regimes, or clinical trials re-scored from a "response" to a "cure" endpoint: sweeping strictness re-ranks which routines, problems, or trials count as hard.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6.5/10)

  • Validity: Tracks a real facet (how much of difficulty lives in the acceptance criterion rather than in producing a credible attempt), but only for the (task, verifier-family) pair, never the task alone. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: strict-tier authorship thoroughness plus oracle artifacts (exact-equality conventions, flaky checks) and the solver population's near-miss distribution supplying the probe. the extended test suite-style base-to-plus drops are known to mix genuine semantic corners with defective or arbitrary tests; the λ-axis itself is an operator choice, so 'elasticity' is parameterization-dependent.
  • Generality: Applies wherever a nested strictness ladder exists (code tests, rubric thresholds, proof rigor grades, clinical endpoints). Breaks where verification is atomic/crisp: formal proof checking (Lean: a proof checks or it doesn't; any lenient tier is artificial), win/loss games, single-token factual QA. Also degenerates for weak populations (empty lenient-pass denominator → NA).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: two tasks with identical strict pass rate can differ entirely in whether failures are near-misses dying on unstated corners versus wholesale failures. Plain success rate cannot separate idea load from rigor load, predict Goodhart/reward-hacking exposure, or flag spec defects (NM≈1 screen). This is the one measure that makes verifier-relativity quantitative.
  • Gameability: Benchmark authors inflate NM cheaply by pinning arbitrary conventions (whitespace, tie-breaking), manufacturing fake 'depth'. Solvers trained on the strict suite (memorized edge cases) drive NM→0, hiding genuine gotcha load; conversely visible-test overfitters inflate NM, conflating solver gaming propensity with task property. Saturated tasks give NM=0 (indistinguishable from 'shallow'); unsolved tasks give NA; flaky nondeterministic oracles mimic mid-range 'hidden requirements'.
  • Cost: Low marginal compute (rescoring stored rollouts); moderate one-time cost authoring strict tiers where none exist, plus human audit to split genuine corners from defects.
  • Kill test: Independent-verifier reconstruction: have two independent teams/procedures build the strict tier for the same tasks and rescore identical frozen rollouts; if per-task NM barely correlates and task rankings reshuffle, the measure is tracking test authorship, not task content. Concretely: wave 1 rollouts rescored under the extended tests vs an independently mutation-hardened suite; Spearman of per-task NM below ~0.4 kills it.
  • Best improvement: (1) Calibrate strictness in adequacy units (mutation score), not author whim; (2) require two independent strict-tier constructions and publish cross-tier reliability with NM; (3) prefer the ordinal graded-response Δ=b_strict−b_lenient over conditional NM to fix empty denominators and population coupling; (4) report an audited split of NM into semantic-corner vs pinned-convention/flaky-oracle mass. Distinct from M23 (error deceptiveness to reviewers) and M24 (static requirement count); keep separate as their behavioral, verifier-side complement.

M22 · Near-miss repair distance

M22 · Near-miss repair distance: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The expected repair effort separating a typical failed attempt from an accepted one, read in two coordinates: a behavioral gap (the fraction of the verifier V's distinct checks the failed attempt still fails) and an artifact gap (the minimum normalized edit distance from the failed attempt to the nearest artifact V accepts). Low values mean failures from population P are near-misses: solvers essentially reach the solution and slip on a sliver; high values mean failures are wholesale wrong, so the difficulty is strategic rather than executional. The (behavioral, artifact) pair is the measure; dissociations are signal: a tiny edit that flips many checks marks a knife-edge task.

  • Facet: Basin geometry around the solution set / depth of typical error: last-mile precision vs. global misunderstanding. Proxies the expected repair cost still owed after a failure, separating slip-difficulty (near-misses) from strategy-difficulty (wrong-track failures), a distinction pooled pass rate cannot see.
  • Relative to: Solver population P twice over: its failure modes determine where attempts land, and its own passing artifacts anchor the nearest-neighbor distances (style-relative); and verifier V, whose check set and input distribution define the behavioral arm. Not solver-independent.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Edit distance is not semantic effort: the one-token fix that IS the whole insight scores as trivial repair. Distance to sampled passers upper-bounds distance to the true solution set, and sparse passing pools at the hard tail bias distances upward, coupling the measure to pass rate; nearest-neighbor within one population's passes further underestimates distance under mode collapse. Edit distance conflates stylistic diversity with semantic distance (canonical-only anchors on zero-pass tasks inflate via style gap); residual size/length correlation survives normalization; degenerate or boilerplate failures compress distances. The behavioral arm inherits the verifier's check redundancy and amplified input distribution (dedup mitigates, not eliminates); crash-everything attempts fail all checks and must be reported separately. Tasks with no failures need a convention (score 0).
  • Related work: Automated program repair and patch distance (Le Goues et al. 2012; Monperrus 2018; Le Goues et al. 2019 survey); student-code repair (Clara: Gulwani, Radicek & Zuleger 2018; Refactory: Hu et al. 2019); APTED tree edit distance (Pawlik & Augsten 2016); competent-programmer hypothesis (DeMillo, Lipton & Sayward 1978); delta debugging (Zeller & Hildebrandt 2002).

AI-eval instantiation. Code-generation eval with unit-test verifier: sample N attempts per task from the eval's model population at fixed temperature; keep parseable, non-truncated failures. Behavioral arm: per failing attempt, fraction of deduplicated test behaviors failed; report the task median plus one-behavior-away mass (fraction of failures failing exactly one deduped behavior). Artifact arm: normalized tree edit distance (APTED on ASTs with identifiers/literals canonicalized; token Levenshtein fallback) to the nearest member of {pooled passing attempts + reference solution}, normalized by target size; task score = median over failures. Conventions: no failures → 0; no passes → canonical-only anchor, flagged. Validate: small distance + low pass rate should recover tasks human auditors tagged "tricky"/subtle-gotcha.

Other-domain instantiation. Proof-based exam grading: for each failing script on a problem, rubric arm = fraction of rubric checkpoints not earned (median over failing students, plus mass failing exactly one checkpoint); revision arm = number of lines or steps a grader must rewrite to turn the script into a full-credit proof (distance to the nearest full-credit script, model solution as fallback). Problems where most failures miss one checkpoint via a small rewrite are slip-hard (precision, edge cases); problems with far misses on both arms are concept-hard, informing whether remediation should drill care or reteach the idea.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Tracks failure GEOMETRY, not difficulty magnitude. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: (a) mechanical coupling to pass rate: sparse passing pools inflate nearest-neighbor distances, so the artifact arm partly re-measures M01; (b) population style diversity and output length, since edit distance conflates syntactic spread with semantic distance. Worst inversion: the one-token fix that IS the insight (off-by-one, wrong invariant) scores as near-miss, so some of the deepest tasks read as slip-easy. The behavioral arm largely measures verifier check redundancy/granularity (M21's territory).
  • Generality: Requires an editable artifact plus a decomposable verifier. Works for code, proofs, structured writing. Breaks where there is no artifact to diff: interactive/embodied tasks (negotiation, robotics, game play) and degenerately for MCQ/short-answer, where every wrong answer is one edit from correct; the measure collapses. Also undefined at both extremes (no failures / no passes) exactly where difficulty questions matter most.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real but narrow: pooled pass rate cannot distinguish near-miss (executional) from wholesale-wrong (strategic) failure, and the knife-edge dissociation (tiny edit flips many checks) is a genuinely novel flag. It is a difficulty TYPOLOGY, not a magnitude: it tells you what kind of hard, not how hard.
  • Gameability: Saturated tasks score 0 by convention (uninformative). Zero-pass tasks fall back to canonical anchors and inflate via style gap. Memorized tasks: failures are truncations/degenerate boilerplate, compressing distances and faking near-miss. Padding redundant verifier checks deflates the behavioral arm; boilerplate scaffolds shared by pass and fail attempts fake artifact closeness. Dedup of checks is judgment-laden and contestable.
  • Cost: Low-moderate: reuses existing eval attempts; APTED nearest-neighbor is O(fails x passes) tree-edit ops per task. No humans needed, but needs both passes and failures in sample.
  • Kill test: Check whether measured distance predicts realized repair cost. Generic: take failed attempts stratified by M22, grant a bounded repair phase (solver + its failure + verifier feedback), measure rounds/success-to-green; if uncorrelated after partialling out pass rate and length, the measure is exposed. Concrete: on wave 1 tasks, give GPT/Claude k=3 repair rounds per own failure; if 'near-miss' subtle-insight bugs convert no faster than 'far' failures, kill the artifact arm.
  • Best improvement: Validate against realized repair: replace static edit distance as headline with conversion rate/rounds under bounded feedback repair (aligning with M34's dynamic notion), keeping static distance as cheap proxy only where calibrated. Always report conditional on pass-rate stratum to break mechanical coupling; use behavior-space (test-signature) distance alongside AST distance; exclude and separately report degenerate/crash/truncated failures; spot-check edit distance against human fix-time.

M23 · Error deceptiveness & detection cost

M23 · Error deceptiveness & detection cost: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. How hard the task's characteristic errors are to detect, as distinct from how often they are made. Two layers: intrinsic loudness of failure, the share of wrong attempts that complete plausibly with a wrong answer (silent) rather than visibly breaking (crash, hang, flagrant constraint violation), and verifier-indexed deceptiveness, the excess rate at which a stated verifier, examining candidates under a restricted protocol (no oracle access, static inspection, a time budget), accepts wrong solutions over matched correct ones. High values mark tasks whose errors are cheap to make but expensive to catch.

  • Facet: Detection/verification cost of the task's characteristic errors: the verifier's side of the generation–verification gap. Separates "commonly produced" from "hard to catch"; targets oversight, review, and QA cost rather than production difficulty, and complements error-mass measures (a loud lure is not deceptive).
  • Relative to: Dual-indexed: to the solver population P whose realized errors furnish the stimuli, and to a verifier population V′ weaker than the oracle V (static readers, LLM judges, budget-capped experts) whose acceptance of those errors is the score; V grounds truth. The silent/loud layer is the most solver-independent but still inherits P's error mix and the probe distribution.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Loudness is partly a property of the probe distribution, not task logic: errors surface only under some inputs, and adversarial test suites differ from production use; environment noise (timeouts) contaminates the loud class, and heterogeneous check semantics (assert-style vs exception-expecting) blur the split. Deceptiveness is driven by the sampled candidate mix, not the task alone: adversarially selected near-misses inflate it (fix and report the mix); and style/length/comment cues leak correctness through imperfect control matching. Verifier side: judges who share training or schooling with the generators have correlated blindspots, inflating scores (partly the phenomenon itself; report per verifier family); acceptance bias/sycophancy and protocol/prompt sensitivity; verifier contamination by public tasks and reference solutions; reviewers inferring the stimulus base rate; and any time budget measures verification-under-budget, not verifiability in the limit.
  • Related work: Chillarege et al. 1992 (orthogonal defect classification); Liu et al. 2023; Tambon et al. 2024 (bug taxonomy of LLM-generated code); Zheng et al. 2023 (LLM-as-judge); McAleese et al. 2024 (CriticGPT); West et al. 2024 (generative–discriminative gap); Saunders et al. 2022 (self-critique); Bacchelli & Bird 2013 (limits of modern code review); Jia & Harman 2011 (mutation testing); Leike & Irving scalable-oversight verifier-gap lineage.

AI-eval instantiation. (1) Silent share: re-execute each failing attempt on the checks that kill it; classify outcomes as wrong-value (silent) vs exception/timeout/non-termination (loud); task score = silent fraction among failures, pooled and per model, with serial re-runs to strip machine-noise timeout artifacts. (2) Deceptiveness: for tasks with a high-mass modal wrong solution, present (spec, candidate) for static review to ≥3 LLM judges from families disjoint from the generators, interleaving the modal wrong attempt with a length/style-matched passing attempt as control; score = accept(wrong) − accept(correct) (or d′), reported per judge family to expose generator–verifier blindspot overlap. (3) Optional human arm (new data): experienced reviewers give time-boxed pass/fail-with-confidence verdicts; task score = 1 − accuracy; cross with human solve time to locate "quick to solve, unreliable to review" tasks where oversight workflows fail. Ex-ante variant: mutants of the reference solution stand in for realized errors.

Other-domain instantiation. Financial-statement audit: collect the misstatements preparers of a given filing type actually make, or seed them mutation-style. Loudness: run the mechanical checks (reconciliations, cross-footing, schema validation) and record which misstatements trip a check versus pass them all. Deceptiveness: qualified auditors perform time-boxed substantive review of misstated and clean statements matched for length and format; the filing type's detection-cost score is the acceptance-rate gap (misstated vs clean) together with the silent share among real misstatements.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Tracks verification difficulty, not production difficulty, a legitimate but different quantity (cost of knowing a task was solved). Strongest alternative explanation of variance: output-modality and harness artifacts. Silent share is largely determined by whether outputs execute (code can crash) or are inert values/prose (all failures silent by construction), plus assert-vs-exception check semantics; deceptiveness variance is driven by the candidate mix and judge-family blindspots rather than task logic. Absent modality conditioning, much of the score is format, not task.
  • Generality: Deceptiveness layer generalizes wherever a ground-truth verifier V exists (code, audit, math with checkable answers). Breaks in open-ended generation (essays, strategy advice, design): no oracle V grounds "wrong," and no crash class exists, so silent share saturates at ~1. Conversely saturates at ~0 for physical/motor tasks where errors are intrinsically loud. Discriminates mainly in the middle band of formal-artifact tasks.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: pass rate says nothing about oversight cost. Two tasks with equal failure rates can differ hugely in whether failures are catchable; this is the QA/delegation/scalable-oversight facet no outcome measure captures, and the safety-relevant one ("quick to solve, unreliable to review"). Honest limit: it adds almost nothing about how hard the task is to solve.
  • Gameability: Saturated tasks: strong solvers leave few wrong attempts, so stimuli shrink to noisy tiny-n exactly where it matters; ex-ante mutants quietly substitute a different construct. Memorized/contaminated judges detect deviations from a known reference trivially, deflating scores; defective tasks (wrong reference) inflate them. Adversarial near-miss selection inflates deceptiveness (acknowledged); paranoid-judge prompting deflates it; loudness is gamed by harness choice (adding asserts converts silent to loud). Judges can infer the ~50% stimulus base rate.
  • Cost: Low for silent share (re-execute logged failures). Moderate for LLM-judge deceptiveness (panel API calls per task). Expensive only for the optional human reviewer arm (new time-boxed expert data).
  • Kill test: Frame-swap rank stability: re-measure task deceptiveness with a disjoint solver population furnishing the errors and a different verifier family judging; if cross-instantiation Spearman over tasks is low (<0.4), it is a property of the (P,V') pair, not the task. Concrete: 100 wave 1 tasks, GPT-family errors judged by Claude/Gemini vs open-weight errors judged by humans; also regress silent share on output-modality dummies; R^2>0.8 kills the loudness layer.
  • Best improvement: Report only frame-fixed triples (error mix, probe set, verifier family) with cross-frame stability stats; report silent share within output-modality strata or as excess over modality baseline; neutralize surface cues by style-transferring wrong and correct candidates through the same rewriter; use d-prime with balanced, blinded base rates; control near-miss selection with matched-edit-size mutant pairs. Relabel as verification difficulty to prevent conflation with solving difficulty.

M24 · Specification & input-domain breadth

M24 · Specification & input-domain breadth: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The breadth of the correctness demand a task places on any solver: how many distinct atomic requirements a solution must jointly satisfy, how many semantically distinct behavior cases the verifier actually polices, and how large and structured the input domain is over which correct behavior is required. It layers three views of one quantity: constraints stated in s alone, cases graded by (s,V), and the structure of s's admissible-input space, all measuring how wide a correct solution must be, not how hard any single case is.

  • Facet: Conjunctive specification load and required coverage of the input domain: breadth of correct behavior, orthogonal to per-case depth.
  • Relative to: Solver-independent: a property of the specification s (stated constraints, domain structure) and of the (s,V) pair (which cases the verifier's grading surface actually distinguishes); measurement injects annotator- and instrument-dependence only at the margin.
  • Timing: ex_ante · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Specification verbosity and house style inflate constraint counts independent of difficulty, making task provenance a batch effect. All constraints are weighted equally, ignoring per-constraint hardness: a task hard via one subtle case, not many cases, scores low (breadth misses depth), and many stated constraints can be trivially co-satisfiable. Annotator segmentation granularity moves the scale. The behavior-case partition inherits the reference solution's idiom (a structure-free implementation collapses genuinely distinct cases) and the verifier's test-generation policy, so it partly measures the instrument rather than the task. Structurally rich inputs with shallow semantics score high. Explicit domain restrictions cut both ways: they shrink the graded domain (easier) while signaling that authors anticipated nasty inputs (harder task family).
  • Related work: IFEval verifiable-instruction counts (Zhou et al. 2023); FollowBench multi-constraint levels (Jiang et al. 2023); atomic-requirement counting in requirements engineering (Davis et al. 1993); category-partition testing (Ostrand & Balcer 1988); input-space partitioning (Ammann & Offutt 2008); the extended test suite test amplification (Liu et al. 2023); problem-space size (Newell & Simon 1972); Weyuker (1988) complexity-measure axioms.

AI-eval instantiation. On a coding benchmark with prompt, reference solution, and amplified test suite, compute three layers per task. (a) Constraint inventory: an LLM annotator from a non-solver family reads only the prompt and emits atomic requirements, each checkable by a single assert, conjunctions split, restatements merged; score = median count over 3 seeds, with inter-seed agreement as reliability and prompt length/readability partialed out as trivial baselines. (b) Graded-behavior breadth: execute the reference solution under a line/branch tracer on every test input; each input yields a coverage signature; score = number of distinct signatures plus signature-distribution entropy (downweighting amplified near-duplicates); enrich signatures with input-feature predicates (empty, singleton, negative, tolerance-boundary) and re-trace a second independently written implementation to de-idiom the partition. (c) Input-space structure: from test inputs compute type-nesting depth, arity, type heterogeneity, value-range descriptors, and domain-restricting contract-clause count; report the vector and its first principal component. Correlate each layer with pooled solver pass rates.

Other-domain instantiation. Estate-plan drafting from a client brief, verified by a senior partner's checklist review. (a) Count atomic obligations in the brief, each answerable by one yes/no review question (bequests, tax elections, formatting/jurisdiction formalities). (b) Count the distinct scenarios the reviewer actually walks through (predecease orders, simultaneous death, incapacity, divorce, after-born children) as scenario classes rather than checklist lines. (c) Score the estate's structural complexity: number of asset types, jurisdictions, beneficiary classes, and contingency layers. The three scores predict revision rounds and error findings across drafters of any skill level.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Tracks a real dimension (conjunctive failure surface: more independently-failable requirements → lower joint pass probability), but only when constraints are independently failable and non-trivial. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: specification verbosity and instrument granularity (prompt house style, annotator segmentation, and test-suite generation policy). Layer (b) largely measures the test suite, not the task. Equal weighting ignores depth: one-subtle-case tasks score low yet are hard; ten-trivial-formatting-constraint tasks score high yet are easy.
  • Generality: Good where specs enumerate obligations (coding, contracts, forms, compliance). Breaks on insight-dominated tasks with minimal specs, olympiad proofs ("prove X": one constraint, huge difficulty), and on holistic-quality tasks (write a moving essay) where atomic requirements don't exist. Layer (b) needs an executable reference + tracer; layer (c) needs structured inputs; both coding-specific in practice.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuinely ex-ante and solver-independent; still discriminates on saturated or floor benchmarks where pass rate is degenerate. Explains WHY a task fails (breadth vs depth), predicts partial-credit profiles and why pass@k plateaus (conjunctive misses aren't fixed by resampling), and supports difficulty-targeted task generation. Real added value, contingent on de-confounding.
  • Gameability: Trivially inflated: split constraints into micro-clauses or pad specs with co-satisfiable boilerplate ("lowercase, ≤100 chars, JSON"): breadth jumps, difficulty doesn't. Amplified near-duplicate tests inflate signature counts despite entropy downweighting. Memorized tasks keep high breadth while being easy via retrieval (mislabels them hard). Defective verifiers grading one case crater layer (b) regardless of true spec breadth. Annotator LLM steerable by phrasing.
  • Cost: Low-moderate: 3 annotation seeds + tracing per task (cents-dollars); the independently-written second implementation is the expensive, often-skipped part.
  • Kill test: Semantics-preserving spec perturbation: rewrite each prompt terse vs verbose and pad with trivial constraints; if breadth scores move substantially while pooled pass rates don't (and partial correlation with pass rate vanishes after controlling prompt tokens and test-suite size), it measures style, not difficulty. Concrete: run on wave 1 against the project's pooled pass-rate data with GPT-rewritten terse/verbose/padded prompt triples.
  • Best improvement: Weight constraints by estimated per-constraint failure probability instead of counting equally; canonicalize prompts (style-normalizing paraphrase) before annotation; report breadth jointly with a depth measure (M13/M21) rather than alone; cede layer (b)'s coverage-signature machinery to M19 (behavioral multiplicity), which it duplicates, keeping (a)+(c) as the distinctive spec-side core.

M25 · Knowledge/concept rarity

M25 · Knowledge/concept rarity: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The difficulty a task inherits from the scarcity of its prerequisite knowledge: how infrequently the concepts, methods, facts, or problem patterns needed to satisfy the specification occur in the exposure distribution of solver population P: the training corpus of machine solvers, or the collective record of problems a human population has already solved. It is scored by the rarest single requirement rather than the average, since one obscure prerequisite gates success. The measure is explicitly distribution-relative: the same specification is easy for a population that has seen the pattern often and hard for one that has not.

  • Facet: Knowledge rarity / long-tail prerequisite demand: the "has P encountered this before?" axis, orthogonal to reasoning depth, search breadth, or specification ambiguity.
  • Relative to: Solver-population-relative: defined against P's shared exposure distribution (training diet for machine solvers; the archive of already-solved problems for human crowds). Makes no solver-independent claim; V and R enter only through what the verifier forces the solution to actually use.
  • Timing: ex_ante · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: (1) Rarity of the reference solution's chosen approach ≠ rarity of the easiest viable approach: a rare-method canonical may admit a common-method alternative, so score the min over refactored alternatives. (2) Deliberately entangled with training-data contamination: corpus frequency measures exposure, and eval items themselves circulate in corpora, a feature when used as a contamination covariate, a bug when read as pure difficulty. (3) Archival popularity is demand-driven as well as difficulty-driven: crowd size, era, and topic hotness move both match counts and historical time-to-answer. (4) Retrieval/matching noise on synthetic or stylized task phrasing; the prompt-surprisal variant mostly tracks verbosity and formatting, not conceptual rarity. (5) Curated easy benchmarks rarely demand truly rare knowledge, so expect compressed dynamic range (floor effects); measure it, don't assume it.
  • Related work: Kandpal et al. 2023 (long-tail knowledge vs LM accuracy); Razeghi et al. 2022 (pretraining term frequency → performance); Zan et al. 2022 (unseen/private-library code generation); Mamykina et al. 2011 (Stack Overflow answer-latency dynamics); Liu et al. 2013 (question-difficulty estimation in CQA); Kuperman et al. 2012 (norm-based familiarity scales, the psycholinguistic analog).

AI-eval instantiation. For a code-generation eval: (a) an LLM tags each task (prompt + reference solution) against a fixed ~60-entry concept taxonomy (regex, DP, bit ops, date arithmetic, unicode, geometry, ...); (b) harvest called names from the reference solution's AST and count their frequencies in a public-corpus sample (e.g., 100k Python files from The Stack v2); task score = −log frequency of the rarest required call/concept, minimized over refactored alternative implementations. Complementary human-record component: embed prompt+solution, retrieve near-duplicate accepted-answer Stack Overflow questions, and report −log neighbor count above a similarity threshold plus median minutes-to-accepted-answer among neighbors; validate combination weights against human time-to-solve on a subsample. Computable-now fallback: LLM-rated 1–5 rarity per tag, median of 3 runs. Report the corpus-frequency component twice: as a difficulty measure and as the contamination-exposure covariate for pass-rate analyses.

Other-domain instantiation. Clinical diagnosis: estimate how rare a case's presentation is in the collective medical record. Embed the case vignette, retrieve near-duplicate published case reports (e.g., from PubMed), and score −log count of close matches plus the median diagnostic delay ("diagnostic odyssey" length) reported among matched cases. A presentation with few precedents and historically long times-to-correct-diagnosis is predicted hard for any clinician population trained on that literature, independent of individual skill.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Partly valid for machine solvers: pretraining frequency → accuracy is one of the few causally supported difficulty links (Kandpal, Razeghi), and it is honestly scoped as one axis. But it measures the source of failure, not magnitude, and the operationalization actually tracks the reference solution's stylistic rarity plus topic demand. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: corpus/archive counts are demand-driven popularity (what people write about) and taxonomy-granularity artifacts; the rarest-requirement min-statistic swings with how finely concepts are cut and with which alias string gets counted, more than with true prerequisite scarcity.
  • Generality: The exposure-distribution-relative principle generalizes to any population with an archive; practice requires a countable corpus, excluding tacit-knowledge domains (motor skill, negotiation). Breaks hardest where difficulty is composition, not knowledge: competition math and ARC-style puzzles built from ubiquitous primitives score floor-easy while being maximally hard; conversely nonce/synthetic tasks (random string ops) have zero corpus neighbors yet are trivial.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: an ex-ante decomposition of WHY tasks fail (exposure-gated vs reasoning-gated), invisible in pass rate; predicts which failures retrieval/RAG or in-context docs will fix; and doubles as the contamination-exposure covariate pass-rate analyses need. That prerequisite-level role is distinct from M17, which screens the item itself, and from M28's curriculum stage (its human shadow).
  • Gameability: Very gameable: private or nonce-renamed APIs make trivial tasks score hard (Zan et al.); obscure-style canonical solutions inflate the min unless the refactoring search is honest; retrieval-equipped solvers nullify it; polysemous string counts misfire; curated benchmarks floor out (compressed range, admitted); corpus snapshots go stale, so scores drift with era; memorized items score "common": fine as covariate, wrong as difficulty.
  • Cost: Low-moderate; no solve data needed. LLM tagging plus corpus counts are cheap once frequency infrastructure exists; min-over-refactorings and SO neighbor retrieval add moderate engineering. The cheap LLM-rated fallback collapses into judge appraisal (M29).
  • Kill test: Intervene on exposure holding the task fixed: inject the scored-rarest prerequisite in-context and check the pass-rate deficit collapses; persistent deficits expose mislabeled reasoning difficulty. Concrete: take code tasks scored rarity-hard via rare-API frequency, rerun with the API's docs/signature in the prompt against frequency-matched common-API controls; additionally nonce-rename a common API; if M25 scores it hard but solvers still pass, the measure is a string-frequency artifact.
  • Best improvement: Redefine interventionally: score = pass-rate lift from injecting the rare prerequisite (knowledge-only hint dose, sharing M32 machinery), killing demand-popularity and taxonomy confounds. Compute rarity over the easiest solver-found alternative, not the reference solution; co-report a reasoning-depth control and taxonomy-granularity sensitivity; report the contamination-covariate use separately from the difficulty reading.

M26 · Specification ambiguity

M26 · Specification ambiguity: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. How underdetermined the intended behavior and success criterion are given everything the specification s shows the solver: the number or entropy of behaviorally distinct reasonable interpretations that survive the shown information; equivalently, the expected disagreement among independent competent readers stating the intended outcome, or among draws of verifier V judging identical candidates. High values mean part of the task is discovering what counts as success, and that even competent work faces an acceptance lottery when V arbitrarily enforces one surviving reading.

  • Facet: Irreducible ambiguity / goal underspecification: spread of surviving interpretations of the target and acceptance criterion (posterior spread, not modal-reading bias); the ambiguity-vs-difficulty separator, distinct from solution-construction difficulty.
  • Relative to: Nominally a property of the spec-to-target map, hence solver-independent; measured against a population of competent readers (interpretation side) or independent draws of verifiers implementing V (criterion side). Protocol-relative in effect: lenient any-verifier-accepts aggregation can flip its sign, and clarification-permitting protocols can discharge it.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Enumerator/reader quality bounds it in both directions: missed readings undercount, invented unreasonable readings overcount, and any 'reasonableness' gate reintroduces judgment; careless, error-prone, or incompetent readers and judges make crisp specs look ambiguous (attention checks and competence screening needed, since diligence mixes into the entropy). Conflatable with default-interpretation divergence: that is bias of the modal reading, this is spread; verify the two decorrelate. Probe and candidate selection are load-bearing: probes mined from one model's disagreements import its idiosyncratic confusions, and borderline-heavy candidate mixes mechanically inflate verifier disagreement, so standardize the candidate distribution. Terse-spec regimes hit a ceiling where nearly everything is mildly ambiguous. Effect on difficulty depends on the acceptance rule (under lenient aggregation ambiguity can help), and ambiguity resolvable by querying the principal makes the measure harness-relative.
  • Related work: Version spaces (Mitchell 1982); AmbigQA (Min et al. 2020); AmbiEnt (Liu et al. 2023); the extended test suite mis-specification audit (Liu et al. 2023); ClarifyGPT (Mu et al. 2023); inherent disagreement in human labels (Pavlick & Kwiatkowski 2019); inter-rater reliability (Cohen 1960; Krippendorff 2004; Artstein & Poesio 2008); grant-review disagreement (Cole, Cole & Simon 1981); incomplete contracts (Grossman & Hart 1986; Hart & Moore 1990); Goodhart taxonomies (Manheim & Garrabrant 2018); METR 'unclear goals' messiness (Kwa et al. 2025).

AI-eval instantiation. Two coupled estimators on a programming benchmark. (a) Ex-ante interpretation count: a probe LLM, shown only the prompt and in-prompt examples (no hidden tests), enumerates up to 6 plausible readings as spec+implementation pairs; execute all on the union of test inputs, cluster by output vectors, drop clusters contradicting any shown example. Score = number of surviving clusters (1 = fully determined), plus a 'punished ambiguity' flag when ≥2 survivors disagree on a hidden test the grader then arbitrates arbitrarily. (b) Ex-post criterion side: K deliberately diversified frozen LLM judges (or human raters) grade M quality-spanning candidates against the rubric; D = 1 − Krippendorff's alpha on a standardized candidate mix; test whether pass@1 variance across judge draws tracks D. Gold check (needs new human data): ~8 competent readers write intended outputs for high-disagreement probe inputs from the prompt alone; mean normalized answer entropy should agree with (a)/(b), be elevated on underspecification-tagged items, and flag tasks to exclude from difficulty rankings rather than rank.

Other-domain instantiation. Judged sports and examinations: for figure skating, gymnastics, or essay exams, compute chance-corrected inter-judge agreement on identical performances per event or prompt type. The 2004 figure-skating scoring reform is a natural experiment: the criterion was sharpened, so the measured drop in judge disagreement quantifies how much apparent difficulty had been criterion ambiguity rather than performance demands.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Tracks spec-target underdetermination, not solution-construction demand; the measure itself concedes it is the "separator," and its sign on difficulty flips with the acceptance rule (lenient aggregation makes ambiguity help). Strongest alternative explanation of variance: rater/probe noise; reader carelessness, probe-LLM idiosyncrasy, and borderline-heavy candidate mixes generate disagreement indistinguishable from spec ambiguity; 1−alpha is spec ambiguity plus a panel-specific noise floor that varies by domain.
  • Generality: Applies wherever a spec mediates success (contracts, essays, judged sports, law). Breaks in formally specified domains (Lean theorem proving, SAT, chess puzzles) where ambiguity is ~0 for every item (no variance, no signal); also breaks under clarification-permitting protocols, where it is dischargeable and harness-relative, and hits a ceiling in terse-spec or taste-based domains where everything is mildly ambiguous.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real and specific: pass rate conflates an acceptance lottery with genuine solving demand; M26 isolates the lottery component and flags items whose "difficulty" is unfair grading (METR unclear-goals). Honest limit: it does not rank difficulty; it is a validity screen/decomposer, and its criterion-side estimator largely duplicates M05/M21 machinery.
  • Gameability: Benchmark owners collapse measured D by using same-family or agreement-tuned judges, or clearly-good/clearly-bad candidate mixes (alpha inflates, D≈0). Memorized tasks deflate it: a probe LLM that has seen the hidden tests "resolves" ambiguity fresh readers face. The 6-reading cap saturates on genuinely open specs; boilerplate pseudo-clarifications suppress enumerated readings without pinning behavior. Defective items with wrong gold labels masquerade as high-ambiguity.
  • Cost: Low-moderate: probe enumeration + execution and K×M judge gradings are cheap LLM calls; the load-bearing gold check (~8 competent human readers per flagged item) is expensive and is what actually validates it.
  • Kill test: Clarification ablation: append the one sentence that pins the intended reading; if high-M26 items' failure rates do not move toward matched low-M26 baselines (and judge D does not collapse), the measure was tracking rater noise, not goal-discovery difficulty. Concrete: on wave 1, add the grader's arbitration rule to flagged prompts and test whether competent-model pass@1 recovers and 1−alpha drops on the standardized candidate mix.
  • Best improvement: Report it in difficulty units via the clarification ablation (Δfailure when the intended reading is appended), which fixes the sign-flip and rater-noise problems; calibrate each judge/reader panel on known-crisp anchor items and subtract that noise floor; merge estimator (b) with M21/M05 protocols to avoid double counting, and verify decorrelation from M20 empirically.

M27 · Human effort horizon (time & price)

M27 · Human effort horizon (time & price): definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The typical resource expenditure (elapsed or active-work time, or its market-price equivalent) that competent solvers drawn from a reference human population P need to deliver a solution meeting verifier V's standard under protocol R. Per task it is summarized by the median (or geometric-mean) time over successful attempts, the fraction of solvers finishing within a fixed budget (a CDF point), or the lowest price at which the task reliably clears in an open market. A derived population-level index is the horizon: the effort level at which P's success rate crosses a threshold such as 50%.

  • Facet: Total serial labor / process cost: the human-effort price of a verified solution (search plus execution time); the price variant adds skill scarcity and a risk premium for ambiguity and verification burden. It is the canonical external anchor for claims like "machines now solve tasks humans need X minutes for."
  • Relative to: Solver-population-relative: indexed to a screened reference population P (its skill, tooling, incentives), with V's strictness and R's harness silently folded into what counts as complete; orderings transfer only approximately to other populations. The price variant is relative to a professional market at prevailing wages.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Reading, typing, and harness friction scale with spec/solution length, not conceptual hardness; labor volume and tedium conflate with cognitive difficulty (a 40-hour data-entry task outranks a 2-hour insight); solver skill variance, motivation, fatigue, and payment scheme shift the speed-accuracy operating point; familiarity with public task idioms (human contamination); right-censoring at the time cap truncates exactly the hardest tail, and failed or abandoned attempts bias medians downward unless survival-imputed; small n per task makes medians noisy; strict verifiers charge solvers for spec disambiguation, mixing in the underspecification facet; "competent" is whatever the screening gate passes, and crowd professionals are noisier than the label implies. Price variant: floor censoring below the market's minimum gig size; quotes bundle client hassle, platform fees, reputation risk, and market liquidity; ETA-based quotes double-count the time facet.
  • Related work: Kwa et al. 2025 (METR task-completion time horizons); Rein et al. 2025 (HCAST human baselines); van der Linden 2006/2007 (lognormal response-time IRT); Mozannar et al. 2024 (instrumented coding); Peitek et al. 2021 (comprehension timing); Taylor 1911 (time-and-motion study); Gulliksen 1950 (speed vs. power tests); Horton 2010 (online labor markets); Mason & Watts 2009 (incentives and crowd effort); Angoff 1971 (threshold setting).

AI-eval instantiation. Stratify ~60-100 tasks of a benchmark by model pass-rate decile; recruit ~10 screened humans per task (calibration-task gate) in an instrumented environment under the same interface and automatic verifier as the AI; log time from spec-reveal to first verified pass, with a 30-minute cap. Fit a lognormal AFT/Rasch speed model with solver-ability terms and bridge tasks; score = adjusted median time-to-pass T50 plus a budget CDF point; impute untimed tasks by regressing T50 on cheap ex-ante features, reporting uncertainty. Validate by fitting, per model, logistic pass rate (over sampled attempts) against log T50 and reporting the 50%-success time horizon. Optional price shadow: median of ~5 sealed-bid freelance quotes per task, exposing non-time difficulty components among tasks where models tie.

Other-domain instantiation. Surgery: order procedures by median operative time of board-certified surgeons from registry data (e.g. NSQIP duration per procedure code), with complication-free outcome as the verifier; validate that trainee failure rates rise monotonically with attending-median duration. Market form: order home-repair jobs by the median of sealed contractor bids, or alpine routes by median days-on-route for guided competent parties.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks difficulty, but its strongest variance driver is labor volume: reading, typing, and mechanical execution scale with spec/solution length, so a tedious 40-hour transcription outranks a 2-hour insight problem. Worse, median time over successful attempts conditions on success: tasks most humans fail outright can score easy because the few finishers are fast. The horizon variant (effort at 50% population success) is sounder but inherits screening-gate arbitrariness. Alternative explanation of most variance: solution token length plus protocol friction plus survivor selection.
  • Generality: Broad wherever humans can attempt the task, which is its selling point. Breaks where humans cannot do it at all (protein-structure prediction, superhuman Go positions: time is undefined/infinite), where elapsed time is environment-fixed (real-time driving, dialogue), and where humans are near-instant (image recognition: floor compression to seconds). Orderings transfer only approximately across populations.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: an interval-scale, externally meaningful unit (minutes, dollars) that separates tasks all sitting at 0% or 100% model pass rate, and anchors cross-time claims ("horizon doubles every N months"). Honest caveat: within one benchmark, log-time and pass rate correlate strongly, so per-task ranking info beyond M01/M06 is modest; the distinctive contribution is the unit, not the order.
  • Gameability: Builders inflate horizons with padded boilerplate tasks ("168-hour" tasks that are 168 hours of typing models shortcut). Under-incentivized baseliners inflate times; contaminated/familiar tasks deflate them; nobody re-baselines saturated tasks. Memorized tasks give models instant passes at any human-hours, corrupting horizon regressions. Defective verifiers make time measure harness debugging. Price variant gamed by lowball never-deliver bids and platform minimums.
  • Cost: Very high; needs new data. ~10 screened humans x 60-100 tasks x capped sessions plus screening: tens of thousands of dollars per benchmark (METR-scale baselining). Regression-imputed times reintroduce circularity.
  • Kill test: Hold conceptual content fixed, vary mechanical labor; if measured time scales with padding while failure-at-generous-budget stays flat, it measures volume not difficulty. Concretely: pad 20 coding tasks with 10x boilerplate all baseliners still finish; if T50 rises ~10x with human ultimate failure rate and model pass-at-large-budget unchanged (and log-T50 adds no predictive power over raw spec+solution token length in the pass-rate regression), the measure is length in disguise.
  • Best improvement: Report a 2D point (effort-given-success plus human ultimate success rate) via a cure-fraction AFT survival model handling censoring and abandonment; subtract a matched transcription/reading baseline to isolate think-time from mechanical floor; require bridge tasks across populations; price variant only from delivered-and-accepted contracts, never quotes.

M28 · Human skill/learning-axis location

M28 · Human skill/learning-axis location: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Locates a task on a calibrated competence axis of a human reference population P: the ability level, rating, curriculum stage, or cumulative training time at which a solver's probability of producing a verifier-passing solution under the fixed protocol first reaches a threshold (canonically 0.5). It asks not how often solvers fail but how much solver is required, read from fitted item-response curves, a rating system's solve curves, expert panels imagining a borderline-competent solver, or position in a learning progression. Discrimination (how sharply pass probability rises with skill) is kept as a companion coordinate, separating skill-gated tasks from tasks hard for everyone.

  • Facet: Skill-demand location: the task's position on the solver competence/acquisition axis (ability required, stage in the learning progression, training time to first acquisition), as distinct from failure frequency in any single cohort and from discrimination.
  • Relative to: Solver population: explicitly indexed to a defined, calibrated human reference population (its latent-ability continuum, rating pool, canonical curriculum, or a hypothesized borderline-competent member) with protocol R (time budget, aids) fixed as part of the scale's definition.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Censoring wherever the task set falls off the scale's calibrated support (floor for easy sets, ceiling for frontier ones) is endemic. Location and discrimination are jointly unstable at small n without pooling; cohort labels are coarse ability proxies; reference populations are self-selected; scales bundle protocol effects (time pressure, contest selection). Panel forms inherit the curse of expertise (judges demonstrably misestimate borderline performance), anchoring, and compression toward mid-scale. Curriculum and learning-time forms are pedagogy-relative (teaching order mechanically sets acquisition order) and underrate tasks that need no advanced concepts but hide traps; lure-driven items break the monotone-in-skill assumption behind every variant. Judge-model proxies add miscalibration and memorization of famous items (rating their fame, not their content); longitudinal forms add cohort selection/dropout, uncontrolled outside practice, and right-censoring at course end.
  • Related work: Lord 1980; Embretson & Reise 2000 (IRT); Elo 1978; Glickman 1999 (rating systems); Angoff 1971; Impara & Plake 1998; Cizek & Bunch 2007 (standard setting); ACM/IEEE CS2013; Parker et al. 2016; Thompson et al. 2008 (curriculum/assessment); Hendrycks et al. 2021 (APPS tiers); Li et al. 2022 (AlphaCode ratings); Jain et al. 2024; Rodriguez et al. 2021; Vania et al. 2021 (IRT for evals); Newell & Rosenbloom 1981; Bloom 1984; Kuperman et al. 2012 (acquisition norms); Kwa et al. 2025 (time horizons)

AI-eval instantiation. Gold: recruit cohorts spanning the skill axis (e.g., intro-course completers, mid-level and senior developers), ~8 attempts per cohort per task on a subsample, fixed 10-minute sittings, pass = the eval's automatic verifier; fit a Bayesian 2PL with cohort-informed priors and partial pooling; report location b (with posterior SD) plus discrimination a. Fitting the same IRT model to k sampled model rollouts yields a model-side b for direct human-vs-model item comparison; divergences flag items hard for different reasons. Proxies computable today: (i) LLM pairwise harder/easier judgments against ~30 anchor problems of known competitive rating, Bradley-Terry-aggregated into a rating-equivalent r*, with off-scale tasks reported as censored rather than extrapolated; (ii) median-of-7 judge ratings on a 5-stage curriculum rubric with worked anchors, instructor-validated on a subsample via weighted kappa; (iii) modified Angoff: 6-10 experts fix a borderline-hire profile, give two-round P(pass) estimates with empirical calibration anchors, rater SD kept as uncertainty.

Other-domain instantiation. Chess tactics: online-platform puzzle ratings (Glicko fitted to millions of logged solve attempts) assign each puzzle the player rating at which solve probability is 0.5, the rating-equivalent form, computable from existing logs; a coach panel Angoff-estimating a borderline club player's solve chance is the ex-ante form. Music exams: boards such as ABRSM place pieces on a grade 1-8 ladder by examiner panel, validated against grade-cohort pass rates; hours-of-practice to first clean performance under a fixed syllabus is the acquisition-time form; CEFR levels do the same for language tasks.

Critique (verdict: fold_into_another, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Tracks skill-demand location only where unidimensional, monotone IRT holds. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: exposure/teaching order and self-selection of the reference population; b moves with when a concept is taught and who joins the rating pool (concept rarity, M25), not with intrinsic demand. Lure-driven and multi-skill items break monotonicity, making fitted b meaningless; panel forms measure judged surface sophistication.
  • Generality: Strong where dense graded human populations exist (chess, music grades, school math, contests, CEFR). Breaks where no calibrated human axis exists: novel agentic/tool-orchestration and frontier research tasks are right-censored beyond the strongest cohort (exactly the AI-eval region of interest), and human-unnatural tasks (e.g., long-context verbatim recall) have no meaningful human skill axis at all.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: separates "how much solver is required" from "how often this cohort fails", invariant (in theory) to attempt-pool composition, unlike pass rate; the (b, a) pair distinguishes skill-gated from hard-for-everyone; gives interpretable human anchors ("1800 Elo", "grade 8") for human-AI comparison. Honest caveat: the Angoff/curriculum/LLM-proxy forms add little beyond M29-style appraisal.
  • Gameability: Ceiling/saturated sets yield censored or wildly extrapolated b; defective or impossible tasks produce the same off-scale signature as genius-level ones (needs M03 to disambiguate); judge-model proxies rate famous items by fame/memorization; trivial tasks dressed in advanced notation inflate panel and curriculum ratings; platform ratings drift with adaptive serving and pool selection.
  • Cost: Gold multi-cohort study: very high (thousands of human-hours, needs_new_data). Near-zero where legacy scales exist (chess logs, contest ratings). Panel Angoff moderate; LLM proxy cheap but weakest.
  • Kill test: Cross-population invariance: estimate location on two independently trained reference populations under identical protocol; if rank correlation falls well below its reliability ceiling, b encodes exposure history, not task demand. Concrete: ~100 stable-rated Codeforces problems; re-fit solve curves for contest-trained students vs ability-matched self-taught industry devs; Spearman < ~0.6 against test-retest reliability kills the task-property reading.
  • Best improvement: Fold into M06 as its human-population parameterization (rating/Angoff/curriculum as named instantiations); reassign instruction-hours to M27/M12/M18. Mandate reporting censoring fraction, item-fit/monotonicity diagnostics, and posterior SD with every b; require a cross-population DIF audit before calling b a task property; drop panel-only forms unless calibrated against empirical solve curves.

M29 · Appraised difficulty (judge & self)

M29 · Appraised difficulty (judge & self): definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Difficulty as estimated by a mind before any attempt: an appraiser inspects the specification s and outputs a predicted chance of failure under (P,R,V). The appraiser is either a third party (an ensemble of judges rating s against a rubric anchored to the target solver population) or the solver itself, via a stated pre-attempt forecast of its own failure probability, or the same forecast decoded from its internal state by a trained probe, whether or not it can verbalize it. The measure maps s to the appraisal (e.g., 1 − predicted P(success)), an ex-ante ordering that can later be scored against realized outcomes.

  • Facet: Perceived/forecast difficulty, the pre-attempt subjective estimate of success odds, in three grades: external expert appraisal, verbalized self-assessment, and unverbalized latent self-knowledge; distinct from realized outcome difficulty.
  • Relative to: The solver population the appraiser has in mind: the judge variant is relative to whatever population the rubric anchors (often implicit); the self variants are explicitly relative to the individual solver, with per-solver values averaged over a solver family for a single ordering.
  • Timing: ex_ante · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Measures the appraiser as much as the task: judge scores fold in judge competence, rubric-interpretation drift, and inter-judge disagreement, and what a strong judge finds hard need not match what population P finds hard. Verbalized self-confidence is coarse and clusters at a few round values (many ties), and trained-in optimism/sycophancy shifts the level even when rank order survives. Familiarity/contamination with a known task inflates confidence independently of derivable difficulty. All variants may key on surface features (length, jargon, topic) rather than genuine appraisal; probes especially can become topic/length classifiers; residualization and cross-domain transfer checks bound but do not eliminate this. Latent probes require an outcome-labeled corpus to train, so they cannot bootstrap on a brand-new task set, and small corpora yield high-variance probes.
  • Related work: Kadavath et al. 2022 (P(IK): models mostly know what they know); Lin et al. 2022 (verbalized uncertainty); Xiong et al. 2024 (can LLMs express uncertainty); Azaria & Mitchell 2023 (internal states encode truthfulness); Gottesman & Geva 2024 (knowledge estimation from hidden states); Zheng et al. 2023 (rubric-anchored LLM-as-judge)

AI-eval instantiation. Three graded variants on one benchmark. (1) JudgeScore: k strong LLM (or human) judges rate each task 1–10 against a rubric whose levels carry anchor examples ("a median solver in P passes ~X% of the time"); score = ensemble median. (2) SelfForecast: prompt each solver "Read this task; do not solve it; output the probability in [0,1] that a solution you write would pass a hidden thorough test suite including edge cases"; take the median of 8 elicitations at T≈0.7; difficulty = 1 − P(pass); for open-weight solvers, a sharper variant reads P("yes") from next-token logits of "Will you solve this correctly?". (3) LatentSolvability: extract residual-stream activations at the last prompt token (mid-to-late layers) and train a cross-fitted ridge/logistic probe per solver to predict its empirical pass rate; the task's score is its held-out prediction. Validate all three by Spearman correlation against measured pass rates; the probe-minus-forecast gap isolates unverbalized self-knowledge.

Other-domain instantiation. Exam items: a panel of instructors rates each question against an anchored rubric ("what fraction of last year's cohort would answer this correctly"), while students preview each question and state their probability of answering it correctly before being allowed to solve it; item difficulty = panel median and 1 − mean stated confidence, each validated against realized per-item error rates (classic calibration designs, Lichtenstein & Fischhoff 1977). Climbing analog: guidebook grades assigned by route-setters (judge) versus a climber's pre-attempt send forecast (self), scored against actual send rates.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: It measures the appraiser as much as the task: variance decomposes into forecastable difficulty, appraiser competence/optimism/calibration, and surface-cue keying. Strongest alternative explanation: it is a "looks-hard" score (length, jargon, topic typicality, plus familiarity/contamination) correlated with pass rates only because surface features are. It is also constructually parasitic: validated against realized outcomes, so at best a cheap noisy estimator of M01, not an independent quantity.
  • Generality: Broad: any spec a mind can preview (exams, climbing, code). Breaks at frontiers with no competent appraiser population (novel research problems, adversarial CTFs where the spec reveals little); the probe variant breaks on brand-new task sets (needs outcome labels: bootstrap circularity) and on solvers without accessible internals.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: The catalog's only genuinely ex-ante mind-based estimate: enables triage, routing, and pricing before any attempt data exist, and its signed error versus realized outcome is exactly M30's deceptiveness; the probe-minus-verbalized gap isolates unverbalized self-knowledge. Honest caveat: once pass rates exist, it adds little to the difficulty ordering itself.
  • Gameability: Highly gameable. Jargon-padding or terse phrasing shifts judged difficulty without changing the task; memorized/contaminated items elicit confident "easy" self-forecasts regardless of derivable difficulty; saturated sets compress ratings onto a few round values (mass ties, zero resolution); a base-rate-only forecaster looks calibrated with no discrimination; probes degenerate into topic/length classifiers; once used for routing or pricing, authors Goodhart specs to appraise as desired.
  • Cost: Judge and self-forecast variants are very cheap (k ratings or ~8 elicitations per task). Latent probe needs open weights plus an outcome-labeled training corpus: moderate cost and circular for unseen tasks.
  • Kill test: Generic: show appraisal adds no predictive power over a trivial surface-feature baseline for held-out realized pass rates, out-of-domain. Concrete: fit a ridge on token length + topic one-hots + jargon counts; if judge-median and self-forecast Spearman with pass rates fails to beat that baseline on a held-out domain, and scores swing under name-obfuscating paraphrase while pass rates hold, the measure is exposed as a looks-hard/familiarity detector.
  • Best improvement: Score forecasts by resolution above the surface-feature baseline (skill score), not calibration alone; replace absolute 1-10 ratings with pairwise comparative judgment to break round-value clustering; model appraiser random effects to separate rater bias from task signal; seed rubrics with anchor items of known pass rate; require cross-domain transfer for probes; always report alongside realized M01 so the residual feeds M30.

M30 · Confidence-outcome gap (deceptive difficulty)

M30 · Confidence-outcome gap (deceptive difficulty): definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The signed gap between solvers' pre-feedback credence that their attempt will satisfy the verifier and the rate at which it actually does: score(s) = mean elicited P(success) minus realized success rate over attempts by population P under protocol R, after subtracting each solver's global calibration offset (stretched-scale form near ceiling/floor). Strongly positive values mark deceptive tasks that defeat self-verification (they feel solved when they are not), while near-zero gaps on low-success tasks mark transparent difficulty, where solvers know they are stuck. It orders tasks by metacognitive opacity rather than by failure rate itself.

  • Facet: Metacognitive opacity: how strongly the task defeats solvers' self-verification, separating deceptive difficulty (confidently wrong) from transparent difficulty (known-to-be-stuck). Largely orthogonal to raw failure rate; flags overconfidence-trap items.
  • Relative to: Solver population P (its calibration/metacognition), jointly with verifier V, which fixes the outcome being predicted; protocol R matters too, since feedback and retry rules determine when confidence is read. The same specification can be deceptive for one population and transparent for another.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Generic trait overconfidence of the population (person/model-centering and proper-scoring incentives reduce it, never fully); confidence also encodes self-assessed skill, fatigue, and elicitation format, not just task properties; bounded-difference artifacts near ceiling/floor; small per-task n makes the gap noisy relative to its typical magnitude; selection under R: solvers submit exactly when they believe they are done, and feedback/retries reshape confidence; verifier mismatch: a hidden or stricter-than-spec V (underspecified s) registers as solver overconfidence, so the gap measures deceptiveness of (s,V), not s alone; for machine solvers, stated confidence may not track internal credence (sycophancy, post-training flattening), so part of the gap reflects the reporting channel rather than the task.
  • Related work: Lichtenstein & Fischhoff 1977 (confidence calibration); Moore & Healy 2008 (overconfidence decomposition); Brier 1950 (proper scoring); Frederick 2005 (CRT items as maximal-gap exemplars); Kadavath et al. 2022 (LLM self-knowledge / P(True))

AI-eval instantiation. After each sampled attempt and before any execution feedback, elicit the model's P(this attempt passes all hidden tests) on 0-100 under a proper-scoring rubric, or read the logprob of "True" on a "will this pass?" probe. Then run the automatic verifier. Task score = mean stated confidence minus pass rate, minus the model's suite-wide calibration offset; use the stretched scale for near-ceiling items. Falsifiable predictions: items audit-tagged as tricky/trap show large positive gaps; hard-but-transparent items sit near zero. Where per-token logprobs of stored attempts exist, a proxy is computable from past runs; otherwise it costs one extra query per attempt. A parallel human harness (same elicitation, Brier-scored bonus) enables human-vs-model deceptiveness comparison on identical items.

Other-domain instantiation. Exam calibration study: each examinee answers an item, then, before any feedback, rates confidence (50-100%) that the answer is correct, incentivized by a proper-scoring bonus. Item deceptiveness = mean confidence minus proportion correct, after removing each examinee's global overconfidence. Cognitive-reflection items ("a bat and a ball cost $1.10...") are designed maximal-gap exemplars: the intuitive wrong answer arrives with high confidence. Clinical variant: physicians' stated diagnostic confidence versus gold-standard outcomes (lab/autopsy) identifies deceptively hard case types as distinct from recognized-hard ones.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Mostly tracks miscalibration, not difficulty magnitude. Strongest alternative explanation: the hard-easy effect; overconfidence rises quasi-mechanically with failure rate (at pass~0 the gap just equals mean residual confidence), so raw gaps largely re-encode failure rate times the population's generic miscalibration curve. Second-order: verifier mismatch (hidden/strict V reads as opacity, really M21's territory) and, for LLMs, the reporting channel (RLHF-flattened stated confidence), a solver artifact not a task property. The defensible task signal is the residual after removing the hard-easy curve, much smaller and noisier than the headline gap.
  • Generality: Needs a discrete pre-feedback submission point, an elicitable credence, and a crisp verifier. Breaks for open-ended judged work (research, design: no verifier, P(success) ill-defined), interactive protocols where feedback precedes submission (gap collapses by construction), and non-reporting populations (young children, animals, uninstrumented models).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: at equal pass rate it separates confidently-wrong from known-stuck tasks, predicting where retry loops, best-of-n self-selection, and unsupervised deployment fail silently, safety-relevant and invisible to success rate. But it is M29's elicitation minus outcome, and the behavioral shadow of M31's self-verifier inadequacy; its unique content is the cheap protocol, not a new construct.
  • Gameability: Blanket base-rate confidence zeroes the gap while conveying nothing; the per-solver offset is shiftable by inflating confidence on easy items. Saturated tasks floor at gap<=0 (trivially "transparent"). Defective or mis-keyed verifiers make every confident solver look deceived: QC bugs register as task opacity. Memorized items show gap~0 (contamination reads as transparency); memorized-then-modified items show spurious huge gaps. Calibration-tuning on the eval distribution optimizes the metric away with no competence change.
  • Cost: Low-moderate: one extra elicitation per attempt, or near-free from stored logprobs; but a difference of means is noisy (needs ~50+ attempts per task, plus incentivized elicitation for humans).
  • Kill test: Residualize per-item gap on the population's fitted hard-easy curve (gap vs failure rate), then test whether residuals replicate across disjoint solver populations and predict self-verification failure. Concrete: on a coding suite with stored attempts, compute residualized gaps for two model families; if cross-family correlation is ~0 and residuals fail to predict item-level best-of-n self-selection errors, the measure is solver miscalibration plus artifact, not task opacity.
  • Best improvement: Redefine the score as the stretched-scale residual from the fitted hard-easy curve rather than the raw gap; for LLMs use behavioral credence (bets, stop/submit decisions, self-selection among own samples) instead of stated probability; add a strict/loose verifier-swap audit so verifier-mismatch cases route to M21; require minimum n and two-population replication before tagging a task deceptive.

M31 · Generation-verification asymmetry

M31 · Generation-verification asymmetry: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The asymmetry between the cost of verifying a candidate solution and the cost of generating one: what a verifier needs to reach a verdict of stated reliability on a candidate, relative to what a solver from P needs to produce that candidate. Where verification is far cheaper than generation, guess-and-check, best-of-N, and delegation work; where verification approaches or exceeds generation cost, the task is hard specifically for retry- and oversight-based solving even when single-attempt difficulty is unchanged. Self-referential variants put the solver in the verifier role: the gap between recognizing correct work and producing it, and the adequacy of checks the solver can author from the specification alone.

  • Facet: Locus of difficulty: generation/search cost versus recognition/verification cost; equivalently checkability, the task's amenability to retry, selection, delegation, and oversight strategies.
  • Relative to: Joint property of the (s, P, V) triple: the verification side is verifier-relative (which V, at what verdict reliability), the generation side population-relative (whose cost); the value shifts when either side's tooling changes. Self-verification variants set V = the solver judging or testing its own outputs.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Verification cost is undefined without pinning verdict reliability: a cheap sloppy check is a different V, so the cost-reliability frontier must be fixed; generation cost depends on P's skill; stochastic outcomes force replicated verification, entangling the ratio with the noise floor; outcome- and process-verification costs differ and must not be mixed. Recognition-gap arms partly measure failure-mode structure (near-miss distractors make harder lineups than garbage), leak correctness via style/length cues despite normalization, carry yes-bias in elicited confidence needing per-solver calibration, and censor tasks at pass-rate extremes, biasing correlations with overall difficulty. Self-authored-check arms are vulnerable to memorized/contaminated test material, flattery from a weak own-sample pool (mutation controls needed), conflation of spec-misreading with legitimate alternative readings on underspecified tasks, and harness verdict noise.
  • Related work: Cook 1971 (P vs NP generate/verify framing); DeMillo et al. 1978 (mutation adequacy); Goldwasser, Micali & Rackoff 1985 (interactive proofs); Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018 and Christiano, Shlegeris & Amodei 2018 (debate, scalable oversight); Manheim & Garrabrant 2018 (Goodhart under weak verifiers); Cobbe et al. 2021 (trained verifiers); Kadavath et al. 2022 (P(True)); Chen et al. 2023 (CodeT); West et al. 2024 (Generative AI Paradox); Gu et al. 2024 (CriticBench)

AI-eval instantiation. Three arms on a benchmark with gold verdicts. (1) Cost ratio (existing logs suffice): median verifier cost (tokens/elapsed time/dollars) to reach verdict error < epsilon, calibrated on a gold-labeled subset, divided by median generation cost of a competent solver's candidate; test the prediction that best-of-N gains over pass@1 shrink toward zero as the ratio nears 1, at matched base pass rate. (2) Recognize-vs-produce gap (new cheap calls over stored attempts): style-normalized lineups of one passing plus several distinct-failure candidates, order-permuted and chance-corrected, and/or elicited per-sample P(correct) scored as AUC against gold; gap = probit(discrimination) - probit(pass rate), defined only where the solver has both passing and failing samples; report coverage, treat all-pass/all-fail as censored. (3) Self-checker adequacy: the solver authors test cases from the specification alone; score 1 - balanced accuracy of their verdicts against gold on its own attempts, with a mutated-reference-solution control and an invalid-test rate separating spec misreading from weak edge-case probing.

Other-domain instantiation. Compare grader-minutes per submission to contestant-minutes per solution at a mathematics olympiad (ratio roughly 0.1) against peer review of empirical research, where referee-hours approach or exceed author project-hours once replication is required; rank intellectual task classes by this measured ratio and check which ones prizes, contests, and delegation markets demonstrably work for: the prediction is they function only where the ratio stays well below 1.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Tracks difficulty type, not level: by its own admission the ratio can move while single-attempt difficulty is unchanged. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: within a fixed-verifier domain, verification cost is near-constant, so the ratio is mostly the reciprocal of generation cost (redundant with M02/M27); across domains it mostly indicates whether a formal checker exists (spec objectivity), a domain property, not instance difficulty. The scalar also depends on an arbitrary point on the verifier cost-reliability frontier, which can reorder tasks.
  • Generality: Conceptually broad (olympiad-vs-peer-review is apt), but breaks where no re-examinable candidate artifact exists: performative/continuous-control tasks (surgery, live negotiation) where verifying means re-running at generation cost by construction, and taste-based tasks with no gold verdict, where verifier reliability is undefined.
  • Confound severity: medium
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: at matched pass@1 it predicts which strategies work (best-of-N gains, delegation, oversight feasibility), which success rate cannot. It is the catalog's only first-class checkability ratio; the deepest known difficulty asymmetry (P vs NP) lives here.
  • Gameability: Adding/removing a test harness flips the value without touching the task; recognition arm leaks via style/length/confidence cues and is censored on saturated (all-pass) or defective (wrong-gold) tasks; self-checker arm passes via memorized/contaminated test suites; verifier cost collapses under verdict caching; designers can fake high asymmetry by withholding checkers (ambiguity masquerading as hardness-to-verify).
  • Cost: Low-moderate: arm 1 from existing logs plus gold-subset calibration; arm 2 cheap ranking calls; arm 3 moderate (test authoring, mutation controls, harness).
  • Kill test: Partial out the denominator: if the ratio's correlation with strategy gains vanishes after controlling generation cost and M04's self-selection lift, the "verification side" contributes nothing. Concretely: on repository-level tasks-style tasks with pass@1 fixed in [0.2,0.4], regress pass@16 minus pass@1 on the measured ratio, generation cost, and M04; also recompute the ratio under unit-test V versus LLM-judge V; if rankings disagree wildly or the ratio adds no unique variance, drop the ratio arm.
  • Best improvement: Report the whole verifier cost-reliability frontier (several epsilons) with non-identifiability flags when epsilon is unreachable, not one point; decompose the ratio into domain-level checkability versus instance residual and report both; migrate the recognize-vs-produce arm's overlap into M04/M23 with near-miss-matched distractor pools (M22), keeping M31 as pure cost ratio plus self-check adequacy; pre-register the best-of-N-gain link as its validation criterion.

M32 · Hint/scaffold dose to criterion

M32 · Hint/scaffold dose to criterion: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Fix a graded ladder of external assistance that protocol R can inject: hint strength, advice strings, decomposition scaffolds, tool access, retry-plus-selection budget. The measure is the minimal dose on that ladder at which solver population P's success under verifier V reaches a criterion τ (an ED50-style titration threshold), with the full dose-response curve (and its slope, the elasticity) as the graded refinement. It quantifies the informational/affordance deficit separating solver from task: 0 for tasks already at criterion, censored at the top when even near-complete help fails; a counting argument links the ideal minimal advice to log(1/p), but realized dose is operationally distinct because structured hints can beat rejection-sampling indices.

  • Facet: Distance-to-solvability: how much external information or affordance must be injected to close the gap, separating scaffold-removable difficulty from irreducible difficulty, and depth of the missing capability from mere unreliability.
  • Relative to: Solver-population-relative (advice is only advice relative to what the receiver can exploit) and verifier-relative through the criterion; the ladder itself is a protocol artifact, so doses are comparable only within one fixed ladder. The ladder is solver-independent; the threshold is solver-specific.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Responds to hint quality and encoding, not just task depth: measured doses are upper bounds unless hints are near-optimal (an equally long bad hint does nothing), and hints leak style/format cues beyond nominal content. Hint potency is non-uniform across tasks (for an underspecified task a clarified restatement is the whole answer), so equal rungs are unequal doses; the scale is ordinal with arbitrary spacing and ordering, and affordances interact non-additively (order effects). Conflates missing information with the solver's instructibility (hint-following skill); long hinted prompts can distract weak solvers, giving non-monotone dose-response. Near-solution rungs measure transcription, compressing the top of the scale; floor/ceiling effects make elasticity depend on P's capability level (a rescuing tier does nothing for hopeless or saturated solvers). Cost multiplies by rungs visited.
  • Related work: Levitt 1971 (adaptive staircase psychophysics); Vygotsky 1978; Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976 (scaffolding/ZPD); Grigorenko & Sternberg 1998 (dynamic assessment); Goldman & Kearns 1995 (teaching dimension); Orponen et al. 1994 (instance complexity); Karp & Lipton 1980 (advice classes); Traub, Wasilkowski & Woźniakowski 1988 (information-based complexity); Zhou et al. 2023 (least-to-most prompting); Chen et al. 2021 (pass@k); Cobbe et al. 2021 (verifier selection); Schick et al. 2023 (tool augmentation); Kwa et al. 2025 (METR elicitation gap)

AI-eval instantiation. Author a fixed 5-rung ladder per task from the reference solution (frontier-model generated, human spot-checked): h1 clarified spec restatement incl. edge cases; h2 one-sentence key idea/gotcha; h3 pseudocode or subgoal decomposition; h4 solution paraphrased in prose; h5 reference solution with ~20% masked. Per (task, model), sample ~32 attempts per visited rung under an up-down staircase starting at h2; dose = interpolated rung where pass rate crosses τ=0.5 (0 if the unaided rate already exceeds τ; censored 5+ if never), secondary score = area under the hint-response curve, optionally converted to bits via compressed hint length. Titrate only tasks below criterion unaided. A computable-now variant needs no authored hints: sweep harness tiers {1 attempt; N attempts + verifier selection; + tools; + decomposition scaffold} and report minimal sufficient tier plus marginal gain per tier; flat-profile (scaffold-inelastic) tasks are hard in a qualitatively different way than steep-profile tasks with equal base pass rate.

Other-domain instantiation. Escape rooms and crossword tournaments with standardized hint systems: difficulty = hints a median team consumes to finish in time. Olympiad coaching grades problems by hint depth the median trainee needs: solved cold, solved after "consider invariants", after progressively revealed lemmas, or only via full-solution study. Surgical training: the minimal coaching level (none / verbal cues / hands-on proctoring) at which a trainee completes the procedure safely.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 7/10)

  • Validity: Tracks a genuine facet (removable vs irreducible deficit) but realized dose is dominated by ladder artifacts: hint potency is heterogeneous per task (for an underspecified task h1 is the whole answer), so equal rungs are unequal doses and the scale is ordinal with arbitrary spacing. Strongest alternative explanation of variance: hint-author/ladder alignment with the solver's particular deficit plus the solver's instructibility (hint-following skill), not task depth. Ladders derived from one reference solution also penalize solvers pursuing different valid strategies: they receive misaligned hints and look needier.
  • Generality: Broad wherever assistance is gradable: education (ZPD/dynamic assessment), escape rooms, olympiad coaching, surgical proctoring. Breaks where the deficit is not informational: motor/tacit-skill domains (a verbal hint does not help you juggle), adversarial games where advice cannot be pre-graded against an adapting opponent, and single-insight "aha" problems where the ladder collapses to 0/1 because any real hint is the answer.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: two tasks at equal pass rate 0.1 separate into hint-elastic (one sentence rescues; shallow, elicitation-gap difficulty) vs scaffold-inelastic (deep, capability-gap difficulty); pass rate cannot see this split. Caveats on redundancy: the top-censored region duplicates M03 (irreducible ceiling), the retry-plus-selection rung duplicates M02, and hints-as-prompt-information borders M16.
  • Gameability: Ladder authorship is the lever: weak hints inflate apparent difficulty, near-solution hints deflate it; if dose becomes a target, authors write trivially rescuing hints. Saturated/memorized tasks censor at 0 (uninformative, same as pass rate). Defective tasks with broken verifiers or wrong references censor at 5+ and masquerade as "irreducibly hard", though h5 failure usefully doubles as a defect detector. Instruction-tuned solvers look easier to rescue, so dose partly scores instructibility.
  • Cost: High: staircase visits ~2-3 rungs x 32 attempts per (task, model) (~10-100x plain eval) plus authored, spot-checked per-task ladders. The harness-tier variant is cheap but coarse.
  • Kill test: Generic: independent-ladder reliability; have two independent pipelines author ladders for the same tasks; if between-ladder variance swamps between-task variance, dose is a ladder artifact, not a task property. Concrete: 100 below-criterion repository-level tasks tasks, ladders authored separately by two frontier models plus a human; same solver, same staircase. Reject if cross-ladder Spearman of doses < 0.5, or if per-task dose-response is frequently non-monotone.
  • Best improvement: Report bits actually delivered at threshold (compressed hint length), minimized over >=2 independent ladders, instead of rung index; calibrate each solver's instructibility on a reference set and partial it out; run the cheap harness-tier sweep first, authoring ladders only for inelastic tasks; make h5-censoring a mandatory verifier-defect audit trigger before labeling a task irreducibly hard.

M33 · Universal-solver (Levin) limit

M33 · Universal-solver (Levin) limit: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The difficulty of specification s is the cost an idealized universal solver incurs: the minimum over V-accepted solutions p of |p| + log(execution/checking time), equivalently the log of total compute a simplicity-ordered, dovetailed enumeration of all candidate solutions expends before V first accepts one. Equivalently, it is the latent (Rasch) location of s over the population of ALL computable solvers weighted by the universal prior 2^-|p|: the limiting, prior-free quantity that every finite-panel, population-relative difficulty estimate approximates. It replaces P with a canonical mathematical population, making difficulty a property of (s, V) alone, up to a reference-machine choice.

  • Facet: Solver-independence as intrinsic search depth: difficulty as the algorithmic (Levin) complexity of the accepted-solution set, unifying minimal solution size and compute-to-solve in one quantity, and defining precisely what "solver-independent difficulty" would mean: the anchor point every population-relative measure in the catalog approximates under some prior.
  • Relative to: Solver-independent by construction: an idealized simplicity-weighted population of all computable solvers replaces P. Residual dependence remains on the reference machine (whose choice sets the prior) and on V, whose acceptances define success.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): thought_experiment
  • Confounds: "Solver-independent" conceals a reference-machine choice: the invariance theorem is asymptotic, and the additive constants it ignores dominate at realistic task scale. The quantity is incomputable, so any empirical instantiation smuggles a prior back in, and available solver zoos are an ecosystem monoculture (shared training corpora, shared pedagogy), biasing every finite approximation toward the current population rather than the universal one. It measures the difficulty of satisfying V, not the intended specification: underspecified tasks receive the cost of the cheapest verifier-passer. It also prices discovery-from-scratch, so tasks trivial given cultural/learned priors but deep for an unbiased searcher score as maximally hard.
  • Related work: Levin 1973 (universal search); Solomonoff 1964 / Kolmogorov complexity (Li & Vitányi 2008); Schmidhuber 2004 (OOPS); Hernández-Orallo 2000 (C-tests, Kt-graded difficulty); Hernández-Orallo & Dowe 2010 (anytime universal intelligence test); Hernández-Orallo et al. 2014 (universal psychometrics); Ellis et al. 2021 (DreamCoder).

AI-eval instantiation. For a generation benchmark with an executable verifier (e.g., code tasks judged by a test suite): fix a reference grammar (a small language subset), enumerate candidate solutions in length-lexicographic order with dovetailed execution against V; difficulty = log2(total machine steps until the first accepted solution). Tractable only on toy tasks. Two practical shadows: (a) prior-swap: replace uniform enumeration with a model's proposal distribution, giving cost ≈ −log P_model(accepted solution) + log runtime, which exhibits every sampling-based effort measure as Levin cost under a learned prior; (b) convergence probe: assemble a maximally heterogeneous solver zoo (model families × scales × generations, classical synthesizers, evolutionary search, human cohorts), fit joint IRT, and test whether item locations converge as the zoo diversifies. Convergence estimates the universal ordering; persistent divergence is itself the finding that difficulty is irreducibly population-relative.

Other-domain instantiation. Formal mathematics with a mechanical proof checker as V: the difficulty of a conjecture in a fixed axiomatic system (e.g., a Metamath- or Lean-style kernel) is the log of total checker steps a length-ordered, dovetailed enumeration of candidate proofs spends before the first valid proof: shortest-proof length plus log verification time. Shadow experiment: give one theorem list to maximally diverse prover cohorts (mathematicians from different traditions and eras of training, plus mechanical provers with different heuristics) and test whether their difficulty rankings converge toward a single ordering as the cohort diversifies.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Tracks blind discovery-from-scratch cost, not difficulty as any real solver meets it. Strongest alternative explanation of variance: (a) reference-machine encoding choices: the invariance theorem's additive constants exceed realistic task scale, so scores largely reflect the chosen grammar; (b) verifier laxity: it prices the cheapest V-passer, so underspecified tasks score as their easiest hack. Culturally trivial facts (capital of France) score astronomically hard; the quantity is knowledge-blind by construction.
  • Generality: In principle any (s,V) with a halting computable acceptor. Breaks where V is not one: interactive/drifting/adversarial environments (M37), coordination tasks (M38), open-ended judged work (essays, design), physical tasks. Also degenerate wherever difficulty is knowledge-rarity rather than search depth (M25 territory).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real but purely conceptual: defines what "solver-independent difficulty" would mean, unifies M13 (solution size) and M02 (search cost) into one limit quantity, and motivates the falsifiable convergence probe (do panel estimates converge as the zoo diversifies?). Adds essentially zero measurement value over M02/M06; honest role is anchor, not instrument.
  • Gameability: Infinitely gameable via machine choice: pick a DSL where the intended solution is a primitive and difficulty vanishes. The prior-swap shadow (−log P_model) inverts under memorization: a memorized task scores near-zero under a learned prior but astronomically hard under uniform enumeration: same task, opposite verdicts. Defective verifiers collapse cost to the print-the-answer hack, ranking reward-hackable tasks as easy.
  • Cost: Free to state; incomputable exactly. Toy enumeration cheap; the convergence-probe shadow (heterogeneous zoo + human cohorts + joint IRT) is very expensive.
  • Kill test: Generic: show the ordering is not machine-invariant at task scale. Concrete: on ~50 small code tasks with test-suite verifiers, run dovetailed Levin search under three reference grammars (Lisp subset, stack language, Python-expression DSL); if Spearman correlation across the three difficulty orderings is low (<0.5), the "canonical" ordering is an artifact of machine choice and the anchor claim dies.
  • Best improvement: Demote from measure to limit-claim with an operational surrogate: (1) always report machine-sensitivity bands (scores under k declared reference machines, plus ordering agreement); (2) replace "universal" with declared-prior Levin cost: condition on a frozen public-corpus model as the prior, fixing the knowledge-blindness pathology and making it estimable; (3) make the convergence probe the deliverable: the convergence rate of IRT locations as the zoo diversifies is the measurable distance from the limit.

M34 · Feedback economics & iterative repair

M34 · Feedback economics & iterative repair: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. Measures the economics of the on-path correctness signal a task exposes under protocol R (how frequently intermediate signals arrive, with what delay, at what per-probe cost, and how informative they are about the true defect), together with the iterative-repair demand this creates: the expected number of probe-revise cycles (and cumulative probe cost) a solver from P needs before producing a solution V accepts. Tasks are easy on this axis when feedback is cheap, prompt, and diagnostic and few rounds suffice; hard when the environment rations feedback (rare, delayed, expensive, or opaque signals) or success requires deep repair chains even under good feedback.

  • Facet: Interactive/sequential-effort facet, debuggability: how much of success must be earned through probe-and-revise iteration rather than one-shot generation, and how well the task's error signal supports that iteration.
  • Relative to: Joint (s,P,R,V), but decomposable: signal interval, delay, and probe cost are properties of the task-under-protocol (s,R); rounds-to-acceptance additionally depends on the solver population's repair skill and on V. Heavily scaffold-dependent, so the repair-depth side indexes difficulty for a specific solver loop, not the task alone.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Conflates task hardness with feedback informativeness: an intrinsically easy task with opaque failure signals scores hard, and visible checks can diverge sharply from the final verifier, so feedback quality varies by task. Repair depth partly measures the solver's self-correction skill, a capability distinct from task hardness, plus prompt/scaffold idiosyncrasies of R. When the on-path signal overlaps V, iterating leaks the verifier and rewards overfitting to visible checks (a test-suite property, not a task property). Round caps censor the hard tail. Probe cost and delay are largely set by the harness/budget, not by s. Rounds-to-success also correlates with plain one-shot difficulty, so hard-but-transparent and easy-but-opaque tasks can tie.
  • Related work: Self-Refine (Madaan et al. 2023); Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023); Olausson et al. 2024 (is self-repair a silver bullet); SWE-agent turn/cost statistics (Yang et al. 2024); Hogarth 2001 (kind vs. wicked learning environments); Ericsson et al. 1993 (feedback in deliberate practice).

AI-eval instantiation. Execute-and-revise harness on any suite with runnable checks: the model drafts a solution, runs only the visible checks (held-out checks stay the final judge V), reads tracebacks/failing assertions, revises; cap K=8 rounds, ~8 seeds per (task, model), two model scales for a slope. Primary score: restricted-mean rounds to first held-out pass, with survival-analysis handling of K-censored runs; secondaries: total tool calls, cumulative generated tokens, elapsed time. Separately profile the channel per task: probe latency and cost, and signal informativeness (e.g., agreement rate between visible-check verdict and held-out verdict, or how often a failure message localizes the actual defect).

Other-domain instantiation. Cooking curriculum design: for each dish, log (a) channel economics: whether the cook can taste as they go (soup seasoning: instant, near-free probes) or only judge after a full attempt (soufflé, sourdough: one signal per hours-long, ingredient-consuming try), and (b) repair depth: the median number of attempt-adjust cycles novices need before a blind taster (V) accepts the dish. Rank dishes hardest that demand many cycles under stingy, delayed feedback.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Split verdict. The channel side (signal interval, delay, probe cost) is a property of the harness/protocol R, not the task s; it tracks environment design. The repair-depth side mostly re-measures (a) one-shot difficulty (harder tasks need more rounds, already M01/M02) and (b) the solver's self-repair skill, which Olausson et al. show is model-dependent and often weak. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: visible-test coverage and error-message verbosity of the harness plus solver self-correction capability, with residual task hardness riding along.
  • Generality: The kind-vs-wicked feedback axis (Hogarth) is genuinely cross-domain (code, cooking, control, tutoring). It degenerates in one-shot domains with no intermediate signal: single-answer factual QA or closed-form math has zero on-path probes, so repair collapses into blind resampling (= M02); and in irreversible one-attempt domains (surgery, launches) it conflates with M35 recoverability.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real but narrower than claimed: two tasks with identical one-shot pass rates can differ hugely in pass-after-K-feedback-rounds. The feedback-gain slope predicts which tasks yield to agentic scaffolds versus stay hard, invisible to plain success rate. The rounds count itself adds little beyond attempts-to-success.
  • Gameability: Iterating against visible checks that overlap V leaks the verifier: solvers overfit visible tests and "pass" wrong. Verbose tracebacks make tasks score easy regardless of substance. Saturated tasks pin rounds at 1 (no signal); memorized tasks never engage the axis; tasks with broken/absent visible checks score maximally opaque despite being trivial. K-round caps censor exactly the hard tail the measure targets.
  • Cost: High: needs new agentic-harness data, ~8 seeds x 8 rounds x 2 scales per task (100+ generations/task) plus per-task channel profiling and survival modeling.
  • Kill test: Generic: show M34 carries no task-stable signal after conditioning on one-shot difficulty and harness; regress rounds on pass@1; check the residual replicates across model families and check-suite swaps. Concrete: on wave 1, compare pass@8-with-feedback vs matched-compute pass@8 blind resampling, and rerun with error messages replaced by bare pass/fail. If feedback-gain rankings don't correlate across two families, or barely move under message ablation, the axis measures harness+solver, not tasks.
  • Best improvement: Reframe the primary score as matched-compute feedback gain (pass@K informative-feedback minus pass@K blind retry), define signal informativeness against V (agreement/MI between visible and held-out verdicts), report channel economics as (s,R) protocol descriptors rather than difficulty, and mandate an error-message-ablation control. This separates it cleanly from M02 (search cost) and M22 (repair distance).

M35 · Error recoverability

M35 · Error recoverability: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The degree to which a task remains completable after a representative solver error: one minus the probability that a typical mistake makes success unreachable within an acceptable extra-cost budget, or equivalently the expected recovery cost (normalized by total task cost) following the median error. It captures how forgiving the task's state dynamics are (whether errors are cheap detours or absorbing traps), so the same nominal solution length is far harder when mistakes cannot be undone.

  • Facet: Environment forgiveness: irreversibility, trap/absorbing states, and the cost of undoing mistakes, the channel through which stakes convert into difficulty.
  • Relative to: Joint property of the task's state dynamics (s), the solver population P (whose error distribution gets injected matters), the protocol R (retries, checkpoints, and undo affordances change what is reachable), and the verifier V (sets the completion standard and the acceptable extra-cost budget). Reported as D(s, P, R, V).
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Hinges on the representativeness of the injected-error bank and on the strength of the reference recovery solver (a stronger recoverer makes every task look forgivable); the extra-budget multiplier is an arbitrary knob; conflates irreversibility with detectability: an undetected error looks 'recoverable' right up to final verification; high stakes can change solver behavior (caution, extra checking), moving realized difficulty independently of the environment's actual forgiveness; protocol affordances such as resets or snapshots can mask underlying irreversibility, so values are only comparable with R pinned; and recovery odds depend on where in the episode the error lands, so injection-point sampling shapes the number.
  • Related work: Reversibility-aware RL (Grinsztajn et al. 2021); safe exploration in MDPs (Moldovan & Abbeel 2012); Human Error / Swiss-cheese model (Reason 1990); METR messiness factor 'irreversible mistakes' (Kwa et al. 2025); absorbing/trap states in the POMDP literature.

AI-eval instantiation. In sandboxed agentic episodes, pause each run at a uniformly sampled progress point and inject an error drawn from a bank of real mistakes previously committed by population-P solvers on this task family; resume with a strong reference agent under 1.5x the normal budget, N replicates per injection point, automatic verification. Report the fraction of injections after which the task remains completable, plus median extra cost when recovered; compare tasks at matched horizon and depth.

Other-domain instantiation. Aviation: in full-flight-simulator LOFT sessions, inject standardized faults/errors at scripted points and measure crew recovery rate and added workload per scenario, an established practice whose per-scenario recovery statistics directly instantiate the index. Craft contrast: pottery (clay reworkable while wet) vs. glassblowing (seconds-wide recovery windows), measured by expert re-completion rates after standardized induced flaws.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Tracks one causal channel (how errors convert into failure), not difficulty magnitude: a trivial one-shot task with an irreversible step scores "unforgiving" while a brutal proof in a fully-undoable editor scores ~1. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: measurement apparatus, not task. Reference-recoverer strength, error-bank composition, the arbitrary 1.5x budget knob, and injection-point position (late injections mechanically fail on long tasks). Also silently mixes in detectability: undetected errors look recoverable (M23 leakage).
  • Generality: Strong for stateful/agentic/embodied domains (ops, surgery, aviation, glassblowing). Degenerates on stateless one-shot tasks (single-turn QA, translation, closed-book exams): with no intermediate state to trap, recoverability collapses to "is a retry allowed": pure protocol R, zero task signal. Near-constant ~1 for pure-thought/paper tasks, so no discriminative variance there either.
  • Confound severity: medium
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real and distinct: explains WHY two tasks of equal length and pass rate differ (self-repair possible vs early-error-determined outcomes), and it is prospective/causal: predicts value of checkpointing, oversight, and how stakes become difficulty. No other catalog entry covers mid-trajectory absorbing states: M34 is signal availability, M22 is final-output repair distance, M23 is detection. Plain success rate cannot separate "hard because unforgiving" from "hard because demanding."
  • Gameability: A too-strong reference recoverer or snapshot/reset affordances saturate everything at 1 (sandbox ceiling); protocol can mask true irreversibility. Error banks from weak solvers inject trivial slips (inflates), from strong solvers only lethal ones (deflates). A recoverer that has memorized the solution "recovers" by regenerating it from scratch, hiding real state damage. Cherry-picking early injection points inflates scores.
  • Cost: High: requires sandboxed causal-injection harness, curated per-family error banks, and N replicates x injection points x tasks at 1.5x budget; automatable but many extra rollouts per task.
  • Kill test: Apparatus-swap invariance: recompute with a different reference recoverer and a different error-bank source; if task rankings churn, the number is apparatus, not task. Concretely: 30 agentic SWE tasks, 2 recoverers (frontier vs mid-tier) x 2 error banks; kill if cross-condition Spearman rho < 0.5, and additionally if recoverability adds no predictive power for success beyond pass rate at matched horizon.
  • Best improvement: Report the recovery-probability-vs-extra-budget curve (AUC), not a 1.5x threshold; split recoverability-given-detection from joint (isolates M23); pin R to no-artificial-resets and publish protocol elasticity alongside; stratify by injection point (early/mid/late) instead of pooling; bracket with weak/strong recoverer pairs; restrict scope to stateful tasks by construction.

M36 · Observability gap

M36 · Observability gap: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The performance lost to information the task withholds: the regret of the best policy restricted to the specification s and the native observation/interaction interface that protocol R provides, relative to the best policy granted oracle access to all success-relevant hidden state and unstated context. Equivalently, the share of the information that verifier V's success criterion depends on which solvers must actively acquire or infer rather than being handed in s.

  • Facet: Partial information: hidden state, unstated context and requirements, and the burden of active information acquisition.
  • Relative to: Solver-independent in the limit (defined via best policies under two information regimes), but in practice estimated with the strongest available solvers from P, making any estimate a population-relative lower bound on the true gap.
  • Timing: ex_post · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: The oracle condition changes the task beyond information content (longer context, attention load, accidental strategy hints), so gaps must be attributed carefully. Weak solvers cannot exploit oracle information, shrinking the measured gap and understating incompleteness. Entangled with memory demands: information observed early but needed late reads as "hidden" to memoryless or short-context solvers. With a fixed population, the gap conflates information truly inaccessible under R with P's failure to acquire information R does make accessible (acquisition skill vs. availability). And when V enforces criteria absent from s, the gap absorbs specification ambiguity/underspecification, not only hidden environment state.
  • Related work: POMDPs (Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra 1998); value of information (Howard 1966); hidden-information games: poker vs chess (Brown & Sandholm 2019); information foraging (Pirolli & Card 1999); GAIA's information-gathering task design (Mialon et al. 2023).

AI-eval instantiation. Run the same agents on each task under (a) the native interface and (b) an oracle condition that appends the hidden environment state and any unstated user/verifier constraints to the context (facts and state, not advice) with matched budgets, N sampled attempts per condition, and automatic verification. D(s) = pass(oracle) − pass(native), pooled over the solver population; optionally add a length-matched padding control to separate information content from context-load effects, and report the count of information-acquiring actions in successful native traces as a corroborating signal.

Other-domain instantiation. Clinical diagnosis: vignette studies give physicians the same underlying case with a restricted workup vs. the complete panel (full history, labs, imaging); D per case type is the diagnostic-accuracy gap, isolating how much difficulty is missing information rather than reasoning. Games: compare matched players on full-information positions vs. hidden-information analogues (e.g., poker situations with and without a hole-card oracle in simulation) to quantify how much of the game's difficulty is observability rather than strategy depth.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks the POMDP-style information facet it claims, thanks to the differencing design. Strongest alternative explanation of variance: gap = pass(oracle) − pass(native) is a bounded difference, mechanically maximized at mid-range native difficulty and squeezed to ~0 at floor/ceiling, so much of its cross-task variance is sigmoid position (p(1−p) shape), not bits withheld. Second: oracle-text authoring leaks strategy disguised as "facts," so the gap partly measures how much reasoning the appendix smuggles in. Third: it conflates info truly unavailable under R with info the population failed to acquire.
  • Generality: Applies wherever hidden state is enumerable and serializable (agentic tasks, diagnosis, hidden-info games). Breaks in fully observable domains (closed-book math: uniformly ~0, fine as a facet but non-discriminating) and, worse, where the hidden information IS the answer (bug-finding, research questions, taste-based verifier criteria): the oracle collapses the task and the gap trivially equals total difficulty. Also fails when hidden state exceeds context (whole-system runtime state).
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Real: plain success rate cannot separate "hard because information is withheld" from "hard because reasoning is deep." This is the only catalog measure of the exploration/acquisition burden, and it is intervention-relevant (fix interfaces vs fix reasoning). Adjacent to M32 (oracle = maximal hint of one type) and leaks into M26 at the unstated-criteria edge, but the facet is otherwise uncovered.
  • Gameability: Saturated tasks read as "fully observable" (both conditions pass, gap 0); memorized/leaked tasks inflate native pass and hide genuine observability demands; defective specs let verifier-side ambiguity masquerade as environment hiding; authors can inflate gaps by writing answer-entailing oracle appendices or deflate them by dumping unparseable raw state; solvers fine-tuned to exploit appended-context patterns inflate oracle pass.
  • Cost: Moderate-high: 2-3x attempt budget (native, oracle, padding control) plus per-task manual authoring of the hidden-state appendix, which is skilled, unamortized labor and itself an error source.
  • Kill test: Build tasks with experimenter-controlled withheld bits and test whether D(s) tracks withheld information monotonically rather than native pass rate. Concretely: paired SWE tasks identical except a needed config value is either stated in the README or discoverable only by running code; if D(s) fails to separate pairs, or regressing D on native pass across a suite shows the p(1−p) hump explains most variance, the measure is exposed.
  • Best improvement: Normalize by headroom (gap/(1−pass_native)) or work on the stretched scale; make the padding control mandatory plus an irrelevant-true-facts control; add an intermediate "query-at-cost" condition (oracle answers only explicit questions) to split availability from acquisition skill; validate appendices by checking a judge cannot solve from the appendix alone.

M37 · Environment drift & opposition

M37 · Environment drift & opposition: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The degree to which the target and conditions of s fail to hold still while solving proceeds, summarized by three scalars. Drift rate: the probability per unit solving time that a solution valid against s as issued would fail V at completion absent solver adaptation (how much of the task is tracking a moving target rather than hitting a fixed one). Noise floor and adversarial gap: one minus the best achievable pass probability when the environment's latent conditions are drawn at random (irreducible luck), and the further drop when those conditions are chosen by an adaptive adversary within bounds declared by R, separating hard-because-moving from hard-because-luck from hard-because-opposed, all distinct from hard-because-skill.

  • Facet: Moving/hostile-target burden: non-stationarity of the specification and environment during the solving window, irreducible outcome stochasticity, and strategic opposition, difficulty located in the environment's dynamics rather than in the solver's skill deficit.
  • Relative to: Solver-independent in principle: drift is a property of the specification/environment process over the solving window with V held fixed, and floor/gap are defined via the best policy. In practice estimated with the strongest available solver ensemble, so reported floors are upper bounds on true noise; realized impact is mediated by P's re-planning ability, and R's declared bounds define the adversary.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): needs_new_data
  • Confounds: Drift mechanically accumulates with task duration, so it must be reported as a rate or it collapses into horizon length. Endogenous change (reactions provoked by the solver's own actions) gets misattributed to the environment, and modular or robust solution styles resist drift, so realized staleness partly reflects solver strategy. Scripted drift and scripted adversaries in constructed evals may not match ecological statistics. Unknown best policy means skill deficit masquerades as noise floor; weak solver populations compress the whole curve. The adversary's capability budget and the designer's seed distribution are arbitrary choices that set the gap's and floor's scales, and verifier noise inflates the apparent floor.
  • Related work: Concept drift (Gama et al. 2014); non-stationary stochastic optimization/bandits (Besbes, Gur & Zeevi 2014); requirements volatility (Nurmuliani, Zowghi & Powell 2004); METR messiness factor "changing requirements" (Kwa et al. 2025); exploitability/best-response gaps (Zinkevich et al. 2007; Johanson et al. 2011); robust adversarial RL (Pinto et al. 2017); skill-luck decomposition (Mauboussin 2012); variance-aware eval statistics (Agarwal et al. 2021); pass^k with user simulators (Yao et al. 2024, tau-bench).

AI-eval instantiation. Build drifting variants of agentic tasks: with hazard rate lambda, a scripted stakeholder amends the spec mid-episode (adds a constraint, moves the target). Ex-ante drift = staleness probability, estimated by grading frozen time-0 solutions against the final verifier; ex-post, report the pass-rate slope in lambda. For hostility: run the strongest available agent N times per environment seed and decompose outcome variance into seed- vs attempt-attributable components (variance components / pass^k); noise floor = 1 - per-seed success under random seeds. Then let a bounded adversary schedule latent conditions (user-simulator moods, tool outages, market moves) against the same agent; adversarial gap = the success drop. Report the triple (drift rate, floor, gap) per task; nearly all current suites are static, so drift and adversaries must be built in.

Other-domain instantiation. Drift: from construction change-order and building-code-update records, estimate the per-project-month probability that the initially approved blueprint would be non-compliant or rejected at completion, and order project types (kitchen remodel vs hospital wing) by this staleness rate at fixed duration. Floor and gap: poker vs chess vs sailing regattas; estimate the noise floor from elite players' per-hand or per-race outcome variance (skill-luck decomposition of results data), and the adversarial gap from how much a fixed strategy's win rate degrades across repeated sessions as opponents adapt: backgammon is high-floor/low-gap, heads-up poker low-floor/high-gap, chess puzzles low/low.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Split verdict across the triple. Drift rate genuinely tracks environment non-stationarity when exogenous, but realized staleness reflects solver strategy (modular designs, late commitment). Floor and gap have identification problems: with the best policy unknown, "noise floor" is skill deficit plus verifier noise dressed as luck, and the gap's scale is set by the designer's arbitrary adversary budget R. Strongest alternative explanation of cross-task variance: solver-ensemble weakness (floor) and designer choices of lambda/budget (drift, gap), self-reported design intent, not discovered environment facts. Conceptual worry: a pure lottery scores high yet is unwinnable, not hard; the measure is at least honest that this is a decomposition of failure attribution, not skill demand.
  • Generality: Conceptually domain-general (construction change orders, poker vs chess, markets), and the ecological instantiations are plausible. But it is degenerate on most existing evaluation domains: static one-shot tasks (closed-book math, exam QA, frozen coding benchmarks) give drift=0, gap=0, floor≈verifier noise: zero variance, no discrimination. Informative only for long-horizon interactive/agentic settings.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: The only catalog measure locating difficulty in environment dynamics rather than solver deficit. Plain success rate cannot say WHY a task fails; the decomposition predicts which remedy works (retries for luck, robustness for drift, game theory for opposition), genuinely actionable. Caveat: the floor component duplicates M03 (irreducible failure mass) and M01's instance-vs-attempt variance split; drift and gap are the new content.
  • Gameability: In constructed suites the designer sets lambda and the adversary, so suites can inflate apparent difficulty with coin flips (floor) or brutal adversaries (gap) at zero added skill demand, and measurement is partly circular (recovering the lambda you scripted). Scripted drift on reused tasks is memorizable: an agent learning the fixed amendment schedule converts drift into a longer static spec. Solvers dodge staleness grading by delaying commitment. Saturated/static suites read all-zero; weak scripted adversaries understate the gap.
  • Cost: High; needs new data. Drifting/adversarial task variants plus N-runs-per-seed matrices for variance decomposition multiply eval compute ~10-100x per task; ecological estimates need records archives (change orders, results databases).
  • Kill test: Invariance audit: recompute floor and gap varying only solver-ensemble strength and adversary budget; if task rankings churn, the scalars are solver/designer properties, not environment properties. Concrete: on tau-bench, estimate per-seed noise floors with a mid-tier agent versus a frontier agent with retry scaffolding; if frontier floors are far lower and cross-task rank correlation drops below ~0.5, the "floor" was skill deficit.
  • Best improvement: Keep drift rate and adversarial gap; fold the noise floor into M03 as an environment- vs solver-attributable refinement. Standardize the adversary budget in cost units (bits or dollars of intervention) for cross-task comparability. Separate exogenous from endogenous drift via inert-solver replay (what changes under a no-op agent). Report floor/gap only after passing the solver-tier invariance audit; always report drift as a rate at fixed horizon.

M38 · Coordination load

M38 · Coordination load: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The number of distinct, independently goal-directed parties whose actions any successful plan for s must predict, influence, or synchronize with, weighted by each party's objective misalignment with the solver (shared goal, through indifferent, to opposed) and by the required synchrony (loose sequencing to tight simultaneity). A lower-bound structural variant counts the minimal set of external commitments that appears on every successful plan.

  • Facet: Multi-party dependence: the burden of modeling, aligning, and synchronizing other agents, as distinct from single-solver search, planning, or execution difficulty.
  • Relative to: Mixed. The structural party count is solver-independent: a property of s and the independently-policied agents embedded in its environment. The realized burden is relative to P's social/negotiation capability and to the coordination institutions protocol R supplies (contracts, standard interfaces, prices, escrow), which can collapse coordination cost; V matters only insofar as it credits partial alignment.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: Raw party count ignores per-party hardness (one opposed veto-holder outweighs ten friendly vendors); institutions and standard protocols collapse coordination cost, so the measure is R-relative; overlaps observability (other minds are hidden state) and adversarialness (opposed parties); restrict to the alignment/synchrony burden to stay distinct; correlates with horizon length, since long tasks accrete stakeholders; in simulated settings the measured load also reflects the fidelity and scriptedness of the simulated parties rather than true strategic behavior.
  • Related work: Coordination theory (Malone & Crowston 1994); communication-path scaling (Brooks 1975); focal points (Schelling 1960); common knowledge and the coordinated-attack/email game (Rubinstein 1989); Hanabi as pure coordination (Bard et al. 2020); cognitive hierarchy depth (Camerer, Ho & Chong 2004); negotiation and mixed-motive agents (Lewis et al. 2017; FAIR Diplomacy/Cicero, Bakhtin et al. 2022).

AI-eval instantiation. For each task in an agentic suite, enumerate the independently-policied simulated parties (users, approvers, counterparties, services with private state and goals) that any successful trace must engage; score each on a misalignment x synchrony rubric and set D = weighted count (ex ante). Validate with task ladders that hold horizon and dependency-graph depth fixed while growing the party set (single user -> user + approver -> user + approver + opposed counterparty), checking that pass rate over N sampled, automatically verified attempts falls along the ladder; ex post, compare the set of parties actually engaged in passing versus failing traces.

Other-domain instantiation. Film production or large event staging: from production records, count the distinct parties (unions, vendors, permit authorities, principal cast) whose commitments sit on the critical path, then regress on-time completion against party count at fixed schedule length; the slope is the coordination-load difficulty gradient. Kidney-exchange chains: plot chain success probability against the number of simultaneously consenting parties per chain.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 5/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks a real burden (eliciting commitments from other minds) but the weighted count is a poor stand-in for it. Strongest alternative explanation of variance: interaction surface / horizon length; parties accrete with plan size, so D largely re-measures how long and branchy the task is. Second: sim authorship (in AI evals the 'strategic burden' of scripted parties is whatever the designer typed, so D varies with scaffolding fidelity, not difficulty). The misalignment weighting also mispredicts: fully aligned parties can be maximally hard (Hanabi, Rubinstein's email game: common-knowledge depth, zero misalignment), while many opposed parties are trivial under an institution (buying from 10 hostile-margin vendors via a market). Overlaps M36 (other minds = hidden state) and M37 (opposed parties = hostility).
  • Generality: Only where other agents exist. Degenerate zero (no variance, no signal) on most of the task universe as currently benchmarked: math, proofs, coding katas, closed-world puzzles. Also breaks in institution-rich domains (markets, standardized APIs, escrow): party count is high but realized coordination cost collapses to near zero, so cross-domain comparisons are incoherent without fixing R.
  • Confound severity: high
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: it is the catalog's only multi-agent axis, and it is ex ante, structural, and causally manipulable (you can add a party), unlike pass rate. The unique residue after subtracting M36/M37, though, is narrower than advertised: synchrony/common-knowledge demands among aligned-or-indifferent parties (kidney-chain simultaneous consent is a clean example). For single-solver suites it adds nothing.
  • Gameability: Upward: pad tasks with scripted always-approving bots; party count and rubric score rise, difficulty doesn't. Downward: wrap counterparties in deterministic service interfaces. Solvers game it by memorizing negotiation templates (ask-escalate-split), collapsing realized burden while measured D stays high. The misalignment-times-synchrony rubric is judge-scored, hence prompt-sensitive and author-inflatable; ladder validation saturates once sim parties are pushovers.
  • Cost: Low ex ante (enumerate parties + rubric, human or LLM judge). Moderate for validation: authoring party-ladder task families with horizon held fixed is real work; multi-party sims are expensive to build faithfully.
  • Kill test: Generic: grow party count two ways at matched horizon/dialog length, (a) compliant scripted parties vs (b) genuinely strategic ones with private reservation policies, and separately include aligned-but-tight-synchrony tasks. If (a) drops pass rate as much as (b), D is horizon in disguise; if aligned/tight tasks fail at opposed-task rates, the misalignment weighting is wrong. Concrete: in a tau-bench-style customer-service sim, add (a) an auto-approving approver vs (b) an approver with a hidden reservation value, same turn budget; regress pass rate on D controlling for steps.
  • Best improvement: Replace the weighted count with a counterfactual ablation: D_coord = pass-rate gap between the real task and its 'pre-committed' twin where every external party is replaced by a signed contract/compliant script. Decompose into per-party influence hardness (M32-style hint/probe titration per commitment) plus common-knowledge/synchrony depth; move opposed-party burden to M37 and hidden-mental-state burden to M36; always report relative to the best institution R supplies.

M39 · Slack / budget tightness

M39 · Slack / budget tightness: definition, instantiations and critique

Definition. The ratio of the resource budget granted for a task (time, attempts, money, materials) to the median resource requirement of competent solvers attempting the same task unconstrained. Difficulty is read as pressure: it rises steeply as this slack ratio approaches 1 from above, and the task becomes infeasible-in-expectation below 1. It orders tasks by tightness rather than size: the same underlying work is a different task under a 10x versus a 1.1x budget.

  • Facet: Resource pressure: tightness of the granted budget relative to the requirement, as distinct from the size or volume of the work itself.
  • Relative to: A property of (s, P, R): the numerator (granted budget) is read off the specification s and/or protocol R; the denominator is population-relative: the median cost for competent solvers in P to reach a V-accepted solution when unconstrained.
  • Timing: both · Feasibility (AI-eval): computable_now
  • Confounds: The denominator inherits every confound of the underlying cost/time-requirement estimate (choice of cost metric, who counts as competent). Requirements are budget-endogenous: solvers adopt cheaper strategies under pressure, so the unconstrained requirement overstates true need and the computed tightness overstates pressure. Granted budgets are often soft norms rather than hard cutoffs, so nominal slack mismeasures effective pressure. The ratio also responds to the dispersion of requirements across P, not just the median: with heavy-tailed solve costs, equal median slack yields very different failure probabilities. The attempts-budget dimension overlaps protocol/harness-generosity measures and must be assigned to exactly one measure, not both.
  • Related work: Slack/float in critical-path scheduling (Kelley & Walker 1959); speed-vs-power tests and test speededness (Gulliksen 1950); time pressure in judgment and decision making (Svenson & Maule 1993); speed-accuracy tradeoff (Wickelgren 1977); anytime algorithms (Zilberstein 1996).

AI-eval instantiation. Estimate each task's median unconstrained solve cost (tokens, steps, or tool calls) from strong-solver runs. Re-run N sampled attempts under hard caps at {4x, 2x, 1.25x, 1x, 0.8x} of that median with automatic verification, and fit the pass-rate-vs-slack curve. Report the task's native tightness (harness budget / median requirement) positioned on that curve: two tasks with equal uncapped pass rate can differ sharply in slack sensitivity, and the curve separates them.

Other-domain instantiation. Timed testing: administer the same exam form at 2x, 1x, and 0.75x of standard time to matched groups; the score-decrement curve measures the form's speededness (the classical power-vs-speed distinction), and any specific administration's difficulty index is its position on that curve. The same design transfers to any deadline-bound professional task, e.g. air-traffic scenario resolution under varying time-to-conflict.

Critique (verdict: keep_with_caveats, score 6/10)

  • Validity: Partly tracks a real phenomenon (speededness/pressure, per Gulliksen), but the scalar is a ratio whose denominator is just another difficulty measure (M27/M02 requirement) and whose numerator is an administrative choice. Strongest alternative explanation of its variance: budget-setting conventions plus requirement-estimation error. Worse, the unconstrained median systematically overstates true need (solvers adopt cheaper anytime strategies under pressure), with task-varying bias (tasks rich in fallback strategies look tighter than they are), which scrambles rankings, not just levels.
  • Generality: Applies wherever a binding budget exists: exams, deadlines, agent harnesses, materials, money. Breaks in unbudgeted/self-paced domains: open-ended research or proving a conjecture has slack ~infinity, so the hardest tasks read as pressure-free. Also ill-defined where no median competent solve exists (denominator undefined), and where timing is environment dynamics rather than granted budget (real-time control: time-to-conflict is part of the task, not the protocol).
  • Confound severity: medium
  • Added value over plain success rate: Genuine: isolates pressure from size. Two tasks with equal uncapped pass rate can differ sharply in slack sensitivity; the pass-vs-slack curve is effectively dP/d(budget), invisible to success rate measured at one budget point. It also explains why plain pass-rate rankings reshuffle across harnesses with different caps. It is the only catalog measure that treats budget as the manipulated variable (M32 adds aid; M39 subtracts resources).
  • Gameability: Denominator is trivially inflatable: verbose or dawdling unconstrained runs pad the median requirement, making any native budget look tight. As an optimization target it Goodharts badly: cut budgets on trivial tasks and stopwatch trivia becomes 'hard'. Memorized/contaminated tasks shrink the median requirement toward zero, reporting huge slack while clean solvers face real pressure. Defective verifiers accepting junk quickly likewise fake slack. Soft caps let agents ignore nominal budgets; hard caps invite cap-aware truncation training.
  • Cost: Moderate-high: unconstrained strong-solver runs plus N attempts at ~5 cap levels per task, roughly 5-10x plain pass-rate sampling. Automatable for AI evals; expensive in human domains (matched groups per time condition).
  • Kill test: Budget-collapse test: randomize granted budgets holding work fixed and check whether P(success) depends on budget only through the slack ratio. Concrete: 30 tasks, capped attempts at matched slack {2x, 1.25x, 1x}; if failure at 1.25x is better predicted by requirement dispersion (CV) than by slack, and unconstrained-vs-2x-capped medians shift by task-varying factors, the median-ratio scalar is exposed.
  • Best improvement: Define it as two parameters of the fitted pass-vs-slack curve (slack at 50% pass; steepness), not the naive ratio; estimate the denominator from lightly-capped rather than unconstrained runs to blunt strategy endogeneity; normalize by an upper quantile (budget/Q90) or add a dispersion term for heavy-tailed costs; explicitly cede attempt-count generosity to M32/M34 to prevent double counting.
